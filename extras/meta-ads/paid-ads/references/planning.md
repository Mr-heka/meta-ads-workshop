# Planning and test capacity

Start with the buyer's situation and available evidence. Search can address expressed demand; social and video can introduce an offer; professional targeting can help reach specific business roles. These are hypotheses to investigate, not platform rankings. Check the actual audience, campaign type and current platform capabilities. A B2B label does not automatically select LinkedIn or disqualify TikTok, and retargeting is not automatically incremental.

Compare each plausible channel on the same dimensions: reachable qualified audience, intent, credible creative supply, measurable outcome, expected conversion delay, delivery constraints and economics. Fund a coherent test the available budget can support. Existing qualified conversion evidence is more useful than a universal demographic stereotype or CPC benchmark.

## Budget scenarios

Work backwards from the business goal using observed rates or explicit low/base/high assumptions. For example, required leads = desired customers / lead-to-customer rate; estimated ad spend = required leads × cost per lead. Keep currency, period, source and cohort consistent. A zero or unknown rate cannot produce a finite forecast. Capacity, refunds, delivery costs and cash timing may constrain a plan that looks attractive from revenue alone.

State the learning question, test spend ceiling, relevant outcome, observation period and conversion-lag allowance before running a test. Include the conditions for no launch, hold, corrective work, stop or expansion. An approved loss limit can justify stopping even without enough data for a performance conclusion. A test ending inconclusively is a valid outcome.

Do not prescribe a universal 70/30 allocation, daily spend floor, conversion count, fixed percentage increase or waiting period. Determine test capacity from the plausible event rate and uncertainty. If making a statistical claim, use a method suited to the experiment, baseline rate, effect size, randomisation and repeated checking; a fixed count of conversions does not establish significance.

## Campaign and audience structure

Use the selected platform's actual hierarchy and controls. Distinguish objectives, offers, markets or measurement needs only where that distinction helps execution or a test. Avoid splitting spend into more cells than the budget can meaningfully observe. Preserve stable IDs and an intelligible naming convention; names help reporting but do not replace campaign IDs.

For each channel record its funnel role, conversion definition, account/campaign scope, audience intent, creative requirement, budget limit, dates, reporting source and decision conditions. Check whether a targeting input is a strict restriction, an expansion signal or unavailable for that objective. Verify audience eligibility, exclusions and any policy-sensitive restrictions against current platform documentation and account state before implementation.

Retargeting windows follow the buying cycle, audience eligibility and observed conversion delay. Separate customer acquisition from retention or upsell; existing customers can be appropriate for the latter. Choose exclusions from the objective and evidence. Frequency controls may depend on platform and objective, so do not promise an unsupported cap. Avoid treating every short visit or prior conversion as a universal exclusion.

## Creative brief

Provide the verified offer, audience problem, intended action, claim sources, format and test hypothesis to the creative owner. PAS, before/after/bridge, feature-to-benefit and proof-led structures are options, not evidence of performance. Proof and urgency need approved sources. Use explicit placeholders for missing facts; never reuse a fictional testimonial, discount, result or scarcity claim as real copy. Match the ad promise to the landing page and downstream fulfillment.
