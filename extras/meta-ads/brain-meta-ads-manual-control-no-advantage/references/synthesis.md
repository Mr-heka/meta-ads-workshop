# Synthesis — Meta Ads Manual Control (vs Advantage+/Andromeda)

Built 2026-06-11 from 24 mined transcripts (26 pulled, 2 thin/skipped, 2 had no captions).
Context lens applied throughout: AU small-business lead-gen advertiser at 2-8k dollars/month.
Note: nearly all sources are e-commerce/ROAS-framed. Transferable units for lead-gen: swap ROAS for cost-per-lead against a target CPA.

---

## Theme 1 — "Creative IS the targeting" (CONSENSUS, strongest in the set)

The post-Andromeda consensus across every credible source: interest stacking and lookalikes are dead as primary levers. The algorithm finds the buyer; the ad defines who gets found.

- **Alex Fedotoff**: "Stop trying to outgamble Facebook algorithm... it's smarter than all of us combined." Facebook scans the image and matches it — match model age/look to buyer age.
- **Máté Hunyor**: "The targeting now happens on the creative side."
- **Chase Chappell**: unique creative formats (review / education / demo) are matched to buyer-journey stages by Meta itself.
- **Blue Sense Digital**: ~30% of people inside any interest group are mis-assigned; interests capture interaction, not intent.
- **Davie Fogarty** (100M+ dollars spent): the whole TOF/MOF/BOF interest playbook is "out of the window."
- **Dara Denney**: "it's ultimately the creative that drives the targeting and the best results."

**The catch for small accounts (CONTESTED — see Theme 7):** Chase Chappell argues under 30k dollars/month you're still *training* the model, so single-interest ad sets still work and going all-broad too early is premature. Sam Piliero (post-Andromeda) agrees in part: day 1 = one CBO with 1 broad + 2 *niche* interest ad sets to guide the pixel toward broad. This is the most relevant disagreement for a 2-8k dollars/month advertiser.

## Theme 2 — ABO vs CBO: a bet on your hit rate, not a doctrine

- **Scalability School** (hidden gem, the cleanest rule in the set): ABO is a bet you're right more often than Meta. "If your hit rate of the tests is high enough... you can squeeze more with [ABO]. If your hit rate is bad, then stick with CBO." Low budget or low hit rate → CBO.
- **Nick Theriot**: one campaign per objective, broad, CBO, all winners in one ad set + a dynamic-creative test ad set in the SAME campaign. Separate testing campaigns find 10-20 dollars/day winners that die at 1,000 dollars/day.
- **Professor Charley T**: testing in dedicated ABO wastes 90% of test spend on losers; test inside CBO where ads must "earn spend." "Spend is a meritocracy."
- **Dara Denney** (pre-Andromeda era, ~2021): isolated testing campaign, one ad set per test, one variable per test, all broad. She concedes the architecture suits 20k+ dollars/month — below that, drip creatives into core campaigns.
- **Sam Piliero**: hybrid — packs (new ad set per creative round) inside one prospecting CBO, force-fed with ad-set minimum budgets of 1x target CPA, hard 7-day removal.
- **Spencer Pawliw**: under 1,000 dollars/day never more than one campaign. Champions + test batches + offer tests as three ad-set types in ONE CBO. "There is no ad account structure that will save your bad ads."
- **Blue Sense Digital**: ABO vs CBO is risk preference; at scale, CBO with min/max spend limits (~60% controlled / 40% free) is the best of both. Rotate: CBO in Q4 (efficiency), ABO in Q1 (testing).

**Net for 2-8k dollars/month:** consolidation wins the argument. One campaign, one (or very few) ad sets, CBO or single-ad-set ABO — the structure matters less than conversion volume per learning unit (see Theme 4).

## Theme 3 — Cost caps & bid control: the most contested topic in the set

Three camps:

1. **Anti (testing and most accounts):**
   - **Charley T**: cost caps + ASC harvest bottom-funnel buyers who'd convert anyway — "your Facebook ROAS might look good but your bank account doesn't go up." If used at all, cap at 10-15%, max 20% of budget.
   - **Blue Sense Digital**: no cost/bid caps under 200k dollars/month. Kill flexible ads and Advantage+ creative enhancements too.
   - **Sam Piliero**: caps, day-parting, week-parting only earn their place at 100k+ dollars/month.
   - **Spencer Pawliw**: no caps, no forced minimums — forcing spend Meta won't fund is ego.
2. **Pro, but ONLY for scaling proven winners:**
   - **Scalability School**: "I don't like cost cap for testing, but I really really love them for scaling." One ad set, all proven winners ("gladiators arena"), 7-day click, cost cap — budget self-expands on high-conversion days (weekends), self-throttles midweek.
   - **Máté Hunyor**: cost cap at ~historical average CPA as a brake when performance dips.
3. **Pro as a mass-launch safety net (e-comm/influencer context):**
   - **The Bottom Line (Kynship)**: launch EVERYTHING (150+ assets), cost caps set 30-50% BELOW current actual CPA so day one spends zero, inch up every 24-48h until spend flows. "The only way you lose is out on opportunity."

**Net for 2-8k dollars/month:** consensus weight says skip caps entirely at this spend; if a manual brake is wanted, the Scalability School pattern (caps only on a proven-winners ad set) is the defensible version.

## Theme 4 — Small-budget operating rules (the most actionable theme)

- **Consolidation math** (Ben Heath): Meta's learning-phase exit is ~50 optimisation events per ad set per 7 days. 50 conversions/week across 5 ad sets = 10 each, Meta never learns; the same 50 through ONE ad set exits learning. One offer, one campaign, one ad set.
- **Charley T low-budget rule** (non-negotiable): if you can't reliably get one ad set out of learning, EVERYTHING lives in one ad set — one campaign, one ad set, a handful of 3-2-2 flex ads.
- **Test capacity formula** (Spencer Pawliw): daily budget ÷ target CPA = simultaneous tests. 100 dollars/day at a 20-dollar CPA = 5 concurrent tests. At 2-8k dollars/month (~65-260 dollars/day) with a ~28-dollar CPL target: 2-9 tests at any time.
- **Test budget formula** (Blue Sense): target conversions × expected CPA ÷ test days. Meta's official "50 conversions in 7 days" back-propagates to 714 dollars/day per test — absurd for small accounts; ignore it.
- **Kill rules ladder** (pick one, stick to it):
  - Hunyor: kill at 1x average CPA spent with zero conversions (cheapest).
  - Denney: kill at ~2x account-average CPA; kill the ad set if no winner in 5-7 days.
  - Fogarty: let each creative spend 2x AOV before judging.
  - Blue Sense: 3x target CPA minimum before kill/keep (prefers 5x); 0-1 conv = cut, 2 = another 50 dollars, 3+ = keep.
- **Learning phase is conversion-volume, not time** (Ben Heath): every significant edit resets it (~48h). Set a fixed optimization schedule — touch nothing more than once every 7-10 days on a small budget. "Don't helicopter parent your ad campaigns."
- **Never touch what's working** (Blue Sense golden rule, echoed by Pawliw and Hampton): do something NEXT to the winner, never to it. Duplicate via post ID (keeps social proof, no learning reset — Hunyor, Scalability School).
- **Break-even reframe** (Ben Heath): early campaigns are proof-of-concept that fund scale. "Much better to generate a 4x ROAS spending a million a month than a 10x spending 10,000 dollars."

## Theme 6 — Analysis discipline (manual control where it actually pays)

- **Decision metrics are CPA/ROAS only** (Ben Heath): CTR, CPC, CPM, hook rate explain WHY, never decide WHAT to kill. His 16% hook-rate ad lost on cost-per-purchase to a 10% one.
- **Hook rate** (3-sec plays ÷ impressions): >10% really good, <5% poor (Heath); Hunyor benchmarks ≥25% for e-comm; B2B runs lower.
- **Frequency**: cold max ~2.5, warm 6-8, hot retargeting 10+ is fine (Heath). Scalability School won't kill a good-CPA ad on frequency alone — with creative variety users see "seven different ads," not one ad seven times.
- **Attribution**: 7-day-click-only once view-through exceeds ~25% of conversions (Scalability School); Pawliw saw reported 7-10x ROAS collapse to a real 2-3x when view-through was stripped. In-platform numbers understate subscriptions/LTV (Heath: 4x reported vs ~20x LTV) and overstate retargeting.
- **Trend indexing** (Charley T): in a DCT, if one creative took 80% of spend and results were good, that creative drove it. "Spend is a meritocracy."
- **Diagnostic pairings** (Heath): good hook + low CTR = weak offer/proof; good CTR + poor conversion = landing page; link clicks vs LP views gap >50% = slow page or junk placements.
- **Stat-sig gate** (Heath): run a free significance calculator before declaring A beats B.

## Theme 7 — When manual wins vs when automation wins (the decision matrix)

**Let automation run (consensus):**
- Delivery/audience finding at ANY spend — broad beats interest stacks once the pixel is trained.
- Creative permutation matching (DCT) — "your guesswork will never beat the scientific method" (Charley T).
- Budget allocation across proven winners (CBO; Hampton's Champion CBO spent $1,179 → $2,200 day one, routing itself).

**Keep manual control (consensus):**
- STRUCTURE: campaign count, ad-set consolidation, exclusions (existing customers — Meta "has a tendency to overspend on your existing customers," Piliero).
- SETTINGS KILL-LIST: Advantage+ creative enhancements OFF (Blue Sense, Hunyor — "Meta ads are not at the level where they can make good ads"); flexible ads OFF for testing (no per-creative data — Pawliw, Blue Sense; Chappell dissents but only for already-validated single-format variations); Audience Network OFF (Hunyor's 14%-CTR accidental-click trap); untick "use as a suggestion" on retargeting lists or Meta turns your list into a broad seed (Piliero — the sharpest gotcha in the set); don't edit live ads, duplicate them (Hunyor); schedule launches for midnight or Meta dumps the day's budget into remaining hours (Pawliw).
- ANALYSIS + KILL/SCALE DECISIONS: human, on CPA only, on a fixed schedule.
- TEST DESIGN: one variable at a time, capped permutations.

**Where ASC/Advantage+ specifically stands:**
- **Nick Theriot** ran the only controlled test in the set: same ads, $7k/week manual prospecting at 2.91 ROAS vs $2k ASC at 2.14. His broad+CBO structure already IS Advantage+ minus the packaging — and ASC kills ad-set-level testing. Meta reps couldn't name a functional difference.
- **Charley T**: ASC is a quality equaliser — "it will make a D-minus account a C-plus; if you're great... your A-minus account a C-plus."
- **Ben Heath / Davie Fogarty**: pro — for simple accounts it's all you need.
- **Chase Chappell**: staged — Advantage+ as a container for proven ads under $30k/month; full automation earned at scale.

**Incrementality warning (Charley T, unique):** automation + cost caps over-index on bottom-funnel. Turning off a "bad CPA" ad can tank site traffic, email opens, and search volume — "Facebook ads are about amplifying a business model," and "ROAS is a lie to begin with." Judge changes on profit volume (total revenue minus ad spend), never per-ad attribution.

## Theme 8 — Spend-tier ladders (who says what kicks in when)

- **Sam Piliero**: <$3k/mo: 1 CBO (broad + 2 niche interests) + separate existing-customer campaign. $3-10k: pack system. $10-30k: "the messy middle" — watch overspend on warm. $100k+: caps, day-parting, incremental attribution.
- **Chase Chappell**: <$30k/mo: interest testing + creative testing + retargeting campaigns (his $8.8k/30d at 5x example). >$30k: CBO broad by product line. >$500k: pure Advantage+.
- **Blue Sense Digital**: <$50k: one testing campaign, ad sets = concepts (persona × angle × offer), 3-5 creatives each, 7-day click, exclude existing customers. Accounts noticeably improve past 100-200 conversions/month. CPMs inflate 20-30%/year — efficiency comes from creative, not knob-turning: "Could you squeeze 5% more with daily bid adjustments? Sure. Could you spend the same time making better creative and double the account? Definitely."
- **Ben Heath**: small = <$3k/month. Aggressive CPA targets (accept 2-3x ROAS / break-even) to buy learning volume.

**$2-8k/month placement:** bottom two rungs everywhere — one consolidated campaign, 2-9 concurrent tests max, no caps, manual kill rules, niche-interest guidance optional in month one, broad after the pixel warms.
