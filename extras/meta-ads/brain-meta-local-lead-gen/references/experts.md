# Experts Mined: Meta Local Lead Gen (radius/city targeting, small-geo saturation, local event fill)

21 videos, 18 channels. Popular vs hidden-gem labels come from the source index built at mining time (gems = small channel, top-decile engagement). Sorted gems first.

## Hidden gems

### Samuel Darby (hidden gem, 2 videos)
Meta media buyer since 2011, specialises in local businesses on small budgets; briefed by a Facebook engineer at Menlo Park in 2015. Angle: Andromeda is the old Power Five intensified, not a new paradigm. Uniquely adds: impression-based testing thresholds (not spend-based, ~2,000-3,000 impressions per ad for a local funnel), the 1-3x target CPL judging rule, format-grouped ad set trios (statics / UGC / carousel) to beat Meta's placement bias, CPM as a creative-vs-adset diagnostic, and honest scepticism that full Andromeda hand-off lasts more than a few months without fatigue.

### Ollie The Ad Man (hidden gem)
Agency operator across 40+ roofing ad accounts. Angle: the complete local-trade funnel end to end with real client numbers (58 leads, £2,500, £42/lead). Uniquely adds: the "anti-advertising" creative doctrine (owner, van, crew, finished jobs), the landing-page-plus-booking-redirect flow, the pay-for-reach reframe, and scale-efficiency-not-spend when margins are thin.

### Joshua cmb | Adding Ally (hidden gem)
Home-service ad operator (painters, detailers, fencers). Angle: lead gen failure is mostly a business-process problem, not a targeting problem. Uniquely adds: the CRM/follow-up layer (GoHighLevel at $97/month, instant SMS on submit), the average-job-value viability test ($10,000 painting job vs the $100 detailer who goes under), and "any CTA that doesn't create a contact first is a bad CTA".

### TradeGrowth (hidden gem)
Trade-growth agency founder. Angle: plain click-by-click 2026 Ads Manager walkthrough for local trades. Uniquely adds: the explicit radius guardrail (~30 miles, never a state, not too tiny), the four-part minimum viable funnel (simple campaign, clear offer, filtering form, 5-minute follow-up), and end-result offer copy examples with "free" as a CPL lever ($3.52-$4 CPL proof).

## Popular / established voices

### Nick Theriot
Highest-spend operator in the set ($10M+ on lead ads for one client). Uniquely adds: conditional pixel firing (unqualified leads hit a no-pixel thank-you page so Meta trains only on qualified traffic), the persistent single-campaign doctrine ($6.5M in one campaign, 192 ad sets), concept-per-ad-set testing with one weekly upload day, and post-lead ThruPlay retargeting plus a 20-30 email nurture sequence.

### Daniel Dimsey (3 videos)
Solo home-service operator (window cleaning) showing his own account. Uniquely adds: exclusion pins ringing the radius boundary, the mandatory manual-entry phone question (min 5, max 20 characters) to kill stale autofill numbers, the ~500,000 minimum population floor before recommending Google Ads instead, and the personal face-in-uniform creative stance over AI images.

### Derek Videll
Course-format walkthrough for owners running their own leads campaigns. Uniquely adds: the quality-vs-quantity dial framing on every setup decision, the hard-rule toggles (untick "use as a suggestion" on location and age), the Higher Intent slide-to-submit bot filter, the $25/day starting "magic number", and the carousel ban plus never-set-an-end-date rule.

### Big Little Gyms
Gym specialist (1,500+ gyms), the heaviest source on small-geo saturation. Uniquely adds: frequency as the saturation metric (keep under ~2, danger near 3), radius benchmarks by density (7 miles suburban / 15-20 rural / 1-2 urban), zip-code layering over radius to stop 50-100 mile bleed, the self-liquidating front-end offer maths, and never toggling a campaign off-and-on.

### Blake Bauer Business
Instant-form specialist. Uniquely adds: first-position disqualifying question with conditional end pages, the work-email filter for B2B, offer-led form headlines, and updating Lead Center statuses because Meta uses that data to find better leads.

### Jonas Olson
17-year pest/lawn operator scaled to 17 locations in 5 states. Uniquely adds: CPL benchmarks per objective (click-to-call $15-40, lead form $12-40, traffic $0.20-0.30), the 5-minute speed-to-lead rule, the 21-22 exposure repetition stat, and the 7-step local ad copy framework with neighbourhood-level call-outs and social proof.

### Ash Davis
Service-business owner arguing Andromeda shifted optimisation from lead volume to client value (his July: $15,000 spend, $51,000 tracked Stripe revenue). Uniquely adds: judge months on revenue not CPL, do not kill ads Meta keeps actively serving (contrarian), educational long-form repurposed as ads, and why lead vendors lose the Andromeda data advantage.

### Caden Thompson
Post-Andromeda framework builder rather than tutorial maker. Uniquely adds: the lag-time/7-day attribution window logic, the 15-30 optimisation events per month floor, and back-calculating cost-per-sold-lead from pipeline ratios when the bottom-funnel event is too thin.

### roasbrez
Freelancer/agency angle (running local campaigns as a service). Uniquely adds: Ad Library competitor mining before creative build, budget-dictates-creative-count logic, and the retainer-vs-per-lead billing comparison.

### Cam Meunier
Brick-and-mortar case study (coffee shop). Uniquely adds: dual simultaneous radius ad sets (5 mile vs 2-3 mile) to test rather than guess, the historical seasonal baseline workaround for untrackable offline conversions, and feeding website copy into primary text as Advantage+ audience context.

### Aqua Leads | Contractor Marketing Pros
Contractor-marketing operator (pool builders). Uniquely adds: interruption-vs-intent platform framing (Meta vs Google), the 7-step video ad script, the paid-per-impression CPL logic, the ~1-in-3 ready-to-buy estimate, and the three-pixel retargeting layer (site, lead-form opens, page).

## Source quality notes

- **Usable sources: 18 of 21** (3 era-flagged excluded above; 0 flagged thin). Above the 8-source floor.
- **Freshness**: all videos passed the <=18-month rule at discovery. The only freshness exceptions are the three era-flags: Kyran Rawson (hard, pre-Andromeda structure as current), Rich From Anywhere and Mike Sherrard (soft, older-era defaults or UI presented as current).
- **Coverage by scope area**: radius/city mechanics and lead quality filters are richly covered (multiple independent practitioners with numbers). Small-geo saturation has solid but narrower backing (Big Little Gyms carries most of the frequency data, Dimsey the population floor). **Local event fill specifically is the thin area**: no source runs event-seat campaigns as their core business; the playbooks here are service-business lead gen (roofing, gyms, detailing, pools, pest) and the event-fill translation is ours, done in the SKILL.md application section rather than claimed from sources.
- **Vertical skew**: heavy on home services and gyms, one real estate source (excluded), no B2B events or workshops. Treat vertical-specific numbers (CPL benchmarks, radius sizes) as directional, not portable.
- **Verified-base conflicts**: Ollie's daily scaling pace vs the 3-day rule; Ash Davis's don't-kill-served-ads vs the kill rule. Both flagged in synthesis.md, verified base wins. (The third historic conflict — instant-form majority vs an LP-only position — is decided in `brain-meta-instant-forms-vs-lp`; note that early anti-form cost evidence turned out to be an unaccepted-ToS artefact. See synthesis.md Theme 5.)
