## What the experts agree on

1. **Broad radius is the targeting; the ad does the narrowing.** Draw the geography, set a minimum age at most, leave interests blank. The creative and copy tell Meta who to find. (Samuel Darby, TradeGrowth, Nick Theriot, roasbrez, Cam Meunier, Daniel Dimsey, Jonas Olson, Big Little Gyms, Caden Thompson. Matches the verified base in brain-meta-ads-manual-control-no-advantage.)
2. **Meta leaks outside your drawn radius unless you stop it.** Ring the boundary with exclusion pins (Daniel Dimsey), untick the "Reach more people likely to respond to your ad" suggestion toggle so the radius is a rule (Derek Videll), layer zip codes over the radius because radius-only can pull people from 50-100 miles away (Big Little Gyms).
3. **One simple campaign, one or few ad sets, creative volume inside.** No funnel-stage ad sets, no retargeting splits for local. (Ollie The Ad Man: 1 campaign, 1 ad set, 4-6 ads. Caden Thompson: one campaign, one ad set, one event. Nick Theriot: one persistent campaign for years, $6.5M through it. Samuel Darby: cold and warm creatives blended in one ad set.)
4. **Lead quality is engineered at the form, not the audience.** Qualifying questions with conditional logic before contact fields; budget/timeline segmentation; manual phone entry; pixel fires only on qualified. (Samuel Darby, Nick Theriot, Daniel Dimsey, Blake Bauer, Derek Videll, Joshua cmb.)
5. **Speed-to-lead decides conversion more than the ad account does.** Contact within ~5 minutes or the lead dies; CRM automation is mandatory. (Jonas Olson, Big Little Gyms, Joshua cmb, TradeGrowth, Ollie The Ad Man, Nick Theriot.)
6. **Small geographies fatigue fast, so refresh is scheduled, not reactive.** Watch frequency against **the canonical bands (2.5 warn / 3.0 rotate / 3.5 refresh / 4.0 immediate)** — see "Frequency: one ladder" below, and use no other numbers. Keep 3-6 ads live (Ollie), rotate 2-3 new variants monthly against the winner (Aqua Leads), and respect a minimum audience size (Dimsey: ~500K comfortable floor; Big Little Gyms: 50-100K+ workable).
7. **Local creative that wins does not look like an ad.** Owner's face, real crew, real jobs, local call-outs, offer-led plain copy. (Ollie The Ad Man, Daniel Dimsey, Jonas Olson, Aqua Leads, TradeGrowth.)
8. **Judge on cost per lead against a target, on a fixed cadence, and never toggle live campaigns.** Kill at breakeven or 1-3x target CPL, scale roughly 20% at a time, never turn a campaign off and back on. (Ollie, Samuel Darby, Nick Theriot, Big Little Gyms.)

Full theme detail and supporting evidence for each consensus point: `references/synthesis.md` (Themes 1-10); no need to reload the quotes here when that file is already open.

## Named frameworks & methods

- **Anti-advertising strategy (Ollie The Ad Man).** 10-20 raw photos: owner next to branded van, crew, finished jobs with customers. Ads that do not register as ads. Paired with his full funnel: mobile LP (86% of traffic was mobile), multi-step qualifying form, post-submit redirect to a self-booking calendar. Proof numbers: 58 leads, £2,500 spend, £42/lead.
- **Ad set trios / format grouping (Samuel Darby).** Each ad set holds exactly 3 ads grouped by format (statics / UGC / carousel) so Meta's placement bias cannot dump budget into video and starve images. Live result: static-text trio at £8.99 CPL vs £15-16 target. Judging rule: spend 1-3x target CPL per ad before calling it. Impression rule: ~1,000 impressions per click-step, so 2,000-3,000 impressions per ad for a local lead funnel.
- **Pre-test then consolidate (Samuel Darby).** Test avatars/pain points in controlled ad sets at $30-40/day each (5 pains x 2-3 hooks x 3 formats), then consolidate proven angles into one CBO campaign after ~2 weeks.
- **Conditional pixel firing (Nick Theriot).** LP, then a JotForm with qualifying logic: qualified answers route to a thank-you page carrying the pixel, unqualified to a page with no pixel. Meta only ever trains on qualified leads. Plus: one persistent campaign, concept-per-ad-set with 3 single-variable creatives, budget moved plus or minus 20% on the last 3 days of data.
- **7-step local ad copy framework (Jonas Olson).** Scroll-stop creative, local call-out ("Hey OKC homeowners"), name the neighbourhood-specific pain, brief solution, emotional why, local social proof, single CTA, PS urgency. His objective benchmarks: click-to-call $15-40/lead, lead forms $12-40/lead, traffic clicks $0.20-0.30.
- **7-step interruption video script (Aqua Leads).** Problem call-out, curiosity confirmation, sell the dream, throw rocks at the enemies, present the opportunity, take it away, qualify-framed CTA plus urgency. Cull monthly on CPL: $18/$16/$12 test kept the $12.
- **Four-part minimum viable local funnel (TradeGrowth).** Simple campaign + clear end-result offer + filtering lead form (3-5 questions, phone mandatory) + follow-up inside 5 minutes with CRM connected. Proof: $3.52-$4 CPL.
- **The friction dial (Joshua cmb and Caden Thompson, citing Hormozi).** Less friction = more volume, more friction = better quality. Start at quantity, walk toward quality (Derek Videll's version: "turn all the dials to quantity when I first start a campaign and then go backwards").
- **Saturation dashboard (Big Little Gyms).** Frequency under ~2 (danger near 3); radius by density (7 miles suburban, 15-20 rural, 1-2 urban); zip-code layering; $30/day floor, ideally $50; self-liquidating front-end offer ($200 spend, 20 leads, 1 sale at $400 = 2x on spend).
- **Manual phone entry filter (Daniel Dimsey).** A required short-answer "What is your phone number?" field (min 5, max 20 characters) forces retyping and kills stale autofilled numbers. Plus exclusion pins ringing every radius.

## Contrarian / disputed takes

- **Instant forms vs landing pages (the sharpest split).** Pro instant forms: Samuel Darby (local buyers act in the moment, leaving Meta loses them), Daniel Dimsey, TradeGrowth, Blake Bauer. Pro LP: Ollie The Ad Man (instant forms prefill junk), Nick Theriot (LP lets you control what the pixel learns). Instant forms versus landing pages is decided in `brain-meta-instant-forms-vs-lp`; read it before choosing. Short version: for cheap, simple offers an instant form with a qualifying question can work; for high-ticket or considered offers, send traffic to a landing page.
- **Don't kill ads Meta keeps serving (Ash Davis, minority).** Post-Andromeda he argues an ad Meta actively spends on is doing buyer-journey work even at 1 appointment in 30 days, and his revenue rose while CPL rose. Directly contradicts the kill-rule discipline of Ollie, Theriot and Darby, and the 3x-CPL/7-day kill rule. Treat as a lens for months where revenue is up, never as licence to keep bleeding ads.
- **Carousel ads — reconciled 2026-07-28.** Derek Videll: never, "algorithm hates carousel ads". Samuel Darby: runs a carousel trio ad set as one of his format groups. **Settled position, matching `brain-meta-placements-and-formats`:** carousel is **not a default format** — video and single-image win most local lead-gen tests, so never reach for it first. When you do have a reason to run one (a sequence, a multi-location list, a step-by-step), run it **Feed-only in its own ad set**, because the swipe-through only renders in Feed and Stories/Reels flatten or crop it, and because mixing it with video in one ad set makes the format comparison meaningless. That resolves both camps: Videll is right that it is a poor default, Darby is right that it is a legitimate grouped format when isolated.
- **End dates.** Derek Videll: never set one (expired campaigns cannot be reactivated). Big Little Gyms: set one, because manually pausing and resuming resets delivery learning. Both agree on the real rule: never toggle a live campaign.
- **Higher Intent vs More Volume form setting.** Derek Videll: Higher Intent slide-to-submit gives zero bot leads. Daniel Dimsey and Blake Bauer: stay on More Volume (the slider confuses older customers; volume feeds the algorithm).
- **Minimum audience size.** Daniel Dimsey: under ~500K in the radius, go Google Ads instead. Big Little Gyms: 50-100K+ is workable for gyms. Both agree small pools fatigue faster; they disagree where the floor sits.
- **Demographic layering.** Big Little Gyms still layers income-bracket zip codes for gyms; everyone else in the set leaves detailed targeting blank. The verified base sides with blank.
- **Scaling pace.** Ollie: 10-20% budget bumps daily. Nick Theriot and Samuel Darby: ~20% every 3 days. Our doctrine matches Theriot; never same-day.

## Execution playbook

### IF/THEN operating rules

- IF building any local campaign THEN one campaign, one ad set (or few, grouped by format), 4-6 ads, radius plus minimum age only, interests blank (Ollie The Ad Man, Caden Thompson, Samuel Darby).
- IF setting the radius THEN drop a pin on the actual location, size it to how far people will genuinely travel, then ring the boundary with exclusion pins AND untick the location suggestion toggle (Daniel Dimsey, Derek Videll).
- IF unsure between two radius sizes THEN run both as parallel ad sets and let the data pick (Cam Meunier: 5-mile vs 2-3 mile simultaneously).
- IF ads are serving outside the service area THEN add exclusion pins first, check the suggestion toggle second, layer zip/postcode lists third (Dimsey, Videll, Big Little Gyms, in that order of effort).
- IF frequency crosses **2.5** or CPL is climbing in a small city THEN ship new creative concepts, do not raise spend (Big Little Gyms). Full ladder: "Frequency: one ladder" below — 2.5 warn / 3.0 rotate / 3.5 refresh / 4.0 immediate.
- IF an ad has spent 1-3x target CPL with no lead THEN kill it (Samuel Darby) — subject to the Neiman gate: no verdict below $100 + 7 days + 3 conversions. IF an ad is above breakeven CPL after a fair read THEN kill it and let the cheapest ad absorb the spend (Ollie).
- IF one ad's CPM is a wild outlier vs the batch THEN suspect the creative (policy-adjacent keyword, image text); IF every ad runs hot THEN suspect the ad set (audience too small, wrong event) (Samuel Darby).
- IF scaling THEN adjust roughly 20% on the last 3 days of data, never same-day, biased against decreasing (Nick Theriot).
- IF lead quality is poor THEN add friction at the form, not the audience: qualifying questions first, budget/timeline options, manual phone entry, price stated as a fact (Darby, Dimsey, Videll, Blake Bauer).
- IF a lead arrives THEN automated SMS/email fires instantly and a human follows inside 5 minutes; the post-submit page pushes straight to a booking calendar (Jonas Olson, Big Little Gyms, Ollie, Joshua cmb).
- IF the true conversion (a sale, a show-up) lags past Meta's 7-day window or lands under ~15-30 events/month THEN optimise on the nearest upstream event and back-calculate cost-per-sold from pipeline ratios (Caden Thompson).
- IF tempted to pause a live campaign briefly THEN don't; it resets delivery learning and rarely recovers (Big Little Gyms).

### Default numbers the experts use

- Starting budget: $20-50/day (Videll $25, TradeGrowth $20, Big Little Gyms $30-50, Dimsey AU $50-100/day). Our doctrine: CBO $50/day per city campaign.
- Radius: ~30 miles / 50 km for a service city (TradeGrowth); 7 miles suburban, 15-20 rural, 1-2 dense urban for a physical venue draw (Big Little Gyms).
- Audience floor: 300-500K comfortable, fatigue risk grows below (Dimsey); 50-100K workable minimum (Big Little Gyms).
- Frequency: **see "Frequency: one ladder" below.** 2.5 warn / 3.0 rotate / 3.5 refresh / 4.0 immediate. The looser "~2, act by 2.5-3" phrasing the sources use is superseded.
- Ads live at once: 3 minimum, 4-6 standard (Ollie); 3 per format-grouped ad set (Darby).
- Judging thresholds: **the verdict rule is the house gate — no kill or scale call below $100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions.** Everything the sources quote is secondary context for whether an ad has had a fair look, not a competing rule: 1-3x target CPL spend per ad (Darby), ~2,000-3,000 impressions per ad for a local funnel (Darby), 7-day CPL view (Theriot). Sibling brains quoting "48-72 hours", "2-3 days per cell" or "at least 7 days" defer to the same gate. At small local volumes, read the noise warning in `brain-meta-creative-strategist-manual` before calling one ad better than another.
- Creative refresh: 2-3 new variants per month against the standing winner (Aqua Leads); new concepts weekly at higher spend (Theriot).
- Form: 3-5 questions, never 15; phone mandatory (TradeGrowth, Dimsey).
- Speed-to-lead: 5 minutes (Olson, Big Little Gyms, TradeGrowth).

### Frequency: one ladder

The sources in this corpus quote at least four different frequency ceilings. **Standardised 2026-07-28 — use these bands and no others**, across this brain and every sibling:

| Frequency (rolling 7-day) | Action |
|---|---|
| **2.5** | Warn. Start briefing the next creative round now. |
| **3.0** | Rotate within 7 days. |
| **3.5** | Refresh now. |
| **4.0** | Immediate — pull or replace the creative today. |

Two supporting rules: a fatigue call needs **two or more signals moving together** over rolling 7-day windows (frequency plus CTR decay or CPL drift), not frequency alone; and **fewer than 3 new creatives shipped in a month** is itself a fatigue flag regardless of what the frequency number says. Small geographies hit these bands far faster than broad ones — that is the whole reason this brain exists.

### Pre-flight checklist

1. Radius drawn on a dropped pin, sized to real travel behaviour, exclusion pins ringing the boundary.
2. Location and age suggestion toggles unticked (hard rules, not suggestions).
3. One campaign, one ad set (or format trios), 4-6 ads covering different angles, cold and proof creatives blended.
4. Offer copy is end-result and local ("Hey [city] business owners"), not trust adjectives; LP copy keyword-loaded for Meta's classifier.
5. Form or LP has qualifying questions before contact fields; phone entry manual; post-submit pushes to booking.
6. CRM wired: instant SMS/email on submit, human follow-up inside 5 minutes, nurture sequence live.
7. Target CPL and breakeven CPL written down before launch; kill and scale rules agreed.
8. Frequency and audience-size baseline noted so saturation is measurable from day one.

### Top 5 failure modes and fixes

1. **Geo leakage**: budget serves 50-100 miles out. Fix: exclusion pins + untick suggestion toggle + zip layering (Dimsey, Big Little Gyms, Videll).
2. **One-ad fatigue in a small pool**: single creative burns the town in weeks. Fix: 4-6 ads live, monthly rotation, and act on the one frequency ladder above (2.5 warn / 3.0 rotate / 3.5 refresh / 4.0 immediate) — Ollie, Aqua Leads, Big Little Gyms.
3. **Slow follow-up kills good leads**: best ad account cannot save a 1-hour response. Fix: automated instant contact plus 5-minute human follow-up plus booking-page redirect (Olson, Joshua cmb, Ollie).
4. **Judging ads too early or on the wrong metric**: pausing at day 1-2 or on CTR. Fix: hold to the house gate ($100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions) before any verdict, and decide on CPL only. Darby's 1-3x-target-CPL spend and ~2,000-3,000 impressions are context for whether the ad got a fair look, not permission to call it earlier (Darby, Theriot).
5. **Frankenstein structure**: funnel-stage ad sets, retargeting splits, constant new campaigns, on/off toggling. Fix: one persistent simple campaign; do things NEXT to winners, never to them (Theriot, Caden Thompson, Big Little Gyms).

## Applied to your business

This section carries no account-specific facts. Account IDs, prices and CPL figures were removed 2026-07-28; fill in your own below before using anything above.

**Write down first:**
- Ad account `<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>` · pixel `<YOUR_PIXEL_ID>` · page `<YOUR_PAGE_ID>` · CRM `[name]`
- The local offer and its price: `[offer]` at `[$price]`
- Budget per local campaign or event: `[$X]` total, `[$Y]`/day
- Target cost per lead `[$X]` and breakeven cost per lead `[$Y]` — write both down *before* launch, not after
- Radius and audience size the radius produces: `[N] km`, `[N]` people. Under ~300-500K, expect fast fatigue and plan the creative pipeline accordingly

**Which of your offers this feeds**
- **Any location-bound offer** — a venue, a service area, an in-person event. Each location is its own local-fill campaign, which is exactly the small-geo problem this brain covers.
- **Offers sold nationally or online**: geography inverted. This brain still helps, by telling you which metros to EXCLUDE so your local campaigns and your national ones stop cannibalising each other.
- **Downstream offers** (membership, retainer, higher-ticket install): not local campaigns themselves, but they are why a higher cost per local lead can be affordable. Work out that number before you set the CPL target.

**Consensus translated into execution moves**
1. **Treat each location like a service suburb.** Pin on the actual venue, size the radius to how far people genuinely travel (roughly 40-50km for a metro event draw; tighter in dense urban, wider rural), ring the boundary with exclusion pins, and set the location and age toggles as hard rules rather than suggestions. That keeps every dollar inside the draw distance.
2. **Run the saturation dashboard on every campaign.** Take a frequency baseline at launch and act on the single ladder above (2.5 / 3.0 / 3.5 / 4.0), with a fatigue call requiring two or more signals moving together over rolling 7-day windows, and fewer than 3 new creatives a month treated as a flag on its own. Keep 3-6 ads live. Record your own fatigue history per location — `[location]` went from `[$CPL]` to `[$CPL]` over `[period]` — because that local curve is the only reliable predictor of the next one.
3. **Engineer friction at the form, not the audience.** Qualifying questions before contact fields (business type, size, timeline, budget), phone entry manual, and route only qualified respondents to the pixelled thank-you page so the algorithm trains on real leads.
4. **Speed-to-lead through the CRM.** Instant SMS/email on submit, post-submit page pushing straight to booking or checkout, human follow-up inside 5 minutes. This decides conversion more than any ad-account setting.
5. **Anti-advertising creative.** Real rooms, real people, real work, location-named call-outs ("`[city]` business owners"), never polished agency templates. Track which register wins for you: `[creative type]` at `[$CPL]` vs `[creative type]` at `[$CPL]`.

**What may NOT apply to you, and why**
- **"Switch Advantage+ Audience back to original."** **Re-correction 2026-08-25: that control DOES still exist.** The 2026-07-28 claim that it was mandatory on Sales/Leads/App objectives was wrong — verified live against the API, `advantage_audience` accepts both 1 and 0 on `OUTCOME_LEADS` ad sets. What IS true: with it **ON**, the API fixes `age_max` at 65 (error 1870189, per Meta's developer docs) and audience includes degrade to *suggestions* Meta may expand past; with it **OFF**, includes bind again. Location is the control that matters most here, and it is fully yours either way: draw the radius, ring it with exclusion pins, untick the "reach more people likely to respond" suggestion toggle. Read every "leave interests blank / go manual" line above as "the remaining controls plus the creative are your targeting" — which was always this brain's actual argument. Advantage+ Placements stays on, separately and by choice.
- **"Load one 3-2-2 DCT per concept."** **Correction 2026-07-28: the DCT container is gone.** Dynamic Creative became the Flexible ad format, and Flexible was removed as a standalone ad-setup option in March 2026 — the functionality is now a **"Flexible media" toggle inside Advantage+ creative**. So "enhancements off but DCT on" is self-cancelling in the current UI. The testing logic is unchanged: a small, human-authored grid (3 creatives x 2 texts x 2 headlines, all your own inputs). Run it either as **separate standard ads** (per-asset data, the right choice when you need to know which creative won) or as **Flexible media with each individual enhancement switched off** (reach and speed, ad-level data only). Pick per round and record the choice.
- **"Don't kill ads Meta keeps serving" (Ash Davis).** Conflicts with any kill discipline. Use it only as a lens when reading a whole month's revenue, never as licence to keep bleeding.
- **Daily 10-20% budget bumps.** Too fast for a small local budget. Step roughly +20% every 3 days on a multi-day average, never same-day.
- **Special Ad Category constraints (housing, credit, employment, social issues) and per-lead client billing.** Only relevant if you are in those verticals or that business shape. If you ARE in a Special Ad Category, note it removes most remaining targeting controls including radius precision — check before you plan.
- **Satisfaction guarantees and "free" giveaway hooks.** Check what you can actually stand behind and what your market's rules allow. Genuine expiring pricing and real dates are the safest urgency.
