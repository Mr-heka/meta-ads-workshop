# Worked example: a failed video and a seven-field form

Illustrative scenario. The observations below are supplied test inputs, not current facts about any real page or ad account.

**Request:** Our lead-generation page is receiving paid social traffic but converting poorly. The hero video returns 404 and the form has seven fields. What should we fix first?

## Establish what the evidence says

The failed media request is a concrete defect. Seven fields describe the form's size, but do not establish that it asks for unnecessary information or that the fields cause the low conversion rate. The request supplies no evidence of a missing Purchase event, no traffic denominator and no downstream qualification results.

Inspect the actual page and completion path. Check whether visitors can still understand the offer and act when the video fails; inspect every required field, labels, errors and the submitted result. Reconcile the conversion definition and reporting window before attributing poor performance to design.

## Repair in a useful order

1. **Restore the intended media or an approved fallback.** Trace the failed URL, access requirements and player configuration. Repair the source if available; otherwise use an accurate approved fallback that preserves the offer and CTA. Verify playback and the failure state on intended devices. A broken video is harmful, but it does not prove the whole page converts at zero.
2. **Verify the form's function and necessity.** Test validation, keyboard use, correction, duplicate-submit handling and the completion state. Map each required field to the actual process. Remove or defer fields that are unnecessary, while preserving required qualification and consent. Do not automatically replace the form with name/email/phone.
3. **Check the conversion path.** Determine what a successful lead means and inspect the authorized destination. A UI success message, stored lead and triggered follow-up are different outcomes. Use a controlled test with the appropriate authority; a page audit does not authorize sending messages or changing live automation.
4. **Improve the page's explanation where inspection shows a gap.** Align the incoming promise with the headline, offer details and next step. Keep required price, date, venue and terms visible where they support the decision. Choose form position from intent and complexity; paid social alone does not decide it.
5. **Review proof and layout.** Preserve relevant approved proof and check readability, mobile wrapping, zoom, movement, contrast and CTA access. A real testimonial does not require exposing a full identity where permission or privacy limits apply. Add no bonus, scarcity claim or promise unless it is part of the approved offer.
6. **Define the remaining experiment.** Once functional defects are fixed, choose one material uncertainty, such as the explanation before the form. Set an outcome and evaluation plan from traffic, baseline rate, plausible effect and conversion delay. Compare qualified leads and downstream outcomes, not just raw submissions.

## Report the result honestly

A completed repair report would identify the files or page elements changed, the successful media and form checks, any unverified downstream effects and the next conversion hypothesis. It would not invent a percentage lift or claim a winner from the repair alone.
