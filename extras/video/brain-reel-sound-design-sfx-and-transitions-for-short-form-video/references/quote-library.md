# Quote Library — reel sound design, SFX and transitions for short-form video editing

## Sync
> "Identify key moments. If you try to make every moment special, none of them will be special." — Film Editing Pro

> Peak-to-peak alignment is "the difference between pro and amateur sound." — Gavin Herman

> "Editing with waveforms visible is vital. When lining up the apex of a sound effect to your edit, you can add markers to make it easier." — Film Editing Pro

> Graphic shown early or late "just kind of feels weird." — Marcus Jones

> "I line things up so that at this exact moment the music picks up." — Herman Huang (marking the problem→solution shift)

## Palette
> "When something moves you should add a whoosh, and when something's highlighted a highlight sound." — Learn By Leo

> Build 50-60 sounds total; "you'll never need to update your sound effects library" again. — Joseph | Video Editing

> "You have to use sound effects very smartly so that maximum things are covered." — NOX CUT

> "Gritty bass hits aren't going to work for your corporate videos... clean bell dings won't work for your street wear promo." — Herman Huang

> SFX should be "just audible... it should not prick." — NOX CUT

> "You don't have to become a DJ." — NOX CUT (music under voice)

## Transitions
> Risers "mostly start on a low note then end on a high note... this creates suspense and intensity." — NOX CUT

> Riser → smash cut → silence: the contrast "hits way harder" with a build-up first. — Tim Runia

> "Surprise only works if it's... surprising." — Tim Runia (smash-cut overuse)

> "The beginning part of that whoosh builds into the next shot... we get a really nice merging of the two scenes." — Jason Yadlovski

> "Your brain remembers unfinished things... way better than things that are concluded." — Dylan (Zeigarnik across transitions)

> "The bigger the shock, the bigger the snap the better." — Kallaway (contrarian snapback)

## Mistakes
> Risers: "only use them if something important is actually going to happen or else they will lose their reputation." — Herman Huang

> Silent motion feels "hollow and fake." — Abhay PS Rajawat

> Over-cutting for variety creates "visual mush." — Learn By Leo

> Low-quality source audio is "like trying to cook a high-end meal with rotten ingredients." — Skymography

> "If someone finishes your video and says 'oh that editing was pretty cool,' you actually missed the mark." — Dylan

> "It's peaking even though our LUFS are looking correct." — Greg Edits Video (check true peak)

> Dry library SFX sound "stocky" — EQ + reverb them. — Gaku Lange (inferred)
