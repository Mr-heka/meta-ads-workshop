## The 10 mistakes new video editors make (review checklist)

From video 03, "10 Mistakes NEW Video Editors Make" (842K views, his most-viewed file). Run any talking-head edit against this.

### Mistake 1: Not considering the edit while filming

> "When you're filming your content you want to make sure that you are asking yourself: am I definitely going to be using this? Is the length of time that I've captured of this shot, is that enough or do I need to get more? You don't want to have extra footage for footage sake because more footage means more footage you need to edit, so it's going to slow down your editing process."

### Mistake 2: Jumping in without a plan

> "Jumping straight into your video editing without having a plan in place for the end video ... what are the goals of the video, why are you making it, what do you want it to achieve, what do you want people to think, to feel, to know or to do after watching your video. Having a plan ... is going to make your editing far more targeted, far more streamlined and much more efficient."

The four-part goal question, **think / feel / know / do**, is the planning anchor.

### Mistake 3: No process to edit down

> "Not having a process in place to edit your videos down. We see so many people just jump into the editing applications, they'll dump their footage into the timeline and they'll just start color grading or tweaking the audio. You really need to focus on the content first ... jumping straight into color grading or tweaking the sound ... is the stuff that you need to do later in the process."

### Mistake 4: Poor file management

> "File management or file organization. You want to make sure that you are organizing everything that you are using in your editing project into a logical place, and ideally all in the one place, in the one folder ... having a folder for your music, for all of your sound effects, all of your footage, all of your b-roll."

Matters most when coming back to a project later or handing it to another editor.

### Mistake 5: Not learning keyboard shortcuts

> "Learning and understanding the keyboard shortcuts are going to speed up your editing tenfold ... simple keyboard shortcuts like being able to play forward, backwards and stop, learning how to trim the top of your clip or the tail of your clip, or ripple edit left and ripple edit right."

Top ones: **JKL** playback, **ripple edit left/right** (aka trim top/tail).

### Mistake 6: Wrong or too-loud music

> "Using the wrong music in your videos ... having music in it that is the wrong fit for your video and the feeling that you want your viewers to have ... The music is the easiest way to make your viewers feel something while they're watching your content. So the mistake here is having the wrong music in your videos or having the music in your videos much too loud so it's distracting and it's annoying."

Test: "what do you want your viewers to think and to feel and to do after watching your video, and how does the music help or add to that? If it doesn't, turn it down or remove it."

### Mistake 7: Over-editing

> "Over editing your videos: making way too many cuts, removing out every little pause or every little breath, just adding cuts for the sake of adding cuts, or going the other way and adding in way too many transitions and too many effects. All that does is annoy the viewers ... If anything stick to simple cuts, remove the mistakes, tighten up stuff if it needs it. The more you simplify your videos the easier they're going to be for you to edit, but also the easier for your viewers to watch."

### Mistake 8: Not saving backup versions

> "Not saving backup versions of your timeline or of your editing project as you progress your edit ... this doesn't give you anywhere to go backwards if you need to ... save [a version] every time you hit a milestone or ideally every time you're moving into one of the next steps."

### Mistake 9: Software-hopping

> "Thinking that the video editing software that you are currently using isn't the one that you should be using, or that there's something better out there. This is a huge thing that is slowing down and stopping a lot of people. The grass is always greener on the other side ... The best video editing software for you is the one that is the fastest and the most efficient for you to do everything you need to do."

Only look at other software when "you start hitting the limits of what you can do." Focus on "really learning editing as an art form and as a process ... you can apply that and apply the fundamentals to any video editing software."

### Mistake 10: Editing front-to-back instead of backwards

Justin frames this as a pro tip rather than a strict mistake. Filming so the last take is always the best one, then editing backwards from the end of the timeline. Full detail in `editing-method.md` under Signature Techniques. "This tip is an absolute game changer."

## How to decide which videos to make (the 5 lenses)

From video 06, "How I Actually Decide Which Videos to Make". The best topics sit where several lenses overlap, "think of this like a Venn diagram ... ideally what you're trying to find is something that's going to overlap multiple of these things because that's where your best content's going to live."

1. **Excitement.** "This is always the starting point for me. What am I excited about right now that my audience would also see value in ... if you're excited about the topic, that's going to flow through into the video content itself ... and there's a good chance that you're not going to procrastinate."
2. **What has already worked.** "From my existing videos that are live on YouTube, which ones have worked well, which ones should I update or remake?" Roundups especially ("best editing software" each year), "people will tune back in from watching previous years to see what's changed."
3. **Adjacent ideas.** "From understanding and knowing what has worked well ... we can also work out what other types of content might our viewers be interested in."
4. **The viewer's lens.** "You're putting yourself in the place of your viewers ... what are their biggest pains, their problems, what are the things that they're actively trying to solve, or the things that they're searching for." Check competitors' top-performing videos as proof of demand. "I'm not by any means saying go rip off their content. I'm saying we're able to gauge that there is demand for that thing."
5. **Search volume.** "What are people actually typing into Google and YouTube ... there are keyword and SEO tools that will even tell you how many searches per month each of those phrases or search terms have." Use it to find topics and to validate ones you already have.

Then a topic goes into an AI prompt to generate the **title**, "which is going to be the framing for the viewer for that specific piece of content."

## The business metric: views do not matter

From videos 06 and 11. The most important strategic principle for Selr AI.

> "There's a very good chance that the most viewed videos on your YouTube channel are probably not your best videos. And if you're picking your next video topics based on what YouTube says is performing, you might be making the wrong videos." (video 06)

> "The YouTube Studio Analytics is designed to show you what's good for YouTube. It's not designed to show you what's good for your business ... When the only metrics you see are algorithm metrics, then you're likely going to make algorithm content. You end up chasing the content that gets views, gets subscribers, but the whole time you might actually be ignoring the content that really builds your business." (video 11)

Primal Video's proof: "YouTube's top 15 have two times the views, but they generate 42% fewer leads. So the videos that YouTube is rewarding aren't the ones that are building the business." (video 06)

Their **north-star metric is email signups**, then affiliate link clicks, not views. "We're now not evaluating which content to make based on which ones have the most amount of views. We've now got the data to show us which ones are actually lining up with our goals and our business." (video 11)

**Application to Selr AI:** judge a talking-head video on whether it drives the real business goal (leads, DM keyword triggers, scoping-call bookings), not on view count. Track downstream impact, not algorithm metrics.

## Caption baselines

From video 08, "Best AI Caption Generator For Video". "Captions are great for attention spans, accessibility, and SEO."

- **Workflow:** auto-generate captions, then correct transcription mistakes, then style fonts/colors/sizes/position to the brand.
- **Tools Justin names:** Descript (edit video and subtitles together, high accuracy, real-time sync), Adobe Premiere Pro (advanced captioning if already in that suite), CapCut (mobile-first, stylised animated captions), YouTube Studio (free, built-in, limited styling), Rev (human or AI captions, compliance standards, translation), Captions/CapCut for shorts.
- **Consolidate tools:** "consider merging your workflow so that you can get more bang for your valuable buck and have one app or subscription do several things for you." Check what you already own before buying another caption tool.

## Recording and gear baselines

From video 10, "Best Camera Settings for VIDEO". Settings are the same on any make of camera; only button placement differs.

- **Resolution:** shoot **4K** for highest quality and so footage holds up when reframed to vertical shorts/reels. **1080p** (full HD) is the lighter fallback for weaker editing machines.
- **Frame rate:** **24fps** for a cinematic/film look; **30fps** for a richer look (what most YouTube, sports, and daytime TV use). Pick one and lock it.
- **The exposure triangle**, aperture, shutter speed, ISO:
  - **Shutter speed** = roughly 2x the frame rate. 24fps -> 1/50s (closest to 1/48). 30fps -> 1/60s. Set once and lock.
  - **Aperture / f-stop** = as low as the lens allows for a blurred background and more light in. Kit lenses start around f3.5; a blurred background needs f2.8 or lower, which usually means a different lens.
  - **ISO** = as low as possible to avoid "digital noise, grain, or anything that you don't want." Pro tip: hold a hand up and set ISO so hand detail is visible without overexposure.
- **White balance:** use a grey card to sample room light, or match the Kelvin of your lights (around 4500-5000K) then adjust by eye. "This definitely impacts your skin tone way more than most people think."
- **Focus:** modern autofocus with face/eye detection "is 100% reliable for the most part" especially on cameras from roughly 2019 onward. Use manual focus only if a camera's autofocus drifts; with manual, use focus peeking to confirm.
- **Audio:** shotgun mic on the camera if within an arm's length of the talent; lapel/wireless mic if further away. "Just make sure that your audio sounds good."
- **Ship-it rule:** "if you do make some mistakes, publish your video anyway, and then in the very next video make small improvements at every iteration."

## Channel-growth fundamentals (context, video 09)

From video 09, "0 to 1,000 Subscribers: 9 Tips" (1M views). Less about editing, more about why the video exists. Worth knowing when planning Selr AI content.

- **Research before you create.** "Doing this topic or keyword research is going to allow you to know that there is demand and know that people actually want the content that you're going to create before you create it."
- **Hook fast, no dragged-out intro.** "Don't drag out your video intro, make it short, sharp, punchy, and let people know they're in the right place."
- **Thumbnail strategy.** Bright colors, big bold text, a face. "You could have the world's best video, if no one clicks it, no one's going to see it."
- **Think of the viewer journey / next best video.** Plan what the viewer watches next so you keep them on-platform.
- **Build an email list**, the direct line of communication "without any algorithm or any notification system stopping that communication." This is the same north-star logic as video 11.
- **Implement, learn, tweak, evolve, repeat.** A growth-mindset 1% improvement per video compounds.
