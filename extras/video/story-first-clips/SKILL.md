# Story-first clips

Find the story before styling the video. Reason from the complete source and the viewer's needs. An automated clip score is discovery metadata, never editorial proof.

## Establish the brief

Preserve the user's latest choices about audience, model, duration, framing and delivery phase. Read the current business model, positioning and brand sources named by the workspace; do not substitute remembered prices, promises or offer details. For owner-education content, examine whether a story gives a concrete gain in time, control, ownership or confidence. Prefer useful explanations, mistakes, examples and changes of perspective over unsupported excitement or tool lists. Do not force every clip to mention these themes.

A request for clips first means clean source footage and speech only. Keep drawing, caption and motion ideas in the sidecar. Do not burn text, add music or sound effects, or reuse an old branded export as the source unless requested.

## Account for the complete source pool

1. Inventory originals, city/event evidence, duration, stable media ID, source hash where available, local residency and archive location. Same filename or similar duration is not proof of duplication.
2. Map full-source transcripts to original identities. Preserve word timestamps and missing intervals. A processed project, partial proxy or existing shortlist does not prove complete coverage.
3. Record separate transcript, visual and listening coverage. Mark every interval reviewed, rejected, candidate or unresolved, with reasons. Do not call a source reviewed because another source covers a similar topic.
4. Check resident bytes before opening large originals. A dataless placeholder is not a usable file. Preserve originals; recover only through the authorised media route, using a bounded scratch cache.

Read whole arguments, including surrounding questions and qualifications. Use transcription to locate stories, then inspect the actual footage. If source speech cannot be checked, record the limitation rather than inventing words or claiming to have listened.

## Select complete stories

For each candidate identify the viewer question, tension, explanation or mechanism, concrete example and payoff. Not every story requires all five as separate sections, but the clip must make sense to someone absent from the room.

Prefer approximately 45–75 seconds when the brief asks for a minute. A complete 30-second story beats padding; a valuable longer story should be held or presented for review, not truncated to fit. Duration is subordinate to the user's explicit brief and truthful meaning.

Reject or hold incomplete answers, unexplained pronouns, room logistics without viewer value, private screen material, repetitive takes and claims whose essential qualification would be lost. Distinguish historical workshop descriptions, personal experience and current product promises. Preserve uncertainty, negation, chronology and attribution. Do not fix contradictory numbers by silently choosing one.

Group overlapping candidates by their actual lesson. Keep the clearest complete version, or explain what genuinely differs. Provide editorial reasons rather than invented virality scores. Parent/editor owns final choices; evidence scouts may propose candidates.

## Build a truthful spoken cold open

When requested, choose a compelling 3–5-second passage from the same story. It can originate midway or at the end. Record its verbatim text, exact source range, original context and why moving it preserves meaning.

A strong opening raises a useful question, challenges an assumption or states a concrete consequence. It must not turn a quoted objection into the speaker's belief, remove a condition, present an answer to a different question, or invent a before/after relationship. Use an authored text hook only when separately requested.

Map the output sequence explicitly: cold open, necessary setup, explanation, payoff. Usually remove the moved passage from its old position when continuity survives; if repeating it is useful, record the reason. A cold open is not permission to splice unrelated remarks into a new claim.

## Make precise clean cuts

Remove clear ums/ers, abandoned starts, repeated fragments and unnecessary pauses only after inspecting their role. Keep meaningful hesitations, emphasis, breaths and the words needed for grammar. ASR filler flags are suggestions, not a deletion list.

Record word-level source ranges and short audio fades at joins. ASR word times are proposed alignment, not proof of a phoneme-safe boundary; distinguish draft in/out points from acoustically checked joins. If listening is unavailable, keep the export explicitly marked as a review draft with that check pending. Keep natural speech speed unless the user asks otherwise. Tight pacing comes from selection and pauses, not rushed delivery or clipped consonants. Do not stitch separate statements to change the apparent answer.

Start with the user's approved source-specific crop. Check movement throughout each new range, retaining comfortable headroom and gestures. An old crop or person mask is not proof that a new interval is framed or private. Bind privacy masks and review evidence to the actual source ranges; do not copy time-local masks onto unrelated footage.

For separately requested branded repurposing, keep the source-backed selection intact before adding copy, graphics or derivative formats. A clean-clip request remains clean footage; do not expand its scope.

## Verify and deliver

Run one real representative conform before a batch. Verify source/output mapping, duration, decodability, visible framing and cut boundaries. Measure audio, but do not call loudness measurements perceptual listening. If listening is unavailable, disclose that narrow gap.

Preserve prior exports. Deliver versioned clean videos with a review index grouped by location and lesson, plus selection and coverage records. Separate selected, exported, structurally checked, visually reviewed and approved status. Report unreviewed sources and failed retrievals explicitly. Do not publish or message others without the relevant authority.

Use [the evidence contract](references/evidence-contract.md) for manifests and review checks.
