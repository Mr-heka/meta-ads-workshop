
> **Claims rules.** These override every framework below. Shipped copy carries
> no outcome guarantees, no refund or money-back language, no support or
> helpdesk promises, and no fabricated statistics or testimonials. Wherever a
> framework says "risk reversal", "guarantee", "money-back" or "free trial",
> substitute a **process** statement: something true about how the work runs,
> never a promise about the buyer's result.
>
> | ❌ Never write | ✅ Write instead |
> |---|---|
> | Money-back guarantee · 30-day refund | *(nothing — omit the section)* |
> | Guaranteed results · You will get X | "The audit and roadmap land in the first two weeks." |
> | Risk-free · Zero risk | "A plan you own either way." |
> | Ongoing support · weekly Q&A · helpdesk | "Everything is handed over and it's yours." |

## Above the fold

**Headline**: the single most important message. Communicate the core value proposition. Specific beats generic.

Starter formulas (full bank in `copy-frameworks.md`):
- "{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}"
- "The {category} for {audience}"
- "Never {unpleasant event} again"
- "{Question highlighting the main pain point}"

**Subheadline**: expands the headline, adds specificity, 1 to 2 sentences maximum.

**Primary CTA**: action-oriented button text that says what they get. "Start Free Trial" beats "Sign Up".

## Core section map

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Social Proof | Build credibility (logos, stats, testimonials) |
| Problem/Pain | Show you understand their situation |
| Solution/Benefits | Connect to outcomes (3 to 5 key benefits) |
| How It Works | Reduce perceived complexity (3 to 4 steps) |
| Objection Handling | FAQ, comparisons, fit/not-fit |
| Final CTA | Recap value, repeat the CTA, reduce friction |

Detailed section types and full page templates live in `copy-frameworks.md`.

## CTA copy

**Weak (avoid):** Submit · Sign Up · Learn More · Click Here · Get Started

**Strong (use):** Start Free Trial · Get [Specific Thing] · See [Product] in Action · Create Your First [Thing] · Download the Guide

**Formula:** [Action verb] + [what they get] + [qualifier if needed]

- "Start My Free Trial"
- "Get the Complete Checklist"
- "See Pricing for My Team"

For Selr AI pages, friction reducers under the CTA are price, speed and social proof. Never a guarantee or refund.

## Page-specific guidance

**Homepage**: serve multiple audiences without going generic. Lead with the broadest value proposition, then give clear paths for different visitor intents.

**Landing page**: single message, single CTA. Match the headline to the ad or traffic source that sent them. Complete the argument on one page.

**Pricing page**: help visitors choose. Address "which is right for me?" anxiety directly and make the recommended plan obvious.

**Feature page**: connect feature to benefit to outcome. Show use cases and examples, then a clear path to try or buy.

**About page**: tell the story of why you exist, connect the mission to a customer benefit, and still include a CTA.

## Voice and tone

Establish two settings before writing:

**Formality:** casual and conversational · professional but friendly · formal and enterprise.

**Personality:** playful or serious · bold or understated · technical or accessible.

Hold the setting consistent, vary the intensity: headlines can be bolder, body copy should be clearer, CTAs stay action-oriented.

---

## Best-practice moves

**Be direct.** Get to the point; do not bury the value in qualifications.

- Weak: "Slack lets you share files instantly, from documents to images, directly in your conversations."
- Strong: "Need to share a screenshot? Send as many documents, images and audio files as your heart desires."

**Use rhetorical questions.** They make the reader think about their own situation: "Hate returning stuff to Amazon?" · "Tired of chasing approvals?"

**Use analogies** when an abstract concept needs to become concrete and memorable.

**Pepper in humour** only where it fits the brand and does not cost clarity.
