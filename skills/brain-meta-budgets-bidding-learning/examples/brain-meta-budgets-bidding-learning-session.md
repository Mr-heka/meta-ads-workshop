# Example session: one worked Q&A grounded in this brain

Demonstrates how an answer should look when this brain is loaded: grounded in the

## User question

> My <city> workshop campaign was getting $8 CPLs at $60/day. I raised it to
> $75/day yesterday and today the CPL is $13. Do I cut the budget back, or is this
> the point where I add a cost cap to force efficiency?

## Brain-grounded answer

**Neither yet. Hold 4 days at $75/day, then step down 20% only if the miss holds.**

1. **A cost cap is off the table at this spend.** $75/day is roughly $2,250/month,
   nowhere near any cap gate in the source set. Build Your Ecomm puts the loosest
   gate at "If you're spending at least $30,000 a month or you have at least 90 days of stable data, that is when you should start using cost caps."
   The wider verified evidence (`brain-meta-ads-manual-control-no-advantage`, in the optional extras pack of this kit) is stricter
   still. Caps here are diagnosis material, not practice.

2. **One day of data is not a verdict.** The playbook rule: a campaign that missed
   its KPI gets 4+ days at the current budget before anything is touched. Nick
   Theriot's check is "have we waited at least four days for that scaling KPI to be hit".
   Read CPL on lifetime or 7-day view, never today-only; yesterday's raise also
   means the ad sets are re-settling.

3. **Note the jump size for next time.** $60 to $75 is a 25% raise in one day. The
   consensus cadence is Rafael Hernandez's: "Go up 20% every 3 to 5 days because if you scale too fast, you'll re-enter the learning phase."
   Next raise: max +20%, then 3 days hands-off.

4. **If CPL is still over $10 after 4 full days, cut 20%.** That lands back at
   $60/day. The hard deck for a city campaign is $50/day; never below it, so
   testing volume survives the slump (Theriot's Hard Deck SOP).

5. **Do not stack other edits while you wait.** New ads, audience tweaks or a
   second budget touch all reset learning. Batch everything into the one weekly
   change round the playbook prescribes.
