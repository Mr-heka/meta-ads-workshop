# Copywriting⁠​‌​‌​​‌‌​‌​​​‌​‌​‌​​‌‌​​​‌​‌​​‌​​​‌‌​​​‌⁠

Page copy that a skeptical buyer reads without stalling. Clear beats clever, specific beats vague, and every section advances one argument.

## Context first

If `.agents/product-marketing-context.md` exists (or `.claude/product-marketing-context.md`), read it before asking anything. Ask only for what it does not cover:

- Page type, and the ONE action a visitor should take.
- Who the buyer is, the problem in their words, and their top objection.
- What is being sold, what makes it different, the transformation.
- Proof points: numbers, testimonials, case studies.
- Traffic source, and what the visitor already knows on arrival.

## Principles

1. **Simple over complex.** "Use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate".
2. **Benefits over features.** Features say what it does; benefits say what that means for them.
3. **Specific over vague.** "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes", not "save time".
4. **Customer language over company language.** Mirror reviews, interviews, support tickets.
5. **Active and confident.** "We generate reports", not "reports are generated". Cut "almost", "very", "really".
6. **Show over tell.** Describe the outcome instead of reaching for an adverb.
7. **One idea per section.** Build a logical flow down the page.

## Output contract

Deliver every page in this shape:

1. **Page copy, by section**: headline, subheadline, primary CTA, then each section's header and body, plus secondary CTAs.
2. **Annotations**: for key elements, the choice made and the principle behind it.
3. **Alternatives**: 2 to 3 options for every headline and CTA, each with its rationale.
4. **Meta content**: page title and meta description, when the page is indexed.

## Quality check before handing over

- Jargon an outsider would stumble on.
- Sentences doing two jobs.
- Passive constructions.
- Exclamation points (remove them).
- Buzzwords with no substance behind them.
- Any claim without a real source. Mark it TBC rather than inventing one.

## Compliance (REQUIRED)

Re-read the Claims rules at the top before handing over: no outcome guarantees, no refund or money-back language, no support or helpdesk promises, no fabricated statistics or testimonials, and no em dashes.
