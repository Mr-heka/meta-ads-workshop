## What the experts agree on

- **Ad review is AI-first and deliberately over-rejects.** The first pass scans image, video, text, the landing page, and account history, and would rather over-reject than let a bad ad through. Manual review is a separate step you must request and it often overturns the machine. (Christian Jamal, Godbless Iboyi, Cabilly & Co.; Cabilly cites Meta's own 10-20% enforcement error rate.)
- **Personal-attribute copy is the number-one avoidable rejection.** Never assert or imply the viewer's race, health, money, age, or employment. Reframe as third-party results. "Are you struggling with debt?" fails; "Thousands of homeowners have reduced their monthly payments by 40%" passes. (Christian Jamal, EduSphere, Optimum Click, Godbless Iboyi.)
- **Content splits into prohibited, restricted, and special-category.** Prohibited is never allowed; restricted is allowed with conditions; special categories force a category selection and strip age/gender/location/lookalike targeting. There are exactly four: **Credit/Financial Products & Services, Employment, Housing, and Social Issues/Elections/Politics**. Health is NOT a special category (health ads are governed by the restricted-content rules instead), and Social Issues is the one that catches coaches and consultants off guard. (EduSphere, Christian Jamal, Shout Marketing.)
- **The landing page is part of the ad review.** Meta crawls the destination. Mismatch, missing privacy policy, or links out to unrelated treatments all trigger disapproval. (Christian Jamal, Godbless Iboyi, Sagapixel, Optimum Click.)
- **Account history compounds like a credit score.** Repeated rejections make the AI stricter on everything next, which is why resubmitting the same rejected ad is the worst move and can escalate to a full disablement. (Christian Jamal, Mind Mentor, Godbless Iboyi, EduSphere.)
- **Diagnose the restriction level before appealing.** Asset, ad account, portfolio, and personal profile are four different severities with four different fixes; personal-profile bans are the hardest to reverse. (Christian Jamal, Cabilly & Co., STEFAN VAN DER MERWE.)
- **The appeal ladder is self-appeal, then human, then legal.** Everyone describes the same escalation; the appeal copy must be factual, accountable, and framed around business disruption, never "I did nothing wrong". (Ben Heath, Cabilly & Co., E & T Academy, STEFAN VAN DER MERWE.)
- **Avoid recovery scams; a genuine permanent ban means rebuild calmly.** Paid recovery services, bought "seasoned" accounts, and auto-submit extensions are traps that can restrict your other assets. Stay calm and compliant. (Ben Heath, Christian Jamal, Cabilly & Co.)

## Named frameworks & methods

- **Business Support Home four-state triage** (Christian Jamal): reach it via Ads Manager > All tools > Business support home, or Google "business support home meta". It shows page restricted, account restricted, ad account restricted, or asset restricted. Asset restricted = low severity (some ads in appeal). Account restricted at the personal level = "90% cooked" (~10% recovery odds).
- **The "credit score" reputation model** (Christian Jamal): every rejection makes the AI stricter on the next submission. Rebuild with a stretch of clean, simple, compliant ads before pushing boundaries.
- **Five-reason rejection diagnostic** (Christian Jamal): (1) personal-attribute copy, (2) landing-page mismatch, (3) restricted category needing special-category selection, (4) visual triggers (before/after, skin, text-heavy), (5) poor account history. Response: read the reason, check against the five, request manual review if compliant, adjust and resubmit only if manual review also fails.
- **The isolation method** (Godbless Iboyi): test one variable at a time across three sequential ads. Ad 1 = copy alone pointed at a safe link (google.com) with a neutral image. Ad 2 = approved copy + safe link + real creative. Ad 3 = approved copy + creative + real landing-page URL. Pinpoints exactly which element triggered the rejection.
- **The three-tier recovery ladder** (Cabilly & Co.): self-appeal (free, mostly AI), partner-agency appeal (different queue, faster, often human), legal demand letter (high financial harm only). Escalate if no response in 10 business days.
- **Ben Heath's appeal-message rule**: if disabled incorrectly, state you do not know why and have not violated anything. If you did breach a rule, admit it, say you have reviewed the advertiser policies, understand the mistake, and will not repeat it.
- **Meta Verified enhanced-support workaround** (STEFAN VAN DER MERWE): when the normal appeal flow is dead (it lives behind the disabled login), buy Meta Verified on Instagram (a separate login surface), choose the Plus tier for faster resolution, and open a support case with a live agent.
- **The "99 problem" quarantine** (Christian Jamal) and **category isolation** (Mind Mentor): keep risky categories on a separate portfolio, ad-account ID, and payment method; move at-risk assets into an already-restricted portfolio so a ban never touches the clean one.
- **Default numbers experts actually use:**
  - Enforcement error rate: 10-20% (1-2 in 10), Cabilly & Co.
  - Personal-account-ban recovery odds: ~10%, Christian Jamal.
  - Appeal turnaround: Meta says ~4 days (E & T Academy); Cabilly says escalate after 10 business days; STEFAN recovered in ~72 hours via Meta Verified.
  - ID upload image size: ~1,500 x 1,000 pixels (E & T Academy).
  - Warm-up: 1-2 days minimum on a new account/page; profile aged 3+ months; wait 1-2 weeks before the real conversion ad (Kapil Rahangdale, Godbless Iboyi).
  - One payment card is shared across ~5 ad accounts (Mind Mentor).
  - HIPAA fines (US-only): $100-$50,000 per user (Optimum Click).

## Contrarian / disputed takes

- **HIPAA pixel ban (contested scope, US-only).** Optimum Click says the Meta Pixel is "basically off limits. Full stop" for US healthcare advertisers because condition-linked pixel data becomes Protected Health Information and Meta will not sign a Business Associate Agreement. This is a US legal constraint, not a Meta platform rule and not an AU obligation. The transferable instinct (keep signals generic, do not send condition data) is sound; the "no pixel at all" verdict does not bind AU advertisers.
- **Meta Verified as a recovery route (single source, novel).** STEFAN VAN DER MERWE is the only source proposing paid Meta Verified on Instagram to unlock a human agent. Nobody disputes it, but nobody else corroborates it either. Treat as a promising last-resort tactic, not proven consensus.
- **Seasonal Q4 scrutiny spike (single source).** Kapil Rahangdale argues Q4 big-brand spend pressure raises rejection/disablement rates platform-wide. Plausible and unopposed, but uncorroborated.
- **Before/after images: banned vs "subtlety is the workaround".** Sagapixel and Optimum Click say avoid before/afters entirely. EduSphere calls them "technically a no-no" but says subtlety is the key workaround. Side with the stricter reading: avoid them, our brand does not need them.
- **"Sue Meta" as a real option.** Ben Heath notes businesses sue Meta "all the time" but calls it expensive with no guarantees. Cabilly & Co. reserves legal for high financial harm only. Realistic path for a small advertiser is the structured appeal or a rebuild, not litigation.

## Execution playbook

**IF an ad is rejected THEN read the exact reason first, check it against Christian Jamal's five reasons, and if genuinely compliant request manual review WITHOUT editing the ad** (editing restarts review and can look evasive). Only adjust and resubmit if manual review also rejects. (Christian Jamal.)

**IF you cannot tell which element caused the rejection THEN run Godbless Iboyi's isolation method**: copy alone to a safe link, then add creative, then add the real URL, one variable per submission.

**IF copy calls out the viewer ("are you struggling with...", "tired of your 9-5", "lose 20 lb") THEN rewrite as a third-party result before submitting** ("thousands of owners have...", "how this helped over 500 people..."). (Christian Jamal, EduSphere.)

**IF the offer is credit/financial, employment, housing/property, or social issues, elections and politics THEN select the correct special ad category in setup and expect an account-level verification prompt; running without the category selected gets the campaign rejected even after verifying.** Social Issues is the trap for coaches and consultants: commentary on jobs, immigration, the economy, health policy or the environment can pull an ordinary offer into the category. Health offers are NOT a special category, but they sit under the restricted-content rules, so treat the claims with the same care. (Shout Marketing, Christian Jamal, EduSphere.)

**IF an account/page/portfolio is restricted THEN open Business Support Home and identify the level (asset / ad account / portfolio / personal) before doing anything** (Ads Manager > All tools > Business support home). Asset-level = wait out the appeal. Personal-level = worst case, ~10% odds. (Christian Jamal.)

**IF you need to appeal THEN write it factual, accountable, and business-framed** (attach evidence, cite the exact account URL/ID and email, reference business disruption); never write "I did nothing wrong". Use the built-in "request a review" first, then the Meta AI assistant pushed to a human, then a partner-agency queue, then legal for high harm only. Escalate if no reply in 10 business days. (Cabilly & Co., Ben Heath, STEFAN VAN DER MERWE.)

**IF an account is permanently disabled THEN do not buy a recovery service or a seasoned account; stay calm and rebuild** on a fresh device with a different payment card, accepting lost learning history. (Ben Heath, Christian Jamal.)

**IF spinning up a new account THEN warm it up**: 1-2 days of low-stakes engagement activity, profile aged 3+ months, verified business and domain, before running conversion ads. (Kapil Rahangdale, Godbless Iboyi.)

### Pre-flight checklist before submitting any ad
1. No personal-attribute callout in the copy (viewer's health, money, age, employment, race).
2. No outcome guarantee or exaggerated claim ("guaranteed", "in 7 days", "make X").
3. No before/after images; no excess skin; keep on-image text light.
4. Landing page matches the ad promise, has a privacy policy and contact info, no broken links, no redirects, no links out to unrelated offers.
5. If the offer is credit/financial, employment, housing, or social issues/elections/politics, the correct special ad category is selected (health is not a special category, but check it against the restricted-content rules).
6. Business and domain are verified; the account is warmed up if new.
7. You are NOT resubmitting an already-rejected ad unchanged.

### Top 5 failure modes and the fix
1. **Resubmitting the same rejected ad repeatedly.** Escalates from rejection to permanent disablement. Fix: change the ad or request manual review, never spam-resubmit. (Mind Mentor, Christian Jamal.)
2. **Calling out the viewer in copy.** The most common avoidable rejection. Fix: reframe as third-party results. (Christian Jamal, EduSphere.)
3. **Landing page mismatch or missing privacy policy.** Silent rejection cause because people forget Meta crawls the page. Fix: match the headline, add privacy/contact/disclaimer, remove links out. (Godbless Iboyi, Sagapixel.)
4. **Panicking and buying a recovery service after a ban.** Wastes money and can restrict your remaining assets. Fix: stay calm, run the appeal ladder, or rebuild. (Ben Heath, Christian Jamal.)
5. **Skipping verification / running a brand-new account hot.** New unverified accounts get restricted or throttled fast. Fix: verify business + domain, warm up 1-2 days, then scale. (Ben Heath, Kapil Rahangdale, E & T Academy.)

**Consensus translated into execution moves:**
1. **Single-account discipline is the biggest exposure for most small advertisers.** One account and no backup pool means the prevention checklist is not optional. Complete Meta business verification and domain verification for every domain you send traffic to (`<your landing-page domains>`), and never resubmit a rejected ad unchanged. Ben Heath's "verification is the first line of defence" is doubly true when there is no fallback account.
2. **Run every hero line through the pre-flight copy check.** Write the outcome, never the viewer. A line like "`<your customer's before-state>` becomes `<your customer's after-state>`" is safe because it names the result; "are you drowning in `<pain point>`?" calls out the reader and is a rejection risk. If your own brand rules already ban outcome guarantees and refund language, this brain simply adds the personal-attribute rule on top.
3. **Landing-page hygiene as policy, not just conversion.** Every landing page must carry a privacy policy, your own support contact address, and a not-Facebook-affiliated disclaimer, and must not link out to unrelated offers mid-page. Sagapixel's "no links out" rule bites hardest on multi-offer sites: a page selling `<your flagship offer>` should not link to a different offer above the fold in a way that reads as a bait-and-switch.
4. **Have the appeal kit ready before you ever need it.** Store the account ID, Page ID, pixel ID, business email, and a screenshot procedure so that if `<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>` is restricted you can file a factual, business-framed appeal within the hour instead of scrambling. Keep the escalation ladder (request review, Meta AI to human, partner agency, legal-only-for-high-harm) documented.
5. **Treat any disablement as calm-and-rebuild, never scam-and-panic.** If the account is permanently gone, rebuild on the same clean business identity, do not buy a "seasoned" account or a recovery service, and accept the learning-history reset. Rebuild-versus-wait is a money-and-continuity decision, so it is the business owner's call, but the default posture is set.

**What does NOT apply to a single-account owner-operator, and why:**
- **Multi-account / anti-detect-browser / IP-separation tactics** (Kapil, Mind Mentor): built for agencies running a stable of client accounts. On one owned account they are irrelevant and would themselves look like evasion.
- **The "sacrificial portfolio" quarantine** (Christian Jamal, Mind Mentor): useful only if you run high-risk categories (trading, betting, online-earning). If your offer is not in one, keep everything on a single clean identity.
- **HIPAA pixel ban** (Optimum Click): US health law. It binds US healthcare advertisers only; check your own jurisdiction rather than importing it.
- **SEBI / India-specific verification UI** (Shout Marketing): generalise, do not copy. A finance-adjacent angle will surface your own country's special-category prompt, not the SEBI field.
- **"Sue Meta"** (Ben Heath): not a realistic path at small-business spend; the appeal ladder or a rebuild is the answer.
