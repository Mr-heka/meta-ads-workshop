# Worked session: "My ad got disapproved, what do I do?"

A full end-to-end walkthrough of the most common query this brain answers. Shows how to move from a raw rejection to a diagnosis, a fix, and (if needed) an appeal, grounded in the frameworks in `SKILL.md` and applied to a single owned ad account (`<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>`).

Use this as a template. The user rarely arrives with a clean diagnosis; they arrive with "Meta rejected my ad" and a screenshot. Your job is to run the diagnostic in order, not to guess.

---

## The query

> "Meta just disapproved my workshop ad. The reason says 'Personal attributes'. What do I do?"

## Step 1: Read the exact reason first, do not touch the ad

The stated reason is the whole ballgame. Here it is "Personal attributes", which maps straight to Christian Jamal's reason (1) of five: personal-attribute copy.

Do NOT edit and resubmit yet. Editing restarts the review clock and, on a rejected ad, can read as evasive. First confirm the diagnosis against the five reasons:

1. Personal-attribute copy (asserts or implies the viewer's health, money, age, employment, race).
2. Landing-page mismatch.
3. Restricted category needing a special-category selection.
4. Visual triggers (before/after, skin, text-heavy image).
5. Poor account history.

The reason string points at (1). Confirm by reading the actual copy.

## Step 2: Inspect the copy against the personal-attribute rule

The user's headline: **"Still drowning in admin? Get your evenings back."**

That is a textbook personal-attribute callout. "Still drowning in admin?" asserts something about the viewer's own situation. Christian Jamal: "Meta does not allow ads that assert or imply things about a person's attributes." EduSphere: "The key is to sell the outcome and not focus on the problem."

## Step 3: Rewrite as a third-party result

Reframe from "you have this problem" to "here is the outcome others got". This is the single highest-leverage prevention move in the brain.

- Before: "Still drowning in admin? Get your evenings back."
- After: **"8 hours a week chasing leads, all followed up overnight instead. See how the workshop does it."**

The rewrite names the outcome, not the viewer. It also stays inside our own campaign rules: no outcome guarantee, no "in 7 days" claim, no refund or support language. Keep it a described result, never a promise.

Run the pre-flight checklist on the rewrite before resubmitting:
1. No personal-attribute callout. Pass.
2. No outcome guarantee or exaggerated claim. Pass.
3. No before/after image, no excess skin, light on-image text. Check the creative.
4. Landing page (<your-landing-page-domain>) matches the promise, has a privacy policy and contact info, no links out to other offers above the fold. Check.
5. Not a special ad category (AI education, not finance/property/employment/credit/health). Pass.
6. Business and domain verified. Confirm.
7. Not resubmitting the old ad unchanged. Pass, this is a real rewrite.

Now resubmit the corrected ad as a fresh submission.

## Step 4 (branch): if it is NOT obvious which element caused the rejection

Suppose the reason string had been generic ("does not comply with our advertising policies") and the copy looked clean. Then run Godbless Iboyi's isolation method, one variable per submission:

- Ad 1: the copy alone, pointed at a safe link (google.com), with a neutral image. Isolates whether the text is the trigger.
- Ad 2: approved copy plus the safe link plus the real creative. Isolates the image/video.
- Ad 3: approved copy plus creative plus the real landing-page URL (<your-landing-page-domain>). Isolates the destination.

Whichever step first gets rejected is your culprit. This beats guessing and beats spam-resubmitting, which hurts account reputation (the "credit score" model).

## Step 5 (branch): if the fixed ad is still rejected and you are genuinely compliant

Request manual review. A human overturns the machine in many cases; Cabilly & Co. cite Meta's own admission that one to two in ten enforcement actions are mistakes. Request the review WITHOUT further editing. Only adjust and resubmit if manual review also rejects.

## Step 6 (branch): if the rejection escalated to an account or portfolio restriction

If instead of an ad rejection you see the account, page, or portfolio flagged, stop and diagnose the level before appealing. Open Business Support Home (Ads Manager > All tools > Business support home, or Google "business support home meta"). It shows one of four states:

- **Asset restricted**: low severity. Some ads are in appeal. Wait it out, no drastic action.
- **Ad account restricted**: the account, not you. Appeal it.
- **Portfolio restricted**: often just an identity-verification prompt on a newer portfolio. Verify and appeal.
- **Account restricted at the personal level**: worst case. Christian Jamal: "You're 90% cooked" (roughly 10% recovery odds).

On a single-account setup this matters doubly: one restriction on `<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>` pauses all spend at once. Have the appeal kit ready before you need it: account ID, Page ID, pixel ID (`<YOUR_PIXEL_ID>`), business email, and a screenshot procedure.

## Step 7 (branch): writing the appeal

If you need to appeal, write it factual, accountable, and business-framed. Never "I did nothing wrong".

- If disabled incorrectly: state you do not know the reason and have not violated anything (Ben Heath).
- If you did breach something: admit it, say you have reviewed the advertiser policies, understand the mistake, and will not repeat it (Ben Heath).
- Attach evidence and reference business disruption. STEFAN VAN DER MERWE: "referencing that this is obstructing business operations" moved the needle, alongside the exact account URL, account ID, and linked email.

Escalation ladder, in order:
1. Built-in "request a review" (mostly AI).
2. Meta AI business assistant pushed to a live human ("I've tried those, what can I do next?").
3. Partner-agency queue (different queue, faster, often human) or, when the normal flow is dead because it lives behind the disabled login, Meta Verified on Instagram to reach enhanced support (STEFAN VAN DER MERWE).
4. Legal, for high financial harm only.

Escalate if there is no response in 10 business days (Cabilly & Co.). Meta's own message quotes about 4 days (E & T Academy).

## Step 8 (branch): if the account is permanently disabled

Do not buy a recovery service or a "seasoned" account; those are the traps the whole brain warns against, and they can restrict your remaining assets. Stay calm and rebuild on a fresh device with a different payment card, accepting the lost learning history. Christian Jamal: "Meta rewards calm, compliant, and predictable behavior... panicking will make it permanent." This is a business-continuity decision, so the rebuild-vs-wait call is the owner's call, but the default posture is calm rebuild, never scam-and-panic.

---

## The one-line version

Read the exact reason, check it against the five, rewrite personal-attribute copy as a third-party result, resubmit clean once, request manual review if still wrong, and only escalate to an appeal (factual, accountable, business-framed) if the restriction is at the account level or above. Never resubmit the same rejected ad unchanged, and never buy a recovery service.
