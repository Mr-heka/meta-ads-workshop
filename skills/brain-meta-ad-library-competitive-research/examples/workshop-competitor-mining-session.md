# Worked example: workshop city campaign competitor mining session

A real-shaped session showing this brain loaded and answering end-to-end. Every expert
quote below is copied verbatim from `references/quote-library.md`; paraphrased claims name
the expert. Business facts come from the "Applied to your business" section of SKILL.md — swap in your own before reusing any of it.

**Scenario.** An education business is preparing a new city campaign for an in-person workshop
on ad account `<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>` (lead-gen funnel, CRM wired).
Before briefing fresh creative, a Meta Ad Library research pass turns up an AU business-education
advertiser whose ad has been active for months. The session works through: is it a winner,
how to find the rest of the field, what to extract, and what to do with it.

**Brain loaded:** consensus points 1-7, Ben Heath impressions-plus-longevity, Nick Theriot
adaptation levers, the execution playbook IF/THEN rules, and the "Applied to your business" map.

---

**User:** I found an AU advertiser selling AI training to business owners. One of their ads has
been running for about six months. That's a winner, right? Should we model it for the workshop
campaign?

**Claude:** Six months puts it past every longevity threshold in the brain. Ads And Leads sets the
floor at 90 days:

> "If it's been live for 90 days or 180 days, even it is for a reason, because it's profitable." - Ads And Leads, *Your Competitors Already Tested These Ads | Steal Their Data*

But longevity is a filter, not proof. Ben Heath's correction is the one our verified manual-control
base agrees with: Meta parks low-spend ads as "active" for months, so run-length alone flags duds
as winners. His fix is free, sort the library by impressions high to low, then keep only ads that are
long-running AND high-impression:

> "the combination of those two metrics, lots of impressions plus um been running for a long time is as close to a guarantee that it's a high performer for that advertiser" - Ben Heath, *How To Spy On Your Competitors Meta Ads for FREE!*

Before calling it a winner, stack corroborating signals. Nick Theriot's duplicate-count tell:

> "if it's been duplicated over and over and over, there's a reason behind that" - Nick Theriot, *How To Spy On Your Competitors' Facebook Ads In 2026*

And Ads And Leads' dynamic-creative tell:

> "So if you see a dynamic ad running for 6 months or even more, you know you have found a super winner. This is a core pillar of their business." - Ads And Leads, *Your Competitors Already Tested These Ads | Steal Their Data*

So: run the impressions sort on their page, check for duplicates and the "multiple versions" label.
Two or three signals stacked, then we treat it as a candidate. One signal alone, we keep looking.

---

**User:** That's one advertiser. Searching "AI workshop" in the library gives me almost nothing.
How do I find the rest of the field?

**Claude:** That is the expected result, and the brain's rule for service and education businesses
covers it: search the audience and the problem, not our own offer name. Gaurav Singh demonstrates
it live, searching the buyer's category instead of the service:

> "5600 results. Ok? How many coaches have run ads related to this keyword and when" - Gaurav Singh, *How to Spy on Competitor's Facebook Ads 2026 (Step-by-Step) || Competitor Ad Research*

For us that means searching the pain and the buyer per the Applied section: "small business AI",
"automate admin", "AI for tradies / gyms / property", plus adjacent educators (business coaches,
done-for-you AI agencies, no-code course sellers).

Two pre-flight checks from the playbook before you read any results:

1. Category set to "All Ads", not the default Issues & Politics (Ads And Leads).
2. The location filter is about delivery, not the advertiser:

> "the location you set is not where you are, or even where the competitor is based that you're looking to take a look at their ads, it's where the ads are being shown" - Ben Heath, *How To Spy On Your Competitors Meta Ads for FREE!*

Use all-locations for discovery, AU for benchmarking.

And if a niche keyword turns up nobody advertising, that is not blue ocean. Learn with Bilal calls it
the opposite:

> "Now what does red flag mean ? That if you are trying to work in a category , want to start in which you have not already found any competitor, then it is called a red flag in marketing" - Learn with Bilal, *How To Do Competitor Analysis, Research for Any Your E-Commerce Business (EASY Strategy For 2026)*

No direct competitors on a given angle, study large adjacent brands and adapt (HubSpot Marketing's
workaround).

---

**User:** OK, the impressions sort confirms their top ad is long-running and high-impression.
Can we rebuild it with our own branding and run it?

**Claude:** No. That is the number one failure mode in the brain, and the sources kill it three ways.

Legal (Learn with Bilal):

> "You cannot download your comparator's ad creative exactly as it is and run it on your store. This cannot happen. This will earn you a copyright strike" - Learn with Bilal, *How To Do Competitor Analysis, Research for Any Your E-Commerce Business (EASY Strategy For 2026)*

Positioning (William Kast):

> "Now, this doesn't only not work, but it also advertises your competitor. Because if you sound exactly like them, why should they go and buy from you and not from the competitor" - William Kast, *Use THIS script framework for your next 5 winning ads*

Decay (Nick Theriot):

> "every time an ad is recreated, it devalues over and over and over" - Nick Theriot, *How To Spy On Your Competitors' Facebook Ads In 2026*

What we do instead: tear it down into the fixed field set from the playbook (format, hook, primary
text, headline, description, offer, CTA line, platforms, start date) and log it in the swipe sheet.
One field trap from Learn with Bilal while you do it:

> "People make this mistake here. They think that the button below which says Order Now or Shop Now is a CTA. However, this is not the case at all" - Learn with Bilal, *How To Do Competitor Analysis, Research for Any Your E-Commerce Business (EASY Strategy For 2026)*

Log the persuasive line, not the button label. Nick Theriot's benchmark: review 3-4 ads per brand
so you log repeating patterns, not a one-off.

Then differentiate with Theriot's levers (new persona or desire per niche, new mechanism, or a
bigger claim) or Kast's awareness stages. Kast's framing of what the research is actually for:

> "the competitor research, this is also a thing many people get wrong is it's not there to to copy what's working for them. It's there to understand what they are saying, what they are missing to say" - William Kast, *Use THIS script framework for your next 5 winning ads*

For the workshop campaign that means the same proven structure re-aimed at our niches
(trades, property, gyms, hospitality, services), saying the thing this competitor is not saying.

---

**User:** Their ad clicks through to a webinar registration page. Do we care about that too?

**Claude:** Yes, that may be the real winner. The strategic sources are unanimous that the ad is the
visible tip. BKA Content:

> "the offer is what hooks people to actually take action on your ads" - BKA Content, *How To Use The Facebook Ads Library (Full Tutorial)*

Ads And Leads' funnel-hack step: click the CTA, check ad-to-page headline congruency, identify the
offer type, then opt in with an email to see the follow-up sequence. Their swipe-file discipline:

> "You build a swipe file. You take screenshots of winning ads and you copy the links of a winning landing pages." - Ads And Leads, *Your Competitors Already Tested These Ads | Steal Their Data*

Nick Parker is the warning label on copying an offer without its invisible machinery:

> "I literally almost copied that thing verbatim... I did not sell a lot because I was missing so many other pieces of the puzzle. I was missing the back end infrastructure" - Nick Parker, *Why Your Competitor Looks More Successful Than They Really Are*

Map their guarantee and urgency structure as intelligence, then translate it inside our walls per the
Applied section: genuine expiring early-bird, application screening, past-cohort facts as history.
No outcome guarantees, no refund language, no support promises in anything we ship.

---

**User:** So when do we know if our adapted version actually works?

**Claude:** On our own account, and fast. Nick Parker's verification loop: test ONE variable at a time,
and the read comes quicker than agencies claim:

> "you can figure it out in like 48 to 72 hours if something's working. And so if you run something in test and you get like three, four, five thousand impressions on an ad and it is winning, you will know" - Nick Parker, *Why Your Competitor Looks More Successful Than They Really Are*

Decision metric is cost-per-result, never engagement (Nick Parker, and our verified base agrees).
For this campaign that means judging against the playbook targets: workshop CPL $6-10.

Handoff from here, per the brain's boundaries:

- Brief the extracted concept as one-concept-each DCT angles via `brain-meta-creative-strategist-manual`.
- Campaign structure, budgets and kill/scale rules belong to `brain-meta-ads-manual-control-no-advantage`.
- Running this research at scale across many competitors is the `ad-concept-miner` tool's job.

**Session output:** one confirmed competitor candidate (long-running, high-impression, funnel
mapped), a swipe-sheet row per the fixed field set, and a differentiation brief (same structure,
new niche persona, saying the missing thing), queued for a single-variable test on
the account.
