# Synthesis, Meta Ad Library competitive research

Built 2026-07-06 from 16 mined YouTube transcripts. Context lens applied throughout: AU workshop/course/community advertiser running manual Meta campaigns on one ad account. Nearly every source is e-commerce or dropshipping framed; the transferable unit for a service business is "best-selling offer/program" in place of "best-selling product", and "positioning gap" in place of "local supplier". Longevity claims are cross-checked against our verified manual-control base (`brain-meta-ads-manual-control-no-advantage`), which warns that Meta's delivery system now leaves low-spend ads active for months.

Exclusions live at the bottom. Two sources dropped from theme backing (one thin, one longevity-only era flag). All quotes trace to a named source.

---

## Theme 1: Longevity is the core winner signal (CONSENSUS, with a hard caveat)

The single most repeated idea: an ad that has been live a long time is probably profitable, because advertisers kill losing ads fast. This is the backbone of the whole discipline.

- **Ads And Leads (Peter)**: minimum threshold of 3 months (90 days). "If it's been live for 90 days or 180 days, even it is for a reason, because it's profitable." A dynamic-creative ad running 6+ months is a "super winner" and "core pillar of their business."
- **HubSpot Marketing**: "If an ad has been running for months, it's probably a hit... Brands don't waste money on bad ads."
- **Gaurav Singh**: counts run-length live on screen (5-6 months) as proof: "They might not be spending money on their ads just like that."
- **Learn with Bilal**: an ad live ~10 months read as proof it is profitable.

**The hard caveat (CONTESTED, and this is the important part):**
- **Ben Heath** breaks the naive rule. Meta's delivery system leaves 5-20 ads in one ad set with only 1-3 getting spend; the rest sit "active" for months with zero impressions, so run-length alone now falsely flags duds as winners. His fix: sort by impressions high to low, then combine with long run duration. "The combination of those two metrics, lots of impressions plus been running for a long time is as close to a guarantee that it's a high performer."

**The wider verified evidence agrees with Ben Heath, not the naive camp.** The manual-control synthesis states learning phase is conversion-volume not time, and Meta over-serves/under-serves inside ad sets. So: longevity is a starting filter, not proof. Impressions-plus-longevity is the trustworthy version.

## Theme 2: Never clone the creative; extract the structure, rebuild original (CONSENSUS)

Every credible source that talks strategy says the failure mode is copying the ad. Reasons differ: legal, and effectiveness.

- **Learn with Bilal** (legal): "You cannot download your comparator's ad creative exactly as it is... This will earn you a copyright strike." Extract hook, primary text, headline, description, offer, video length, CTA, then rebuild.
- **William Kast** (effectiveness): copying "doesn't only not work, but it also advertises your competitor. Because if you sound exactly like them, why should they go and buy from you and not from the competitor." The correct purpose of research is finding what they are NOT saying.
- **Nick Theriot** (decay): "every time an ad is recreated, it devalues over and over", cites a trending song losing views on reuse; literal copying yields high CPM and terrible cost per purchase.
- **Gaurav Singh**: take inspiration and add "your own chili masala," feed the primary text to ChatGPT for a better version.
- **Nick Parker** (the trap of copying without the back end): near-verbatim copied a VSL early on, "did not sell a lot because I was missing... the back end infrastructure" (follow-up, upsells, downsells).
- **MUSAB MULTIMEDIA STUDIO** (softer version): "copy their style and upgrade this", not direct copy.

Consensus is strong. The mild dissent is only in intensity: MUSAB says mimic-and-upgrade, while Kast and Theriot say the copy itself is the mistake and you must say the missing thing.

## Theme 3: Extract a fixed checklist per ad, not a vibe (CONSENSUS on the fields)

Sources converge on roughly the same teardown fields, which lets you build a repeatable swipe sheet.

- **Amir Rasheed** (most granular): format (single image/video/carousel), aspect ratio (1:1 vs vertical), headline, description, CTA, and creative angle type (needs-based, wants/aspirational, UGC-style, or CGI-style). Opens with Meta's own claim that 56% of campaign performance depends on creative.
- **Learn with Bilal**: hook (the angle used to present the product), primary text, headline, description, offer/discount, video length (cites Meta's 30-45 second guidance), and CTA framed as the persuasive line, not the button label. "People make this mistake here. They think that the button below which says Order Now or Shop Now is a CTA. However, this is not the case at all."
- **Ads And Leads**: three-point autopsy, platform icons (where they scale), hook (first 3 seconds / first sentence), format (studio vs raw iPhone).
- **Nick Theriot**: review 3-4 ads per brand and log repeating patterns in hook/desire, filming style, and script.

Contested edge: **Amir Rasheed** and **Ads And Leads** foreground the creative-format fields; the strategic sources insist the offer/guarantee is the real conversion driver (see Theme 6). Both are logged; they are complementary, not opposed.

## Theme 4: Two search modes: brand name vs keyword; and product vs audience (CONSENSUS)

You find competitors two ways, and service businesses search differently from product businesses.

- **Every source** covers brand-name search and keyword search in the Ad Library (Google "Facebook Ad Library" → set country → All Ads → search).
- **Gaurav Singh** (the sharp distinction for a service business): a product business searches its product name; a service/coaching business searches the *target audience or field*, not the service name. Searching "coach" returned 5,600 results.
- **Ads And Leads**: use quoted keyword search (e.g. "red light therapy") when you do not yet know named competitors, to surface the full niche.
- **HubSpot Marketing**: if no direct competitor runs ads, study large adjacent-industry brands (handmade jewellery → Mejuri; local gym → F45) and adapt.
- **Nick Theriot** (direct vs indirect): direct competitors share product + avatar; indirect competitors share the underlying desire via a different mechanism. Both must be mined separately, and they get different adaptation levers (Theme 8).

This is the most decision-relevant navigation theme for a service business: search the buyer and the problem, not your own offer name.

## Theme 5: The Ad Library location filter is about delivery, not the advertiser (CONSENSUS on the mechanic, often missed)

A recurring gotcha that trips beginners.

- **Ben Heath**: "The location you set is not where you are, or even where the competitor is based... it's where the ads are being shown." He recommends selecting all locations when researching a competitor so no delivered ads are missed.
- **Amir Rasheed / MUSAB MULTIMEDIA STUDIO**: both demo the country filter as a required pre-search step.
- **Gaurav Singh**: full filter panel, country, category (All Ads vs political), language, platform, active/inactive status, and custom date range.

Slight tension: Heath says "all locations" to see everything for discovery; a target-country filter is better for relevant benchmarking. Both are right for different jobs.

## Theme 6: Mine the OFFER and the funnel, not just the creative (CONSENSUS among the strategic sources)

The deepest sources push past the ad into the offer structure and the landing page.

- **BKA Content** (clearest statement): reads a home-builder ad's stacked offer verbatim, a "smart move guarantee" with "up to $10,200 cash back", "fast cash offers", or a "guaranteed three-year lease", and says "the offer is what hooks people to actually take action on your ads", so the guarantee stack is what to replicate, not the visuals. (Cited here for its uncontested offer-focus point; its separate longevity heuristic is era-flagged, see Exclusions.)
- **Ads And Leads**: funnel-hack past the ad, click the CTA, check ad-to-landing-page headline congruency, identify offer type (discount/webinar/free consult), and actually opt in with your email to see the follow-up sequence.
- **Nick Parker** (why the front-end can lie): an ad with suppressed engagement generated 200-300 leads/week at $0.42/lead because the real driver was a strong back-end offer (extended trial with a deadline), invisible from the ad alone.
- **Ben Heath / Gaurav Singh / Amir Rasheed / Nick Theriot**: all click the CTA to land on the real destination page and reverse-engineer the funnel (sales page vs homepage vs VSL).

Consensus: the ad is the visible tip; the offer and the follow-up are where the money is. This is the most important theme for a workshop/course model, where the seat is a front end for ascension.

## Theme 7: Surface signals mislead; verify with a controlled test (CONTESTED, and the strongest anti-noise voice)

Nick Parker is the batch's dedicated "noise vs real winner" source, and he directly disputes the naive reads the tutorial sources rely on.

- **Nick Parker** on engagement: likes/comments/shares are an unreliable proxy, low engagement can still be a winning ad (the $0.42/lead example above). On production polish: a rival gym ran 100% polished video, grew for a year, then closed, "just because they came out and looked innovative, doesn't mean it was working." On offers: copying an offer verbatim without the back-end monetisation fails (the "30-day pass" prospect got 2-3 conversions because he lacked the downsell that was "closing those within the first 3 days" for others).
- **Nick Parker's verification loop**: evaluate → test ONE variable at a time (offer, image type, language, headline) → find winners and scale. Benchmark: you know inside 48-72 hours or 3,000-5,000 impressions, not the "30 days" agencies claim.
- **Ads And Leads** partly disputes Parker on polish: "In 2026, ugly ads often outperforms pretty ads" because polished cinematics subconsciously signal "advertisement" and trigger scroll-past ("We have been trained for over 20 years to ignore commercials"). Parker's own gym data once showed images beating video. So the two agree polish is not proof, but Ads And Leads goes further and says polish can actively hurt.

**The wider verified evidence sides with Parker.** The manual-control synthesis is explicit that CTR/engagement/hook-rate explain WHY, never decide WHAT, and that decisions are made on cost-per-result on a fixed schedule. Treat every Ad Library read as a hypothesis to test on your own account, never as a settled winner.

## Theme 8: Adaptation levers: how to rework a winner legally and effectively (Kast + Theriot, complementary frameworks)

Two sources give the deepest machinery for turning a spied angle into original, better creative.

- **William Kast**, awareness-stage-specific script engineering. Uses the five awareness stages (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware) to decide which angle to mine; product-aware and most-aware are "easiest/most targeted", unaware is "the hardest to crack" and "usually the biggest". Gives full script skeletons (see Named frameworks in SKILL) and prompts Claude directly: "give me five product aware frameworks that are currently top performing on social media." Judged the unaware-stage output weaker and needing human awareness expertise, especially the hooks.
- **Nick Theriot**, three adaptation levers: new mechanism (mainly for indirect competitors), new desire/persona (for direct competitors), and a larger/bigger claim (one-upping direct competitors). Case: a paint-by-numbers brand capped at $100k/month because every competitor targeted "stress"; pivoting the same proven structure to a "girls' fun night out" persona took it past $1M/month.

Consensus between them: mine the structure, then change the variable that differentiates (awareness stage, mechanism, persona, or claim size). Neither endorses swapping only the branding.

## Theme 9: Tooling: manual Ad Library, ad-spy SaaS, AI, and scrapers (CONTESTED on necessity)

The batch splits on whether you need paid tools.

- **Manual-only camp**: Gaurav Singh, Amir Rasheed, MUSAB MULTIMEDIA STUDIO, HubSpot Marketing, Learn with Bilal, the free Ad Library plus ChatGPT/Claude is enough.
- **Ad-spy SaaS camp**: William Kast (Atria/Foreplay), Nick Theriot (Trend Track over Foreplay because it pulls live from Facebook's system, "foreplay, you only see ads saved by other users"), Mr. Paid Social (Foreplay's longest-running sort; Charlie the Disruptor's ad shown at 416 days live), Basil Naser (Ad Whisper, $19/mo, adds spend/revenue estimates like Clash Royale's $87k spend → $228k revenue over 7 days).
- **Automation/scraper camp**: Jake Dawson (Apify + Make.com + Google Sheets, ~75 cents per 1,000 ads, $5 free credit/month) for scaling swipe collection beyond manual browsing.
- **Multi-method camp**: Mr. Paid Social chains six methods (ChatGPT Deep Research for discovery, Meta library, TikTok Creative Center, SimilarWeb, Foreplay, and Instagram-algorithm priming). The algorithm-priming trick, follow ~10 competitor accounts, click their CTAs, scroll until their ads appear, uniquely surfaces live engagement numbers the library hides.

Key limitation flagged across the camp: the raw Ad Library does NOT tell you which ad performs best (Mr. Paid Social: "What this doesn't do is tell us which one of these ads is performing the best"). Tools that add a longest-running sort or spend estimate exist to close that gap. For a small account, Ben Heath's free impressions-sort covers most of the value without a subscription.

## Theme 10: Independent performance signals beyond run-length (emerging consensus)

Sources name several corroborating signals to avoid relying on longevity alone.

- **Impressions high-to-low sort** (Ben Heath): the most reliable single addition; long-running AND high-impression is the near-guarantee.
- **Duplicate count** (Nick Theriot): an ad duplicated repeatedly is usually being relaunched because it works, "if it's been duplicated over and over and over, there's a reason behind that", with the honest caveat it could also mean repeated rejections.
- **"Multiple versions" / dynamic-creative label** (Ads And Leads): a dynamic ad (5 headlines x 5 images) running 6+ months is a super winner.
- **Ad volume and consistency** (Ads And Leads: 50+ active ads = "spending real money"; Ben Heath: a competitor running 10-15x your active ad count signals you need more creative output).
- **Follower/scale proxies** (Amir Rasheed: Sana Safinaz 1.7M FB / 2.7M IG followers; 37 running ads), weak proxies, use for context only.

Consensus is that no single number is proof; stack two or three (long run + high impressions + duplicated + dynamic) before treating an ad as a real winner.

## Exclusions

- **Go Digitz, "How to Find Competitor Ads on Facebook 2026"**: excluded from theme backing, flagged thin. Pure UI navigation with no strategic layer; adds nothing the other tutorials do not.
- **Blake Bauer Business, "How To Use The Facebook Ad Library To Find Winning Ads"**: era-flagged and dropped from longevity backing. Its central heuristic ("oldest currently-running ad = winner") is presented WITHOUT the impressions caveat that Ben Heath and the wider verified evidence require, and it is dropshipping/product-brand framed. Its neutral mechanics (per-ad detail fields, keyword-vs-brand search, the screenshot-to-ChatGPT rebuild workflow) overlap uncontested consensus but are not needed as unique backing.
- **Learn with Bilal** is retained but its dropshipping/physical-sourcing framing (Shopify best-seller URL trick, local supplier sourcing) is translated to our context and not carried literally; its longevity read is used only as one voice within the Theme 1 caveat, not as standalone proof.
