## What the experts agree on

1. **Longevity is the core winner signal, but it is a filter, not proof.** A long-running ad is probably profitable because advertisers kill losers fast (Ads And Leads: 90-180 days; HubSpot Marketing; Gaurav Singh; Learn with Bilal). Ben Heath adds the correction the whole batch now needs: Meta leaves low-spend ads "active" for months, so pair run-length with an impressions high-to-low sort. Our verified manual-control base agrees with Heath.
2. **Never clone the creative; extract the structure and rebuild original.** Reasons split legal and effectiveness (Learn with Bilal: copyright strike; William Kast: a copycat advertises the first-mover; Nick Theriot: each recreation devalues the concept; Nick Parker: you inherit the copy but not their back end). MUSAB softens it to "copy the style and upgrade".
3. **Extract a fixed field checklist per ad, not a vibe** (Amir Rasheed, Learn with Bilal, Ads And Leads, Nick Theriot): format, hook, primary text, headline, description, offer, CTA (the persuasive line, not the button), platforms, and start date.
4. **Search two ways, and service businesses search the audience not the offer** (Gaurav Singh: search "coach" → 5,600 results; Ads And Leads: quoted keyword; every source: brand name). If no direct competitor advertises, study adjacent large brands (HubSpot Marketing).
5. **Mine the offer and the funnel, not just the creative** (BKA Content: "the offer is what hooks people"; Ads And Leads: opt in to see the follow-up; Nick Parker: the real driver is often the invisible back-end offer). Click the CTA to reverse-engineer the destination page.
6. **Surface signals mislead; verify on your own account** (Nick Parker: engagement, polish and follower counts all lie; test one variable, know inside 48-72 hours). Our verified base agrees: decide on cost-per-result, never on engagement.
7. **Stack corroborating signals before calling a winner** (Ben Heath impressions; Nick Theriot duplicate count; Ads And Leads 50+ active ads). No single number is proof. **One signal in that stack has expired:** the dynamic-creative / "multiple versions" label no longer indicates a deliberate permutation test, because Dynamic Creative was retired into Flexible / Advantage+ creative — see the correction under the Ads And Leads method below.

## Named frameworks & methods

**Ads And Leads, the structured spy method (thresholds attached):**
1. Filter volume + consistency: 50+ active ads = "spending real money".
2. Longevity threshold: minimum 3 months (90 days); 90-180+ signals profitability.
3. Dynamic-creative tell: a "multiple versions" ad (5 headlines x 5 images) running 6+ months = super winner. **⚠️ Unreliable as of 2026-07-28 — do not lean on this.** Dynamic Creative was retired into the Flexible / Advantage+ creative format, so "multiple versions" no longer maps cleanly to a deliberate permutation test. It now also appears on ads where Meta's own creative automation generated the variants, and it is absent from plenty of genuine multi-asset tests. A "multiple versions" label today tells you almost nothing about the advertiser's intent or the ad's performance. Substitute the signals that still work: **impression bucket + run length read together**, and **duplicate count** (the same advertiser running many near-identical live copies), which remains a strong tell because it reflects a decision a human made with money.
4. Three-point autopsy: platform icons (where they scale) → hook (first 3 seconds / first sentence) → format (studio vs raw iPhone).
5. Funnel-hack: click CTA → check ad-to-page headline congruency → identify offer type → opt in with email to see the sequence. Benchmark: 10-15 hours to map a client's full landscape.

**Ben Heath, impressions-plus-longevity:** sort the library by impressions high-to-low, then keep ads that ALSO have long run duration. Long + high-impression is "as close to a guarantee" of a real winner. Start with the highest-impression, longest-running ads first to beat overwhelm.

**What you will actually see when you sort by impressions.** The Ad Library does not give you a number. It gives you a **bucket**, and knowing the buckets before you open the tool saves a lot of confusion:

`under 1K` · `1K-5K` · `5K-10K` · `10K-50K` · `50K-100K` · `100K-500K` · `500K-1M` · `1M+`

Read them like this:

- **`under 1K`** — barely delivered. Combined with a long "active" run, this is exactly the dud Heath warns about: Meta parks low-spend ads as active for months. Long run + `under 1K` = **not** a winner.
- **`1K-5K` / `5K-10K`** — real but small. Could be a test still running, could be a small local advertiser where this genuinely is meaningful spend. Judge relative to the advertiser's size.
- **`10K-50K` upward** — money is behind it. This is where the longevity signal starts meaning something.
- **`100K+`** — either a serious scaled winner or a brand doing awareness. Check the format and the CTA before assuming performance.

Two more things on the screen you should expect:

- **The "Low Impression Count" badge**, shown on ads with **fewer than 100 impressions**. Meta hides granular data on these. Treat as noise, always — never model an ad carrying this badge.
- **Buckets are per-ad, not per-advertiser, and they cover the ad's whole lifetime**, so a long-running ad accumulates a higher bucket simply by existing. That is precisely why Heath's rule is impressions AND longevity read *together*, not either alone. An ad that reached `100K-500K` in three weeks is a stronger signal than one that reached the same bucket over eighteen months.
- In the **EU**, additional transparency data (reach and demographic splits) appears that is not available elsewhere. If you can filter to an EU country, you get a better read.

**Nick Theriot, direct/indirect split + three adaptation levers.** Direct competitor = same product + avatar; indirect = same desire, different mechanism. Levers to rework a winner without cloning: (1) new mechanism (mostly indirect), (2) new desire/persona (direct), (3) bigger claim (direct, one-up them). Case: paint-by-numbers capped at $100k/mo on "stress" → new "girls' fun night out" persona → past $1M/mo. Manual 30/60/90-day method: set the date filter to exclude the most recent 30/60/90 days to isolate surviving ads (example: 480 active → 79 at the 90-day cut).

**William Kast, awareness-stage script engineering.** Five stages (unaware → problem → solution → product → most aware); mine product-aware/most-aware first (easiest), unaware last (hardest, biggest). Product-aware script skeleton: hook (compare to competitor + call out frustration) → agitation → new mechanism → proof stack → reframe investment (e.g. "50 cents per night") → risk reversal → urgency CTA. Also the 6-beat switcher framework and the myth-busting takedown. Claude prompt: "give me five product aware frameworks that are currently top performing on social media."

**Nick Parker, verify-with-a-test loop.** (1) evaluate their ads/page/retargeting feed, (2) test ONE variable at a time (offer, image type, language, headline), (3) find winners and scale. Signal speed: 48-72 hours or 3,000-5,000 impressions, not the "30 days" agencies claim. Discovery extras: page-transparency path to a competitor's library; visit their site to get pixeled into their retargeting pool.

**Mr. Paid Social, six-method chain:** ChatGPT Deep Research for discovery (CSV of 10 competitors) → Meta library → TikTok Creative Center (per-ad metrics + AI "why it works") → SimilarWeb (traffic benchmarking, tracks up to 25 sites) → Foreplay longest-running sort (416-day example) → Instagram algorithm-priming (follow ~10 competitors, click CTAs, scroll until their ads appear, to see live engagement).

**Amir Rasheed, creative-format checklist:** format (single image/video/carousel), aspect ratio (1:1 vs vertical), headline, description, CTA, angle type (needs / wants / UGC / CGI). Meta's own claim: 56% of performance is creative. Log design-variant counts (one advertiser showed 3, another 7).

**Jake Dawson, scraper pipeline:** Apify "Facebook ads library scraper" + Make.com + Google Sheets, ~75 cents per 1,000 ads, $5 free Apify credit/month. Route by format (carousel/video/single-image); set "run synchronously" = yes so Make waits for the scrape.

**Learn with Bilal, red-flag rule + tracking window:** only enter a niche where 3-4+ competitors already advertise; zero competitors = red flag (unproven), not blue ocean. Track a candidate ad daily for 3-5 days (Meta's ~3-day learning-phase minimum) before modelling.

## Contrarian / disputed takes

- **"Long-running = winner" vs "you can't trust run-length anymore".** The tutorial camp (Ads And Leads, HubSpot, Gaurav Singh, Learn with Bilal, and the era-flagged Blake Bauer) treats a long run as proof. **Ben Heath disputes it**: Meta parks low-spend ads as "active" for months, so run-length alone flags duds as winners; add the impressions sort. Our verified base sides with Heath. Use longevity as a first cut, never as the verdict.
- **Polished creative: neutral vs actively harmful.** Nick Parker says production polish is not proof (a slick all-video gym grew then closed). **Ads And Leads goes further**: "In 2026, ugly ads often outperforms pretty ads" because cinematics signal "advertisement" and trigger scroll-past. So polish is at best no signal, at worst a negative one.
- **Do you need paid spy tools?** Manual-only camp (Gaurav Singh, Amir Rasheed, MUSAB, HubSpot, Learn with Bilal) says the free library plus ChatGPT is enough. Paid-tool camp (Kast, Theriot, Mr. Paid Social, Basil Naser) argues the library's blind spot, it will not tell you which ad wins, justifies Foreplay's longest-running sort, Trend Track's live pull, or Ad Whisper's spend estimates. Ben Heath's free impressions-sort largely closes the gap without a subscription.
- **Mimic-and-upgrade vs say-the-missing-thing.** MUSAB and HubSpot say adapt the winning formula. **Kast and Theriot disagree in kind, not degree**: the copy itself is the mistake; research exists to find what competitors are NOT saying, then differentiate on mechanism, persona or claim.
- **HubSpot's Advantage+ endorsement is out of scope for us.** HubSpot's step 5 says "don't skip AI, use Advantage+ to test versions and auto-reallocate budget." We run manual control and do NOT hand testing to Advantage+ (see "what does not apply").
- **Engagement as a proxy.** Mr. Paid Social prizes the Instagram-priming trick precisely because it shows live engagement the library hides. Nick Parker warns engagement is an unreliable proxy on its own. Both can hold: collect engagement as context, never as the kill/keep decision.

## Execution playbook

### IF/THEN operating rules

- **IF you are researching a service/education business (coach, course, workshop)** THEN search the audience or problem, not your own offer name (Gaurav Singh: "coach" → 5,600 results). Search the buyer's pain, the outcome, and adjacent categories.
- **IF you cannot find direct competitors advertising your offer** THEN study large adjacent brands and adapt (HubSpot Marketing). Do NOT read "nobody advertising this" as blue ocean; Learn with Bilal calls zero competitors a red flag.
- **IF an ad is long-running** THEN do NOT call it a winner yet; sort by impressions high-to-low and keep only long AND high-impression ads (Ben Heath) — anything sitting in the `under 1K` bucket or carrying the "Low Impression Count" badge is a dud regardless of run length. Then check **duplicate count** (Theriot), which still works. **Do NOT rely on the "multiple versions" / dynamic-creative label** — Dynamic Creative was retired into Flexible / Advantage+ creative, so that label no longer distinguishes a deliberate test from Meta's own automation (correction 2026-07-28).
- **IF you have found a genuine winner** THEN teardown the fixed field set (hook, primary text, headline, description, offer, CTA line, format, platforms, start date) and funnel-hack the destination by clicking the CTA and opting in (Ads And Leads, Amir Rasheed).
- **IF you are tempted to swap only the branding** THEN stop; rework via a differentiating lever instead, new mechanism, new persona/desire, bigger claim (Theriot), or a different awareness stage (Kast).
- **IF an ad shows low engagement or looks unpolished** THEN do not dismiss it; low engagement can hide a strong back-end offer (Nick Parker) and raw footage can outperform (Ads And Leads).
- **IF you want to act on a spied concept** THEN treat it as a hypothesis, brief it, and test ONE variable on your own account. Parker's 48-72 hours / 3,000-5,000 impressions gives you an early directional read; the verdict still waits for the house gate ($100 + 7 days + 3 conversions).
- **IF you need to see a competitor's live in-feed engagement** THEN prime your Instagram (follow ~10, click their CTAs, scroll), the library hides engagement (Mr. Paid Social).
- **IF you are mapping many competitors at scale** THEN consider the Apify + Make + Sheets scraper (~75c/1,000 ads, Jake Dawson); for a handful, do it manually.

### Default numbers experts use

- Longevity threshold: **90 days** minimum, **90-180+** = strong (Ads And Leads). *(The old "dynamic ad at 6+ months = super winner" rule is retired — the label is no longer meaningful, see correction above.)*
- Impression buckets you will see: `under 1K` · `1K-5K` · `5K-10K` · `10K-50K` · `50K-100K` · `100K-500K` · `500K-1M` · `1M+`, plus a **"Low Impression Count" badge under 100 impressions** (always noise).
- Volume signal: **50+ active ads** = spending real money (Ads And Leads).
- Signal speed on your own test: Parker's **48-72 hours or 3,000-5,000 impressions** is a *directional read*, not a verdict. **The verdict rule is the house gate: no kill or scale call below $100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions**, and at low lead volume even that is a fair-look floor rather than proof one creative beat another (see the noise warning in `brain-meta-creative-strategist-manual`).
- Candidate tracking window before modelling: **3-5 days** (Learn with Bilal).
- Competitor density to enter a niche: **3-4+ actively advertising** (Learn with Bilal).
- Ads per brand to find patterns: **3-4** (Nick Theriot).
- Time to map a full landscape manually: **10-15 hours** (Ads And Leads).
- Review depth: study the **highest-impression, longest-running** ads first (Ben Heath).

### Pre-flight checklist

1. Country/location filter set correctly, remember it is delivery location, not the advertiser's location (Ben Heath). Use all-locations for discovery, our target city/country for benchmarking.
2. Category set to "All Ads" (not the default Issues & Politics) (Ads And Leads).
3. Search decided: brand name if known, audience/problem keyword if not.
4. Sort set to impressions high-to-low, not just most-recent (Ben Heath). Know the buckets before you look (`under 1K` → `1M+`) and discard anything badged "Low Impression Count".
5. A swipe sheet ready with the fixed field columns.
6. A clear question: what angle/offer/format gap am I looking for, not "what can I copy".

### Top 5 failure modes and the fix

1. **Trusting run-length as proof.** Fix: pair with the impressions sort and duplicate count (Ben Heath, Theriot).
2. **Cloning the ad with your logo swapped in.** Fix: extract structure, then differentiate on mechanism/persona/claim/awareness stage (Kast, Theriot). It is also a copyright-strike risk (Learn with Bilal).
3. **Copying the offer without the back end.** Fix: opt in and map the follow-up sequence; assume the real driver may be invisible (Nick Parker, Ads And Leads).
4. **Judging on engagement or polish.** Fix: decide only on cost-per-result after a controlled test on your own account (Nick Parker; matches our verified base).
5. **Searching your own service name for a service business.** Fix: search the audience, the problem, and adjacent categories (Gaurav Singh, HubSpot).

## Applied to your business

Research is worthless without a decision waiting for it. Fill this in before you open the Ad Library, or you will spend ten hours building a swipe file nobody uses.

**Write down first:**
- Your offer ladder: `[front-end offer]` at `[$price]` → `[ascension offer]` at `[$price]`. The ladder is what makes competitors' offers legible — you are looking for *their* front-to-back structure, not their ad design
- Ad account `<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>` · pixel `<YOUR_PIXEL_ID>` · page `<YOUR_PAGE_ID>`, and confirm the pixel and CAPI actually fire before you test anything you find
- Your target cost per result per offer: `[$X]` — the only number that will decide whether a spied concept worked
- Which funnel this research feeds, and which it does not. Offers you do not advertise get no research time

**Consensus translated into execution moves:**
1. **Search the buyer and the problem, not your own product name.** A service or education business that searches its own offer name finds almost nothing. Search the audience and the pain instead — the outcome your buyer wants, the job they are trying to escape, the adjacent categories they already buy from. List your direct competitors (same product, same buyer) and your indirect ones (same desire, different mechanism — often not a company at all, but the spreadsheet or the sixty-hour week) and mine both.
2. **Mine the OFFER stack, not the visuals.** The visible ad is usually a front end for something else. Funnel-hack by clicking the CTA and opting in, then map the sequence: what they promise, how they create urgency, what the real back-end offer is. Translate the *structure*; check every claim against what you can actually stand behind before importing it.
3. **Longevity + impression bucket, then verify on your own account.** Use the impressions sort to find genuine winners (discard `under 1K` and anything badged "Low Impression Count"), brief them as one concept each, and test one variable at a time. Judge on cost per result against your own target, with the verdict gated at $100 + 7 days + 3 conversions.
4. **Feed the swipe file into briefs, never into clones.** Rework via a new mechanism, a new persona, a bigger claim, or a different awareness stage. Say the thing your competitors are not saying.
5. **Re-check competitors on a cadence, not obsessively.** Once in a while is enough. The point is to avoid packaging your offer identically to everyone else in your category, not to react to every ad they launch.

**What may NOT apply to you:**
- **"Hand testing to Advantage+" (HubSpot step 5), and the 3-2-2 DCT test ad set this brain used to prescribe as the alternative.** **Correction 2026-07-28: the DCT container no longer exists.** Dynamic Creative became the Flexible ad format, and Flexible was removed as a standalone ad-setup option in March 2026 — it is now a **"Flexible media" toggle inside Advantage+ creative**. "Enhancements off but DCT on" is self-cancelling in the current UI. Keep the grid logic (3 creatives x 2 texts x 2 headlines, all your own inputs) and choose consciously: **separate standard ads** when you need per-asset data on which concept won, or **Flexible media with each individual enhancement switched off** when you want reach and speed and can live with ad-level reporting only. Note the same correction breaks the "multiple versions" winner-detection tell above.
- **Dropshipping and product-sourcing mechanics.** If you sell a service or education, translate "best-selling product" to "best-selling offer" and "local supplier" to "positioning gap".
- **Paid spend-estimate tools.** Nice to have. The free impressions sort closes most of the winner-detection gap without a subscription — start there and only pay for a tool once you can name what it would change.
- **Competitors' budget figures.** "$200/day across 4 ad sets" tells you about their business, not yours. Set budgets from your own CAC math.
