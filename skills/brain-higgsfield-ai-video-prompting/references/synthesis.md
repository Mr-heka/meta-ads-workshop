# Synthesis and decision notes

These are practical options distilled from a retained transcript collection and
checked against Higgsfield's current published guidance. They are not model benchmarks.
Use the [excerpts](quote-library.md) to inspect source wording and the original source catalog (not included in this kit)
for provenance. Examples of a creator's workflow do not establish universal limits.

## Input and continuity

A prepared still can make composition and appearance easier to specify before
animation. Use it when those details matter and the selected operation accepts it.
A text-only scene or motion-reference workflow may fit a different brief. The old
“80%” start-frame statement is unsupported in the retained corpus. It is neither
a measured score nor a verified creator quotation.
Do not convert it into a prerequisite or use it to blame every failed result on input.

In image-to-video, emphasize movement and preserved details without repeating the
entire visible scene unnecessarily. Include identity or setting instructions when
needed; “only camera, action and audio” is a drafting shortcut, not a forbidden-field
rule. A supplied reference does not guarantee geometry, text or identity fidelity.

## Camera and narrative

Describe a camera move through its intended effect: a pullback reveals context;
a close approach emphasizes a reaction. Keep framing, movement direction and subject
action compatible. Ordinary filmmaking vocabulary is descriptive; it is not proof
that a named preset exists or that a model obeys that word reliably.

Use timed beats for a sequence when needed, with durations fitting the chosen
configuration. A written cut is a request, not an enforced edit. Where exact timing
matters, inspect the result and consider assembling separate coverage in an editor.
There is no universal six-shot, two-second or fifteen-second rule in this brain.

## Identity, references and sound

Assign each reference its actual role before choosing controls. Character sheets,
product views and editorial stills serve different purposes. Select the reference
that supplies the needed evidence; do not ban editorial images for every task.
Never derive an asset handle by guessing URL structure or use an unattached @ name.

If sound is supported and relevant, specify dialogue, ambience and music intent.
“no dialogue” does not mean complete silence. If generated audio is unwanted, use
available controls or remove it in post, then listen to the final output. A reported
music defect near a particular timestamp is an observation from that example,
not a current universal failure threshold.

## Iteration and batch work

Review a real result before diagnosing it. Identify the failure: wrong direction,
identity drift, unintended speech, timing, unwanted cuts or missing visual detail.
Change the relevant prompt/reference/setting; keep useful parts and preserve the brief.
A fixed five-to-ten retry budget is not established by these sources for future jobs.

For an authorized batch, use the actual operator's job and asset records. Log only
what the task authorizes and avoid retaining private prompts or assets by default.
A feedback sheet supports review only if it is actually saved, loaded and applied;
it does not make a model self-improving. Concurrency, job cost and retry behavior
belong to the verified execution surface, not this historical collection.

## Claims deliberately excluded from operating rules

- A universal best model or feature to skip, inferred from a creator preference.
- Fixed cost comparisons, savings percentages or recurring attempt counts.
- Guaranteed identity lock, exact internal cuts, or full positional control.
- A rule that every audio-capable model invents speech when sound is unspecified.
- A source or expert absent the retained corpus, supplied from the old synthesis alone.
- Membership prices, workshop facts, service offers and ad-launch instructions.

The current owner preserves supported creative choices and checks provider controls.
Use this brain for reasoning and source context, not as a second executor.
