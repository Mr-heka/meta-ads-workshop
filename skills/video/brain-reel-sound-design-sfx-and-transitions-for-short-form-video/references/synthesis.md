## 1. Intros

- Joseph: opening title card = gear/mechanical SFX on letters popping + second clicky gear layer + sci-fi glitch on background rectangle scale — 3 layered sounds on one intro beat.
- Artlist: stack 3-5 sound layers on an opening shot, never one (astronaut example: radio, intro impact, breathing atmos, 2 impacts).

## 2. Motion-graphic sounds
- Joseph (core): dotted-line animations → clicky SFX; typewriter text → "data" SFX; icon pop-ins → UI click; rectangle/box pops → sci-fi glitch. Separates "texture" sounds (clicks/UI/gear) from "movement" whooshes — texture for anything that isn't a pop/zoom.
- Joseph palette discipline: personal library of 50-60 sounds TOTAL in folders (whooshes, risers, hits, clicks, gears, other). Audition ~100, keep best 10 per category. Sources: Epidemic Sound (preferred), Artlist.io.

## 3. Sync rules
- Joseph: align the PEAK of the whoosh to the fastest point of on-screen motion (most motion blur frame). Order: 1) sound-design all movement, 2) texture everything else, 3) emotional sounds (risers/hits) — step 3 optional.
- Gavin Herman: same peak-to-peak rule independently; "the difference between pro and amateur"; ~10s per cut.
- Artlist: sound may PRECEDE the visual cut (hear it before it lands); impact sound can sell an unseen action.
- 9jBic4IDfxA: impact needs preceding quiet; silence marks transition to next topic.

## 4. Topic transitions
- Artlist: heavy layering at transition points (3 impacts + riser under one cut); reverse an impact to make a riser.
- Joseph: base whoosh + flavor layer (electric/sci-fi) for stylized transitions; music mood swapped per section.
- Gavin Herman: music-to-music = reverse the outgoing track's last big beat as a riser into the new track + volume keyframe; risers/suck-backs between transitions, reversed to exit into silence; end sections with impact+reverb; pan follows on-screen movement.
- 9jBic4IDfxA: separate color-coded tracks; dialogue -15..-25 dB, music -7..-25, never peak 0 dB.

## 5. Mistakes + sources
- Paid: Epidemic Sound (Joseph, Gavin, Vanessa, 9jB — Gavin: plugin AI search by phrase), Artlist.io.
- Free: YouTube Sound Library (lower quality).
- Anti-patterns: one sound per moment instead of layers (Artlist); ducking music volume instead of EQ-cutting voice mids (Gavin); platform trending audio = mute/licensing/ads risk (Vanessa); inconsistent track volumes = "biggest mistake" (9jB).
# Digest 2 (transcripts 8-14)

## Mapping
- FDOi29blAFI → Ben Claremont, "How To Edit Reels Like A PRO in CapCut"

- HqPDRa7i97s → Joseph | Video Editing, "Master Viral Sound Design for Short-Form Content"
- I66_LmOTlSk → Jacob Nordin, "Sound Design for Cinematic Filmmaking / My Process"
- J0ydlAqg6_8 → Tim Runia, "The ONLY 7 Cuts You Need To Tell Any Story"
- JOm_EqBoW0M → Tim Runia, "The ONLY 3 Sound Effects You Need To Tell Any Story"
- KtSh7PGqmBE → The Bhavya Shah, "How to Always Choose the Right Music for Any Video"

## 1. Intros
- Joseph (short-form): whoosh peak aligned to the most motion-blurred frame of the opening animation; two simultaneous motions = small whoosh under bigger whoosh.
- Tim Runia: 4 whoosh flavors — heavy/deep (big or close objects), light/high (fast or comedic), whip (whip-pans), soft wind (aerials). His own hook timeline: risers + whooshes + impacts + clicks stacked.
- Tim Runia: smash cut = abrupt scene change with music/sound cutting out simultaneously; sparing use only.
- Bhavya Shah: highest-intensity beat first, hit SFX + riser built into it; reverb-tail bridge to next segment.
- Ben Claremont: clean hard cut beats built-in transition presets (they "look weird").

## 2. Motion-graphic sounds
- Joseph (CORE PALETTE RULE): double-click SFX = text popups; digital click + bell = glowing elements; highlighter-scrape = rectangle/line drawing; glitch/data = flickering text/counters. Consistency = pick 2-3 sound families and REPEAT them, never a new SFX per element.
- Tim Runia: clicks (shutter/pen/mouse) for snappy 3-4-shot sequences, one per shot; click+whoosh = comedic. "Story Sound Pack" ships pre-mixed layer transitions (one file instead of stacking).
- Abhay: viewer's brain expects sound whenever something moves — silent motion feels "hollow and fake". Non-real elements: any SFX works if speed/timing match; real-world objects need matching foley.
- Sources: Epidemic (Studio auto-score, NLE plugins), Artlist stems (mute distracting instrument), Envato Elements (+AI gen).

## 3. Sync rules
- Joseph (stated twice): SFX on a hard cut → waveform peak exactly ON the cut; SFX on motion → peak mid-motion, SFX length matched to motion length.
- Tim Runia: riser peak at the END of a build, blended into the following hit via overlapping click.
- Jacob Nordin: J-cuts (audio in before visual) and L-cuts (audio lingers after) = main tool for motivated topic cuts; applied to ambience beds.
- Tim Runia cuts: flow cuts match motion direction; action cuts land mid-movement; jump cuts land ON the beat; match cuts align eye position. Pacing = sync tool (fast=tension, slow=emotion).
- Ben Claremont: eyeline-matched punch-ins; ripple out even a few frames of silence.
- Bhavya: time text/graphics to a track's natural beat accents ("two counts" holds).

## 4. Topic transitions
- Tim Runia: new beat = wide → medium → close; hard pivot = riser → smash cut → SILENCE ("hits way harder with a build-up"); reveal = riser + impact.
- Jacob Nordin: whoosh marks intent; riser smooths into a different energy level; deliberate silence between loud sections so the return lands.
- Bhavya: join music sections via cut repeated beat + reverb tail into an impact.
- Ben Claremont: talking-head topic cuts = ripple trim + wide↔close punch-ins, eyeline anchored.

## 5. Mistakes + volume targets
- Abhay's three: (1) whoosh-on-every-cut exhausts viewers in 2-3 min — SFX mark IMPORTANT moments only; (2) skipping ambience makes video feel fake; (3) identical SFX file repeated is irritating — vary pitch/speed/volume slightly each use. Volumes: dialogue 0..-3dB, music -20..-25, SFX -12..-15.
- Tim Runia: flat pacing = tune-out; smash cut overuse kills it.
- Jacob Nordin: don't fill every second; normalize final mix; test on multiple speakers.
- Ben Claremont: CapCut built-in library = copyright-strike risk.
# Digest 3 (transcripts 15-21)

## Mapping
- LFHtCTjwKjc → NOX CUT, "Sound Design Master Class | Full Course"
- LXTwhlf3Qas → Greg Edits Video, "Get PERFECT Audio in Davinci Resolve"
- LmXpbP7dD48 → Kallaway, "How to Create Irresistible Hooks"

- c0YINYT4Ozw → Skymography, "How To Sound Design ANYTHING."
- eimXwqq438c → Jason Yadlovski, "SOUND DESIGN in DaVinci Resolve"

## 1. Intros
- NOX CUT: text reveal = info-UI sound; every directional animation = whoosh (small whoosh for micro-moves); white flash frame = camera-flash SFX; line-draw = Hi-Tech UI sound. Riser opens, ALWAYS followed by an impact for the reveal (Dhruv Rathee pattern) — riser = low→high note = suspense.
- Film Editing Pro: chimes synced to title text + drone underneath as lead-in.
- Jason Yadlovski: tactile clicks per intro text pop; whoosh-in/out brackets the intro; reversed impact = riser under title.

## 2. Motion-graphic sounds
- NOX CUT mapping: pop = text pop-ups; paper SFX = highlighted stats; camera-flash = white frames; whoosh/smush = scroll/zoom. Palette trick: duplicate/stretch/break ONE sound into variants; one sound covers multiple simultaneous events ("use SFX smartly so maximum things are covered"). Free pack: nxdigg.com.
- Jason Yadlovski: ascending-pitch click trick — duplicate a click, pitch up incrementally = mini-riser out of clicks for text-pop sequences.
- Film Editing Pro: reverse impact for sinister beats; whooshes add levity; blend textures close in tone/frequency.
- Skymography: fill lows/mids/highs of the spectrum deliberately; Artlist.

## 3. Sync rules
- Film Editing Pro: edit with waveforms visible; align the APEX of the SFX to the edit (add markers). Restraint: "if you try to make every moment special, none of them will be special" — quiet beats get subtle treatment, the climax gets the full stack. Cut music so hits/dips land on edit points.
- Jason Yadlovski: sync to the brightest frame; reverse a breath so its peak lands as the eye opens; nudge one frame to fix drift.
- Skymography: hierarchy — dialogue > music > SFX accents; prominence tracks what's narratively primary.
- Greg: dialogue to ~-14 LUFS (YouTube), true peak under -1/-2dB, EQ then compression, in that order. LUFS alone isn't enough — check true peak.
- Kallaway (verbal sync): context lean → contrast-word "stun gun" (but/however/yet) → contrarian snapback.

## 4. Topic transitions
- NOX CUT: whoosh default for directional moves; heavier riser-whoosh for big wipes; flash SFX on white frames; smush for scroll/zooms.
- Film Editing Pro: reverse cymbal (suck-back) + boom/bass-drop at climax cuts; visual movement + lighting change = the cue a transition deserves accent.
- Jason Yadlovski: whoosh front starts BEFORE the cut, tail lands after = merges scenes; slow + de-pitch a whoosh to deepen it; layer two whooshes; riser before whoosh for anticipation.
- Kallaway (spoken transitions): context lean → interjection → snapback; "compress speed to value" (~4s countdown); staccato sentences at transition points, lengthen after.
- Dylan: each cut = a decision about feeling; withhold resolution across transitions (Zeigarnik effect).

## 5. Mistakes + mix targets
- NOX CUT #1 mistake: dialogue/SFX/music all at 100% — SFX "just audible, should not prick", music quietest.
- Skymography: low-quality source audio; under-layering; mixing too loud; fear of non-literal sounds.
- Film Editing Pro: tone-mismatched score; zero ambience; identical SFX per repeated action; dry foley without room reverb; over-accenting soft beats. Targets: dialogue ~-12dB, SFX -10..-30, score -20..-30.
- Greg: LUFS ok but true-peak clipping.
- Dylan: chasing flashy transitions instead of emotional pacing; "if someone says 'that editing was cool' you missed".
- Sources: nxdigg.com free pack, Blackmagic free SFX library, Artlist, filmeditingpro.com.

## 1. Intros
- vidIQ: opening = "visual hook" that works with sound OFF; dramatic whoosh cue right at punchline/reveal.
- Learn By Leo: opening stills get motion (slow scale/drift); abrupt scale-in punch on the important line.
- RyanNangle: zoom-through transitions (mask + Ken Burns into object) + matched shake + speed ramp; motion first, impact layered under.
- Marcus Jones: whoosh on anything sliding onto screen.

## 2. Motion-graphic sounds
- Marcus Jones: two SFX types — exaggeration/foley tied to a visual beat (camera-click for photo pop; whoosh for slide-ons) vs "bit" sounds (meme stings — comedy only). Third: ambience beds for mood.
- Learn By Leo: BASE RULE — "when something moves add a whoosh; when something's highlighted, a highlight sound." Two categories, kept separate.
- Herman Huang: palette consistency via frequency LAYERS — small set of building-block layers recombined = varied but consistent; commit to ONE sound style/pack per project.
- Packs: Learn By Leo free pack + freesound.org + Epidemic; Happy Editing transition pack (risers/hits/whooshes, consistent style); Artlist.

## 3. Sync rules
- Marcus Jones (CORE): graphic/text must appear at the same instant the phrase is spoken — early or late "feels weird"; same for music pacing; don't use music to paper over a slow section.
- Herman Huang: re-cut so the music beat lands exactly at the problem→solution topic shift.
- Learn By Leo: zoom punch timed to the important line, not before.

## 4. Topic transitions
- Marcus Jones: punch-ins/outs disguise cuts inside a talking segment; hide a cut behind a graphic/slide overlay timed to the cut.
- Learn By Leo: A-roll sparingly (important lines only), B-roll/motion graphics as connective tissue; don't over-cut ("visual mush"); a clip can hold 10s if it earns it.
- RyanNangle: zoom-through object masks, cutout pop-ins (freeze of next subject scales 0%→pop), boomerang reverses; each with shake + speed ramp.
- Herman Huang: music transitions — reversed beat as riser into new track; exit via nested tail + Great Hall reverb, not hard cuts; risers/suckbacks under entries AND exits.
- x7DITSVQChM: "radio edit" first — mark the music's beat-drops, place major visual transitions at those points.
- vidIQ: cut-in (push toward subject) / cut-back to emphasize and create momentum.

## 5. Mistakes + sources
- Music as pacing bandage (Marcus). Irrelevant B-roll/graphics vs what's said (Marcus). Risers without payoff lose their power; don't overdo hits (Herman). Mixing sound styles/packs across one project (Herman). Over-cutting = visual mush; caption-spam obnoxious (Leo). Dry library SFX sound "stocky" — EQ+reverb them (x7D).
- Sources: freesound.org, Epidemic, Artlist, Happy Editing, YouTube Audio Library, Leo's free pack.
