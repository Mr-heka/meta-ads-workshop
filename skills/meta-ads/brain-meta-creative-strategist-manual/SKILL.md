## The doctrine: creative IS the targeting

Post-Andromeda, the strongest consensus in the whole corpus: **you no longer pick the
audience with knobs — you pick it with the ad.** Interest stacks and lookalikes are
dead as primary levers; Meta finds the buyer, the creative decides who gets found. So
all leverage moves to research + hook + angle + format. Structure exists only to keep
learning volume consolidated (see [[brain-meta-media-buyer-manual]]); the creative is
where the account is won or lost.

- **Nick Theriot**: "It's the ad level... each ad has its own audience it targets." "I believe that my creative creates the audience." "You pick the audience by the bait, which is the ad itself." He's run zero interests/lookalikes since Dec 2022.
- **Máté Hunyor**: "The targeting now happens on the creative side."
- **Dara Denney**: "It's ultimately the creative that drives the targeting and the best results."
- **Blue Sense** (the leverage argument): squeezing 5% from bid tweaks vs "spend the same time making better creative and double or triple the account? Definitely." And: ~30% of any interest group is mis-assigned — interests capture interaction, not intent.
- **Theriot's time split**: "10 to 15 minutes a day inside the ad account... the other 7 hours and 45 minutes building better ads."

Manual-control corollary as the sources describe it (turn the AI creative settings OFF): Advantage+ creative enhancements off (Hunyor: "we don't need AI made images"), Flexible Ads off for testing (no per-creative data). The ONE endorsed automation was **dynamic creative (DCT) permutation matching** — Meta permutes *your* capped, human-designed 3-2-2 grid; you own the inputs, Meta only matches. Full settings kill-list: [[brain-meta-media-buyer-manual]].

> **Correction 2026-07-28 — the DCT container no longer exists as described.**
> Dynamic Creative was replaced by the **Flexible ad format**, and Flexible was then removed as a standalone option from ad setup in **March 2026**. The functionality now lives as a **"Flexible media" toggle inside Advantage+ creative**. So "enhancements off but DCT on" is **self-cancelling in the current UI** — there is no longer a path to the permutation engine that sits outside Advantage+ creative.
> **The testing logic survives; only the container is dead.** A capped, human-authored grid (3 creatives x 2 primary texts x 2 headlines, every input written by you) is still the right unit of test. You now run it one of two ways:
> **(a) Separate standard ads, one per combination you actually want to read.** You get clean per-asset data. Costs you setup time and splits budget across more ads — so cap the grid hard at the 3-4 combinations you genuinely want an answer on, not all twelve.
> **(b) Flexible media ON inside Advantage+ creative, every individual enhancement (image touch-ups, text generation, music, 3D, catalogue items) switched OFF one by one.** Meta permutes your assets and reports at the ad level only. You trade per-asset attribution for reach and setup speed.
> Pick per round, and write down which you picked — otherwise you cannot read the round later. If you want to know *which creative won*, use (a). If you want volume through a proven concept, use (b).

> **Re-correction 2026-08-25 — "switch to original audience" is NOT gone.**
> The 2026-07-28 version of this note claimed Advantage+ Audience had become mandatory on Sales, Leads and App-promotion objectives. That was wrong: **the toggle exists and binds at the API layer** — `advantage_audience` accepts both 1 and 0 on `OUTCOME_LEADS` ad sets, verified live 2026-08-25. What IS true: with Advantage+ Audience **ON**, the API fixes `age_max` at 65 (error 1870189, confirmed in Meta's developer docs), and interests, detailed targeting and lookalikes degrade to *suggestions* Meta may expand past, not fences. With it **OFF**, includes bind again — the pattern for warm-list ad sets. Either way the doctrine on this page holds: whichever way the toggle sits, the creative is the strongest targeting lever you own.

## What the experts agree on

1. **The hook is most of the spend.** Hormozi: "Once you've written your headline, you've spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar." A great hook beats the control 2-5x.
2. **Hook must land with zero context for the least-interested prospect** (Theriot's rule #1) and do two jobs at once — instant clarity + curiosity.
3. **Research beats guessing.** Angles come from real customer language — 2-3 star reviews, comments, DMs, Reddit, and the Ad Library — not a brand book. Theriot: "the fastest way to scale isn't random testing, it's a repeatable, research-driven creative system."
4. **Show, don't say.** Visually show the transformation (Theriot's Emotion Baiting); "Fancy equals friction, simple equals sales" (Hormozi) — 3rd-grade reading level.
5. **Volume and diversity over polish.** Brief 6-10 "ugly" concepts across different angles; let cold testing find the winner. Ugly ≠ bad — it means native, not studio.
6. **Secondary metrics diagnose, they never decide.** Hook rate / CTR / thumbstop explain *why*; cost-per-lead / CPA / ROAS decide what dies. Heath's 16% hook-rate ad lost to a 10% one on cost-per-purchase.
7. **Never touch the winner.** Iterate *next to* it; duplicate via post ID to keep social proof. Old winners still win 7-8/10.

## Named frameworks & methods

- **5 Stages of Market Awareness** (Theriot): Unaware → Problem Aware → Solution Aware → Product Aware → Most Aware. Stage decides what the ad may say. "90% of marketers only run Stage 5 (discount) ads" and can't scale — build across stages to keep pulling colder, larger audiences.
- **Emotion Baiting** (Theriot): tap existing desire → name your unique mechanism → lead with the emotional outcome, reveal product second → show the transformation → first-person emotional copy.
- **3 Essential Hook Elements** (Theriot): (1) understood instantly with zero context by your least-interested prospect; (2) call out something they already want, then the named mechanism; (3) instant clarity + curiosity together.
- **Empathy-First / ugly ads** (Barry Hott): "Think how they think, speak how they speak... the ads will write themselves." Hook in the customer's own words (past-tense, first-person, 3rd-5th grade). SETTING is the most under-weighted relevance signal. "Blend in to stand out." A full ugly ad ≈ 60 min on an iPhone. The Sniff Test flags anything that "smells like an ad."
- **Scaling triangle** (Theriot): scale on **desire** (bigger/universal), **sophistication** (evolve claims as the market tires), **awareness** (open colder stages) — psychology, not budget.
- **Give-give-give-ask + lead magnet** (Hormozi): "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation." Lead magnet = "a complete solution to a narrowly defined problem" (reveal a problem / free sample / one step). Put the ask in the ad, not the content.
- **SPCL proof** (Hormozi): Status, Power (say-do correspondence), Credibility (visible third-party proof), Likeness. Proof is the educator's "Vegas lights."
- **3-2-2 / 3222** (Charley T / Pawliw): the capped permutation grid the creative feeds — 3 creatives × 2 texts × 2 headlines (Pawliw adds 2 landing pages). Originally the DCT container; **since March 2026 the container is gone and only the grid logic survives** (see the correction in the doctrine section). Keep the discipline of a small human-authored grid; choose consciously between running the combinations as separate standard ads or as Flexible media inside Advantage+ creative.
- **Concept-miner loop** (ad-concept-miner): Ad Library pull → teardown → concept board → on-brand variations. Winner signal = one advertiser running many near-identical live copies, or a concept recurring across advertisers. Theriot's version: ads running 6-12 months = proven.
- **8/9 angle sets**: a general set — Pain / Outcome / Social proof / Curiosity / Comparison / Urgency / Identity / Contrarian. A lead-gen set — Build day / Bottleneck / Proof / Authority / Event urgency / Contrarian / Lead magnet / Comparison / Founder-led.

## Numbers that matter (creative)

- Hook rate = 3-sec plays ÷ impressions: >10% good, <5% poor (Heath); Hunyor benchmarks ≥25% ecom. But it never decides kill/scale.
- Hott: "live in the problem" 15-17s before the product; "stack five problems in 15 seconds."
- Test volume: 6-10 concepts/round (Hott); 2-3 new test grids / 7 days, ~1 in 10 wins (Theriot).
- **Read window — one rule, everything else is context.** The house gate decides: **no kill or scale verdict below $100 spend AND 7 days AND 3 conversions.** The expert figures (Hott's ~20 conversions per ad set, ad-creative's 1,000+ impressions, Theriot's 48h/7-day window) are diagnostics that tell you whether an ad has had a fair look — they are not competing verdict rules and they never override the gate. On small budgets, hold longer than the gate, never shorter. See "How to read a round where nothing wins" below for why.
- **Copy limits vary by placement — check before writing, do not trust one flat number.** Primary text is ~125 characters *visible* before the "See more" fold in Feed, but Reels and Stories overlay far less (often ~10 words of headline render before truncation), right column truncates harder still, and Advantage+ creative may rewrite or re-crop text per surface. Practical rule: **write the hook into the first 5-7 words** so it survives every crop, keep headlines under ~40 characters as a safe ceiling and descriptions under ~30, then **preview every placement you are actually running** and read what survives. If the message only works at full length, it belongs in the creative as burned-in text, not in the copy fields.

## Copy — compliance walls (hard, non-negotiable)

Run every headline/body/CTA through your own brand and claims rules before it ships. Hard walls: no invented statistics or fabricated facts, no outcome guarantees, no refund or money-back promises you have not resourced, no support or helpdesk promises you cannot staff. Process language over hype. **Do NOT carry practitioners' own money-back guarantees or income-claim styles into your copy** — mine their structure, not their promises.

## Contrarian / disputed takes

- **All-broad now vs still-train-with-interests** under $30k/mo (Theriot/Fogarty vs Chappell/Piliero) — most relevant to a modest lead-gen account.
- **AI-generated creative**: against for branded work (Hunyor; image generators rarely hold exact brand colours and type → render branded assets from your own template) vs fine for fast non-branded demo assets.
- **CTA placement**: Hormozi keeps the ask out of organic, only in ads ("CTAs in content kill reach") vs hard-DR practitioners who put a CTA in every asset.
- **Ugly vs brand-locked**: Hott's max-ugly phone footage vs a locked brand render system — reconcile by job, not by preference: ugly phone footage for founder and customer video hooks, the brand system for text-and-logo statics and carousels. Both, not one.
- **How long to judge**: Theriot 48h/7-day vs Hott ~20 conversions vs a 1,000-impression floor. **Resolved 2026-07-28, not left open:** the house gate ($100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions, all three) is the verdict rule across this whole library; the three expert windows are diagnostics for whether an ad has had a fair look. Sibling brains quoting "48-72 hours", "2-3 days per cell", "2,000-3,000 impressions" or "at least 7 days" all defer to the same gate.

## Execution playbook

- IF choosing targeting THEN pick the audience with the ad itself, no interest stacks or lookalikes (per Nick Theriot)
- IF briefing a round THEN brief 6-10 native "ugly" concepts across different angles, not one polished ad (per Barry Hott)
- IF writing a hook THEN it must land with zero context on the least-interested prospect, clarity plus curiosity together (per Nick Theriot)
- IF generating angles THEN mine 2-3 star reviews, comments, DMs, Reddit and the Ad Library, never guess from a brand book (per Nick Theriot)
- IF reading results THEN let hook rate and CTR diagnose only; kill or scale on cost-per-lead / CPA (per Ben Heath)
- IF an ad is winning THEN never edit it: pause and duplicate via post ID, iterate next to it (per Máté Hunyor)
- IF Meta offers AI creative help THEN switch the individual enhancements off one by one; the permutation engine itself is the one keeper, and since March 2026 it is reachable only as "Flexible media" inside Advantage+ creative (see the correction above — "enhancements off but DCT on" no longer parses)

Defaults: hook rate >10% good, <5% poor (Heath); 6-10 concepts/round (Hott); 2-3 new test grids per 7 days, ~1 in 10 wins (Theriot); the grid is 3 creatives x 2 texts x 2 headlines (Charley Tichenor); copy hook in the first 5-7 words, ~40 headline / ~30 description as safe ceilings, then preview per placement; 15-17s in the problem before the product (Hott). **The verdict rule is the house gate and only the house gate: no kill/scale call below $100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions** (brain-alex-neiman-method). The 48h / 1,000-impression / ~20-conversion figures are diagnostics, not thresholds you may substitute for it.

Pre-flight:
1. Research pack in hand: 2-3 star reviews, comments/DMs, Reddit phrases, Ad Library pulls (Theriot).
2. Awareness stage picked per angle; not everything Stage 5 (Theriot).
3. Hook passes the zero-context test (Theriot) and the first frame stops the RIGHT person (Hott).
4. Sniff Test clear: no first-frame logo, studio lighting or blank studio background (Hott).
5. Individual Advantage+ creative enhancements off, one by one (Hunyor, Pawliw). **Correction 2026-07-28:** you can no longer switch "Flexible Ads off and DCT on" — Flexible was folded into Advantage+ creative as the "Flexible media" toggle in March 2026, so decide instead between separate standard ads (per-asset data) and Flexible media with enhancements off (reach, ad-level data only), and record which you chose.
6. Copy checked against your own brand and claims rules, compliance walls honoured.

Failure modes:
- Only running Stage 5 discount ads, then stalling: build angles across all five stages (Theriot).
- Editing or killing the top spender: "an atrocity" (Hott); duplicate via post ID instead.
- Scaling on hook rate or CTR: they explain why, CPA decides what dies (Heath).
- Polish over volume: "fancy equals friction" (Hormozi); ship 6-10 native concepts (Hott).

## Creative testing methodology

The part most creative advice skips. This is the operating system: how many, how long, what counts as a win, and what to do on the far more common rounds where nothing does.

### The unit: a concept, not an ad

A **concept** is one argument for why someone should act — one angle, one mechanism, one promise. A concept can ship as several ads (different hooks, different first frames, same argument). You test concepts against each other; you iterate hooks *within* a concept. Confusing the two is why most testing produces no learning: if the winner and loser differ in angle *and* hook *and* format, you have learned nothing transferable.

### How many per round

- **4-6 concepts per round** at $50-90/day. Hott's 6-10 is right at agency budgets; below $100/day, 6-10 concepts starves each one below the read threshold before you can judge it.
- **1-2 hooks per concept** in round one. Widen to 3-4 hooks only on a concept that already earned a second round.
- **One round at a time.** Overlapping rounds make attribution impossible at small spend.
- Above ~$200/day, go back to Hott's 6-10 and 3 hooks each.

### The concept lifecycle

1. **Brief** (before anything is made). One page: the awareness stage, the exact desire, the mechanism named in plain words, the customer sentence it came from (real quote, sourced from reviews / DMs / comments / calls), the format, and the single thing this concept is testing that the last round did not. If you cannot name what is new, it is not a concept, it is a variation.
2. **Test.** Ship it into the live testing ad set alongside the current control. Do not change budget, audience or landing page mid-round.
3. **Read.** At the gate ($100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions, all three) — not before. See "reading a round" below.
4. **Iterate.** A concept that beats or matches the control earns 3-4 new hooks against the same argument, plus one format variant. This is where most of the actual wins come from — a good angle with a better opening.
5. **Retire.** A concept goes to the archive when it fails two consecutive rounds, or when a proven winner fatigues past the refresh triggers below. Archive, don't delete — old winners come back. Theriot's rule holds: old winners still win 7-8 times out of 10 when reintroduced after a gap.

### Expected hit rate — set the expectation before you start

**Roughly 1 concept in 10 becomes a real winner** (Theriot's figure, and the most consistent number in the corpus). At 4-6 concepts a round that means **most rounds produce no winner**. That is the normal result, not a failure of the round.

Plan for it: budget the *programme*, not the round. Ten to fifteen concepts over six to eight weeks is a realistic path to one control-beating ad on a small account. If you expect a winner every fortnight you will kill good concepts early and churn through angles faster than you can generate them.

### Fatigue triggers — when a running winner must be refreshed

Act on **two or more signals moving together** over rolling 7-day windows. One signal alone is noise.

| Signal | Warn | Act |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **2.5** | **3.0** rotate within 7 days · **3.5** refresh now · **4.0** immediate |
| CTR decay vs the ad's own 7-day peak | **-20%** | **-30%** |
| CPL vs the ad's own 7-day best | **+30%** | **+50%** |
| New concepts shipped this month | — | fewer than **3** = fatigue is coming whether or not the metrics show it yet |

Compare an ad to **its own history**, never to another ad or to someone else's benchmark. The frequency bands are the same ones used across this library (see `brain-meta-local-lead-gen`); use these and no other ladder.

### Refresh cadence

- **New concepts: weekly at $200+/day, fortnightly at $50-90/day.** Below that, monthly.
- **New hooks on a live winner: every 2-3 weeks**, shipped *next to* the winner, never as an edit to it.
- **Never edit a winning ad.** Pause-and-duplicate via post ID so the social proof (likes, comments, shares) carries over. Editing resets it.

### Naming convention — makes a winner attributable

Without this, you cannot answer "what actually worked" three months from now. Use one string, same order, every time:

```
[YYYYMM]_[CONCEPT]_[ANGLE]_[FORMAT]_[HOOK]_v[N]
```

Example: `202607_LATENIGHT-ADMIN_PAIN_UGC-PHONE_QUESTION_v2`

- `CONCEPT` — the short slug from the brief, stable across every ad that carries that argument
- `ANGLE` — PAIN / OUTCOME / PROOF / CURIOSITY / COMPARISON / URGENCY / IDENTITY / CONTRARIAN
- `FORMAT` — UGC-PHONE / STATIC / CAROUSEL / TALKING-HEAD / SCREEN-RECORD / TESTIMONIAL
- `HOOK` — the hook *type*, not the words: QUESTION / STAT / CALLOUT / CONFESSION / DEMO / MYTH
- `v[N]` — increments on hook iterations inside the same concept

Keep the same slug on the creative file, the ad name, and the brief. One shared vocabulary is the whole point.

### How to read a round where nothing wins

**This is the most common outcome at $50-90/day and almost nothing written about creative testing addresses it.** Work through it in order.

1. **Did anything actually get a fair read?** Check spend per ad, not campaign spend. If no single ad cleared $100 and 7 days and 3 conversions, **the round did not conclude** — you have no result to interpret. Cut the number of concepts, not the budget, and rerun. Six concepts on $60/day gives each about $70 a week: not one of them reaches the gate. Four concepts, two weeks, is a readable round. Six concepts, one week, is not.
2. **Did they all fail the same way, or differently?** All flat CTR = the *angle territory* is wrong; every concept is arguing something the buyer doesn't care about. Go back to research, not to production. Healthy CTR but no conversions = the creative is fine and the problem is downstream (page, form, offer, price). That is not a creative round failure; stop testing creative and fix the page.
3. **Compare against the control, not against each other.** "Nothing won" usually means "nothing beat the control". That is a legitimate, useful result: the control is stronger than you thought, and it buys you time. Keep spending on it.
4. **Look for the near-miss.** One concept with better hook rate but worse CPL is a hook worth reusing on a different argument. Salvage the component, retire the concept.
5. **Do not conclude from differences smaller than the noise floor.** See below. Most "the pain angle beat the outcome angle" reads at small spend are not real.
6. **Then decide once**: rerun the round with fewer, sharper concepts, or step back to research. Do not do both, and do not launch a replacement round the same day — you will just recycle the same thinking.

### The noise warning — read this before you believe any small difference

At **4 leads a day**, a 3-conversion gate is **less than one day of data**. Differences that small are not signal. Two ads at 3 and 5 conversions over a week are, statistically, the same ad. A concept that produced 4 leads and one that produced 2 have told you nothing about which argument is better.

What this means in practice:

- **A 3-conversion gate is a floor for "has this had a fair look", not a threshold for "this one is better".** It stops you killing early. It does not license you to declare a winner.
- To call one creative genuinely better than another you want **30-50+ conversions on each side**, which at 4 leads/day and two ads is roughly **a month per comparison**. Most small accounts will never have that. Accept it.
- So at small spend, **judge concepts on direction and magnitude, not rank order**. A concept at half the control's CPL over a full month is real. A concept 15% better over a week is not.
- **Prefer decisions that don't need the statistics**: kill things that spent real money and produced zero (that *is* readable), keep things that clearly carry the account, and spend the rest of your effort on making the next concept genuinely different rather than on adjudicating between two near-identical ones.
- Never let a secondary metric (hook rate, CTR, thumbstop) break a tie that conversions can't. It will feel like evidence. It isn't — Heath's 16% hook-rate ad lost to a 10% one on cost per purchase.

## Hook bank

Fill the brackets from real customer language — reviews, DMs, comments, call recordings — never from imagination. The structures are the reusable part; the words must be theirs. Every hook here must still pass the zero-context test: a stranger with no interest understands it instantly.

**Pain / callout**
- "[Audience], if you're still [painful old method], this is for you."
- "Nobody tells you that [common situation] is why [bad outcome]."
- "You don't have a [surface problem] problem. You have a [real problem] problem."
- "[Doing the thing] at [specific inconvenient time] is not a business. It's a job you can't quit."
- "The reason [outcome] keeps not happening isn't [what they blame]."

**Confession / first-person**
- "I spent [time/money] on [thing] before I worked out [the actual lever]."
- "I was [specific embarrassing detail] until [change]."
- "This is going to sound stupid, but [counter-intuitive truth]."
- "Three years of [effort]. Here's the only part that mattered."

**Question**
- "What if [current constraint] wasn't actually the constraint?"
- "How many hours a week do you lose to [task]?"
- "Why does [common approach] work for [group A] and not [group B]?"
- "Would you rather [unappealing status quo] or [reframed alternative]?"

**Stat / specificity**
- "[Number] out of [number] [audience] do [thing]. Here's what the other [number] do instead."
- "[Specific number] hours. That's what [task] costs you a year."
- "It took [surprisingly small number] to [outcome]."

**Myth-bust / contrarian**
- "[Widely repeated advice] is wrong, and here's what it costs you."
- "Stop [common practice]. Do [alternative] instead."
- "Everyone says [X]. The ones actually [achieving outcome] do the opposite."
- "[Popular tool/method] isn't the problem. [Real problem] is."

**Comparison**
- "[Option A] vs [Option B] for [audience], honestly."
- "I ran [A] and [B] side by side for [period]. Here's what happened."
- "The difference between [group who succeeds] and [group who doesn't] is one thing."

**Demo / show-don't-tell** (visual hooks — the first frame does the work)
- Open mid-action on the transformation, no intro, no logo: "This took [short time]."
- Screen recording, first frame already showing the result: "Watch what happens when [action]."
- Before-state held on screen for 2 seconds, then cut: "[Before] → [after]."

**Identity**
- "If you're the kind of [audience] who [specific behaviour], you already know [truth]."
- "This is for [narrow identity]. Everyone else can scroll."
- "[Audience] don't need [thing they're sold]. They need [real need]."

**Urgency / scarcity** (only ever with a genuine, verifiable constraint — never manufactured)
- "[Real deadline] is [date]. After that, [factual consequence]."
- "[Genuine limit] left."

**Objection-first**
- "'[Their exact objection]' — fair. Here's the honest answer."
- "You've probably tried [thing]. Here's why it didn't stick."

**Rules for using this bank**
- Rewrite every bracket in the customer's own words, past tense, first person, 3rd-5th grade reading level.
- One hook = one idea. If it needs a comma to hold two thoughts, it's two hooks.
- The hook must land inside the first 5-7 words for copy and the first 2 seconds for video.
- Log the hook *type* in the ad name (see the naming convention) so you can see which types work for your audience across concepts — that pattern is worth more than any individual hook.
- Never let the hook promise something the offer doesn't deliver. A hook that overshoots buys a click and loses the sale.

## Applied to your business

Everything above is general. This is where you make it yours. Fill the brackets in before briefing a single concept — a creative round built on guesses about your own numbers is the most expensive kind.

**Write down first:**
- Your front-end offer and its price: `[offer]` at `[$price]`
- What it ascends into (membership, retainer, higher-ticket program): `[offer]` at `[$price]` — this is what makes a higher cost per lead affordable
- Your target cost per lead: `[$X]`. Your break-even cost per lead: `[$Y]`
- Current daily budget: `[$Z]/day`. Divide by your CPL: that is roughly `[N]` leads a day — now go back and read the noise warning above with that number in mind
- Your best-performing creative to date, and its numbers: `[name]`, `[$CPL]`, `[frequency]`. That is your control. Everything you test is measured against it, not against the other new ads

**Then work the doctrine through:**
- **Build the angle bank from your own customers' words.** Pull the exact sentences from your reviews, DMs, comments and call recordings. If you have fewer than 20 real customer sentences written down, that is the first job, before any production. Aim at awareness stages 2-3 (problem-aware and solution-aware) — that is where the cold, larger audience is.
- **Brief 4-6 native concepts per round** at a modest budget (6-10 only above ~$200/day), problem-first, real setting, no logo in frame one. Use the concept-lifecycle steps above.
- **Run the grid consciously** — separate standard ads for per-asset data, or Flexible media inside Advantage+ creative for reach. Decide, write it down, don't drift between them mid-round.
- **Mine competitors' ads that have been live 6-12 months** for proven concepts, then rework them via a new mechanism, persona or claim. Never clone.
- **Split production by job**: phone video for founder and customer hooks, designed statics for anything text-led. Ugly means native, not careless.
- **Judge on cost per qualified lead at the gate**, then reread the noise warning before declaring anything a winner.

**Probably does NOT apply to you:**
- **Discount-led (Stage 5) creative**, if your price is holding or rising. Close with proof and genuine deadlines instead.
- **Money-back and risk-reversal framing** as the practitioners use it freely. Check what you can actually stand behind. If you cannot honour it, mine their structure and drop the promise.
- **Ecommerce hook-rate benchmarks (25%+)**, if you sell a considered, higher-ticket offer at low volume. Weight qualified enquiries over thumbstop numbers.
