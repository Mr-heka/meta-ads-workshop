# Landing-page decision methods

This rebuilt reference retains the useful methods in the inherited collection. Creator labels below are inherited attribution, not authenticated biographies or independent replication. Operational suggestions are editorial adaptations to the user's actual page. The [original synthesis](../history/original/references/synthesis.md.original) preserves the complete historical claims and numerical examples.

## Diagnose the conversion path

Identify the incoming promise, page purpose, intended action and what counts as a qualified result. Inspect the page and the route through media, CTA, form, confirmation and downstream handling. A technical failure deserves a direct repair; an unclear message or alternative layout is a hypothesis about user behavior. Do not diagnose either solely from a low reported conversion rate.

Compare equivalent traffic, definitions and observation windows. Check tracking gaps, availability, spam, qualification and conversion delay. A higher submission rate can accompany worse downstream outcomes. Neither the hero nor the traffic source is automatically the cause.

## Make the opening understandable

Amariq's five-element model combines headline, supporting explanation, action, visual and trust. Dupont's three questions ask whether the visitor understands relevance, credibility and benefit. The Website Architect adds identity and what the organization does. Use these as comprehension checks, not a demand to fit every component above every device's fold.

The initial viewport matters, but it is not necessarily seen by every visitor, and the collection does not establish a universal five-second decision window. Use page-specific evidence where available. Preserve meaningful identity, mechanism and logistics when they help the visitor understand the offer; a benefit-only headline is not always the clearest description.

## Write from supported outcomes and real language

Whaling's results-and-roadblocks exercise starts with desired outcomes and perceived obstacles. Useful draft patterns include “How to [result] without [roadblock]” and “[approach] for [audience].” A timeframe belongs only when the offer and evidence support it. Do not invent rapid outcomes to fill a formula.

Sanwarwala's sales-call mining method can reveal the language customers use. Work from authorized recordings or supplied research; preserve meaning and privacy. Suby's multiple-headline approach encourages exploring alternatives before choosing. The number of drafts, headline lines or words should fit the task rather than become a quality threshold.

Sanwarwala's inherited Hormozi Value Equation lens asks about the desired outcome, perceived likelihood of achieving it, time delay, and effort or sacrifice. Use it optionally to find an unexplained benefit, missing relevant proof or unclear process; it is not a validated conversion formula and does not authorize stronger outcome or timing promises.

Keep the incoming ad promise, offer, price and eligibility consistent with the page. Exact repetition is optional when a clearer paraphrase preserves meaning. Guaranteed PPC's segment-specific pages offer one personalization approach; controlled dynamic content is another when it has safe fallbacks and accurate inputs. Test messy or missing inputs. Do not repeat a creator's claim that all keyword insertion fails.

## Choose media for the message

A real person, product view, demonstration, illustration or video can each be useful. The source collection disagrees about image versus video and does not establish a winner for this page. Evaluate relevance, clarity, rights, bandwidth and the actual mobile render.

For video, verify the player, captions or equivalent access, controls, loading and failure state. Avoid requiring audio to understand the essential action. A click-to-play treatment often suits an explanatory video, but source anecdotes do not prove that background video always fails. Inspect current browser behavior before prescribing a technical workaround.

Keep text readable over imagery and preserve the CTA as content moves or wraps. Use responsive layout driven by content; a fixed viewport height can clip material, while a mandatory 80–90% height can create other problems. Scroll cues and a visible following section are options for avoiding an apparent page ending, not proof that a particular height converts better.

## Present proof accurately

Sanwarwala emphasizes specificity, ScoreApp relevance to the lead magnet, and Exposure Ninja discoverability. Select proof that addresses the actual uncertainty: a documented result, relevant testimonial, demonstration or legitimate credential. Keep its scope and date clear. Do not fabricate a full name, portrait, review rating, customer count or logo to meet an anatomy template.

Preserve consent and approved anonymization. Two or three items near a CTA and a longer proof section are different layout options; neither count is universal. Static proof is easier to keep visible, while a carousel or disclosure may serve a constrained layout if important evidence remains discoverable and its controls are accessible. Do not delete valuable proof merely to shorten a page.

## Set the action and qualification deliberately

Jordan Le and O'Herlihy favor prominent forms; Sanwarwala favors explanation before the ask for some audiences. Awareness and intent inform the choice, but traffic source alone does not settle it. A short, familiar action may fit the hero; a complex or consequential action may need explanation first.

Priestley's progressive-capture method can reduce the first step, but later steps still need to complete the actual process. Map each field to a requirement. Name/email/phone is an example, not a default data entitlement. Do not infer a person's location accurately from IP or make phone optional when the authorized service requires a call.

Use the [form reference](primary-sources.md) for labels, instructions, grouping, validation and recovery. Check keyboard use, success and failure states, duplicate submissions and the intended destination. Do not automatically connect a new follow-up sequence or send a message as part of a page redesign.

## Make the CTA truthful and usable

Describe what happens next. “Download,” “Reserve,” “Apply” or “Learn more” may each be accurate for different actions. Avoid a button implying instant access when it submits an application. A primary action plus a secondary call option can serve distinct needs if hierarchy and availability are clear.

Sanwarwala's under-button logistics method can answer real objections: timing, location, requirements or response process. State only approved facts. Repeat useful actions where a visitor may be ready, without mechanically adding a CTA and three checkmarks after every section.

Scroll-to-form, inline forms and dialogs are implementation choices. Verify focus, keyboard behavior, mobile access, errors and return context. A popup is not inherently broken; an inaccessible or misleading one is a defect. Preserve required privacy, terms and other essential links. Evaluate chat or navigation against the actual task rather than stripping every non-form interaction.

## Build the rest of the page around the decision

Amariq's understand/evaluate/trust/act journey and Exposure Ninja's credibility, appeal, objections, proof, ease and result checks cover different needs. Use benefits, mechanism, process, qualification, proof, FAQ and a closing action where they resolve uncertainty. Features are useful when they explain how the promised benefit is delivered.

Suby's direct-response sequence includes callout, promise, intrigue, problem, solution, credentials, benefits, proof, offer, bonuses, value, price, scarcity, guarantee, action and closing reinforcement. Preserve it as an optional sequence, not compulsory sections. Offer terms, guarantees, prices, availability and bonuses come from approved sources; this framework grants no authority to invent or change them.

A closing mini-hero can restate the offer and next step for readers who finish the page. Urgency requires a real reason. Do not copy another business's scarcity or response-time promise. Match page length to the explanation needed, not a presumed attention span or fixed number of sections.

## Use quiz and lead-magnet methods where they fit

Priestley's assessment pattern moves from a relevant question through a short intake to a score, insights and next steps. Explain what the score measures; do not present a generic quiz as a validated diagnosis. ScoreApp and Cattoni emphasize a clear free resource, its contents and relevant proof. A bonus is an offer decision, not a required conversion fix.

Keep the chosen funnel intact. A checkout-page creator's recommendation to eliminate opt-ins does not replace a lead-generation task. If a native form precedes the page, inspect what the visitor already supplied and avoid redundant or confusing asks.

## Verify and learn

Inspect the rendered page at relevant widths, with zoom, keyboard interaction and the actual content length. Check media loading and form behavior before judging persuasion. Use [performance evidence](primary-sources.md) to separate loading, interaction and stability rather than subtracting load time from an invented decision window.

Flux's content-before-layout process and multiple visual concepts can improve exploration; Ali Raza's performance-first review can reveal faults. Choose the starting point from the observed problem. A hero-first experiment is an option, not a mandatory priority over broken submission or misleading terms.

Formal experiments depend on baseline, minimum effect of interest, variability, allocation and observation time. There is no universal 1,000-visitor weekly cutoff or spend/day/conversion combination that proves a result. Qualitative observations can expose usability problems but do not establish a conversion lift. A long-running ad in a public library does not prove spend, profitability or a winning offer.

The original lift figures are mostly agency reports, some concentrated in one operator. Retain them as historical claims when researching; do not average them into a benchmark, promise them to a client or use them to certify this page. Report actual changes, functional evidence, remaining hypotheses and the next justified check.
