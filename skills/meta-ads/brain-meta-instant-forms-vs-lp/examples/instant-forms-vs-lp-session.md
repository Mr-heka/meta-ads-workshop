# Worked example: my Meta leads are junk, form or landing page?

A hypothetical but representative session showing this brain applied end-to-end.
The scenario is invented for illustration; every rule, number and citation comes
from the brain's synthesis and quote library.

---

## The ask

> "We're running Meta lead ads for a home-services business. Cost per lead
> looks great, about $12, but half the numbers are fake or never answer. Do we
> ditch the instant form and send everyone to a landing page instead?"

## Step 1: Reframe off cost per lead

The $12 CPL is the wrong scoreboard. Zwolinski's rule governs the whole
decision:

> "always compare cost per closed deal not cost per lead" — Jacob Zwolinski

> "a $50 landing page lead that closes 30% of the time is better is better than a 15 lead form lead that closes 5%" — Jacob Zwolinski

So the answer is not form-vs-LP yet. It is: why is this form leaking junk, and
would a page actually close more per dollar. A cheap form full of fakes can
still beat a page if we fix the filter, and it can lose badly if we do not.

## Step 2: Locate the leak, it is almost always no filter

The brain's number-one failure mode is junk leads from an unfiltered form. The
fix is not to switch to a page, it is a qualifying question placed BEFORE the
contact fields:

> "add in this first basic question right here. It's a basic filtering question and it improves the lead quality so much on your forms" — Blake Bauer Business

> "So we don't have to actually get these crap leads in and filter them later. We can filter them right here." — Blake Bauer Business

Cardinal ran exactly this play and quantified it: conditional questions dropped
their unqualified-marked-as-spam share from 57% to 19%, and a single
qualifying question "reduced our cost per new member by 44% month over month."

## Step 3: Kill the fake phone numbers at the source

Fake or unanswered numbers are a separate lever from the qualifying question.
The brain's rule: if leads never answer the phone, turn on SMS/OTP verification
and make phone mandatory.

> "They now allow you to verify the phone number with a one-time passcode. That means they can't just put in wrong information." — Dr. Matt Shiver

> "Now please don't put it as an option. They must put in their phone number. So untick it." — Godbless Iboyi

Cardinal stacked OTP after the conditional question and drove spam to zero:

> "As you can see in by June our total unqualified leads by reason were were gone. We had no spam." — Cardinal Digital Marketing

But this brain flags OTP as a genuine two-sided lever, not a free win. Turning
it on is a real volume brake:

> "I'm not going to verify the phone number of the leads by one-time passcode because users may panic and I want to get more data." — Tareq Istiaq

Resolution from the contrarian section: test OTP on/off. It is a real quality
lever and a real volume brake, so measure it rather than assume.

## Step 4: Decide the actual form-type toggle

The account already has lead volume, so the cold-start caveat does not bind.
Ashad's warning only applies to a cold pixel:

> "The form type can be more volume or higher intent. When you're getting started, I would recommend go for more volume because since Meta doesn't have any data existing on your campaign or on your ad account, higher intent may result in zero leads or less leads" — Hasib Ashad

With volume present, move to Higher Intent for the review step:

> "higher it adds higher item adds a step where the cost per where the person has to confirm their info before they send it. It cuts the random clicks by 30 to 50%" — Jacob Zwolinski

## Step 5: Only NOW ask "or a landing page?"

Run Zwolinski's decision matrix against this business:

- Call within 5 minutes and have a dedicated follow-up person? If yes, a form
  stays viable.
- Job value over ~$2,000, or the offer needs a VSL / education / heavy social
  proof? If yes, a landing page earns its place.

For a mid-ticket home-services offer with fast phone follow-up, the answer is:
keep the instant form, but the fixed, filtered version. Reserve the landing
page as an A/B test judged on cost per booked job, not CPL. Gavin Wiener's
default supports starting on the form for momentum:

> "Uh typically, if someone's just running ads for the first time, if we can, I'd like to just run instant forms out of the gate, so we can get momentum" — Gavin Wiener

## Step 6: Wire speed-to-lead or none of it pays off

The brain is blunt that follow-up plumbing decides whether any form pays back:

> "Mistake number nine, any CTA that doesn't create a contact first is a bad CTA." — Joshua cmb | Adding Ally

> "on go high level, you can come to automations as soon as a Facebook lead form is um submitted by whatever your name is" — Joshua cmb | Adding Ally

So: instant CRM sync, auto-response and internal notification the moment the
form submits, plus Audience Network excluded on the campaign (Smart Marketing
Zone: "very cheap leads... wastes our budget").

## The verdict handed back

1. Do not switch to a landing page reflexively. The CPL was never the problem.
2. Add one qualifying question before the contact fields (Blake Bauer, Cardinal).
3. Make phone mandatory and A/B test OTP verification on/off (Godbless Iboyi,
   Shiver vs Istiaq).
4. Move to Higher Intent now that volume exists (Zwolinski).
5. Wire instant CRM sync + auto-response, exclude Audience Network.
6. Then, and only then, run the LP as a parallel test judged on cost per closed
   deal (Zwolinski).

## What made this fast

- Refusing the CPL scoreboard reframed a form-vs-page question into a
  fix-the-filter-first question.
- The failure-mode list pointed straight at the missing qualifying question.
- Treating OTP as a two-sided lever (Cardinal vs Istiaq) kept it a test, not a
  dogma.
- The decision matrix stopped the reflex switch to a landing page that would
  not have closed better per dollar.
