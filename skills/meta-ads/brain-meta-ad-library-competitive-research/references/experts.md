# Experts Mined, Meta Ad Library competitive research

16 videos mined 2026-07-06. Nine are hidden gems (small channels, top-decile engagement); seven are larger channels kept for strong engagement relative to size. Per creator: name, popular vs hidden-gem, angle, and unique contribution.

---

## Nick Parker: hidden gem
- **Angle:** agency-owner counter-narrative. The batch's dedicated "noise vs real winner" voice.
- **Uniquely adds:** the sharpest warning that surface signals (engagement, production polish, follower counts) mislead; the page-transparency discovery path; the get-pixeled-by-the-competitor retargeting trick; and a controlled-test verification loop with a real signal-speed benchmark (48-72 hours or 3,000-5,000 impressions). Also the strongest "the front-end ad hides the real back-end mechanism" cases (the $0.42/lead example, the copied-VSL failure, the Planet Fitness segmentation play). This is the most decision-relevant source for us on not trusting the library at face value.

## William Kast: hidden gem
- **Angle:** agency-side creative strategist. Reframes competitor mining as awareness-stage script engineering, not cloning.
- **Uniquely adds:** the deepest structural machinery in the batch. Named script skeletons (product-aware sequence, the 6-beat switcher framework, the myth-busting takedown), the five-stage awareness model as the lens for which angle to mine, and the exact Claude prompt for generating awareness-stage frameworks. The "rhetorical competition, say what they are NOT saying" framing.

## Ben Heath: popular
- **Angle:** practitioner-agency-owner. Treats the impressions-sort update as the key unlock.
- **Uniquely adds:** the single most important correction in the set, longevity alone is now unreliable because Meta leaves low-spend ads active for months, so sort by impressions high-to-low and combine with run duration. Also the location-filter-is-delivery gotcha, active-vs-inactive history view, and the "discount unreplicable influencer ads, drop to smaller competitors" rule. His view matches our verified manual-control base.

## Nick Theriot: popular
- **Angle:** agency strategist focused on differentiated creative over plagiarism.
- **Uniquely adds:** the direct-vs-indirect competitor split, the duplicate-count performance signal, the manual 30/60/90-day exclusion-filter method (no paid tool needed), the "review 3-4 ads per brand for hook/style/script patterns" rule, and the three adaptation levers (new mechanism, new persona/desire, bigger claim) with the paint-by-numbers $100k→$1M case.

## Ads And Leads (Peter): hidden gem
- **Angle:** agency owner teaching a structured "spy method": volume/consistency filter → longevity threshold → dynamic-creative tell → three-point autopsy → funnel-hack.
- **Uniquely adds:** the most systematic manual workflow. Concrete thresholds (50+ active ads = real money, 90/180-day longevity, 6-month dynamic ad = super winner), the "ugly ads beat pretty ads in 2026" claim, the opt-in-to-see-the-follow-up funnel hack, and the 10-15 hour benchmark to fully map a client's competitive landscape.

## Gaurav Singh: hidden gem
- **Angle:** beginner-friendly walkthrough with the product-vs-audience search distinction.
- **Uniquely adds:** the crucial-for-service-businesses rule, coaches/consultants search the audience or field ("coach" → 5,600 results), not their own service name. Plus the swipe-to-ChatGPT rewrite step ("your own chili masala") and the full filter panel.

## Amir Rasheed: hidden gem
- **Angle:** digital-marketing-agency trainer running the most granular single-competitor teardown.
- **Uniquely adds:** the fullest creative-format checklist (format, aspect ratio, angle type: needs/wants/UGC/CGI), the 56%-of-performance-is-creative framing, design-variant counting (3 and 7 variations on one advertiser), and the formal PPT-benchmarking process across a named competitor set.

## Basil Naser: hidden gem
- **Angle:** demos a paid AI spy SaaS (Ad Whisper, $19/mo) that chat-queries a competitor's whole account.
- **Uniquely adds:** the spend-vs-revenue estimate layer (Clash Royale $87k → $228k over 7 days), chat-based script extraction, and an AI-recommended budget/ad-set structure to compete ($200/day across 4 ad sets). Useful as a signal that spend estimates exist; not something we need to buy.

## MUSAB MULTIMEDIA STUDIO: hidden gem
- **Angle:** beginner manual walkthrough for a dropshipping/course-selling audience (West Africa).
- **Uniquely adds:** the active-only + date-range filter discipline, and the plain "copy the style and upgrade" mimicry framing. Mostly reinforces consensus rather than adding new mechanics.

## HubSpot Marketing: popular
- **Angle:** polished brand-channel tutorial covering find-to-apply, including a 5-step reverse-engineering framework.
- **Uniquely adds:** the adjacent-industry workaround (no direct competitor → study Mejuri/F45 and adapt), the multi-brand CTA-convergence signal ("when multiple brands use the same CTA, it works"), and the clean "adapt the formula, don't copy" statement. Note: its step 5 endorses Advantage+ to auto-test versions, which we do NOT follow (see SKILL "what does not apply").

## Jake Dawson: popular
- **Angle:** automation/workflow builder. Treats the library as a data-pipeline problem.
- **Uniquely adds:** the Apify + Make.com + Google Sheets scraper pipeline (~75 cents/1,000 ads), the format-routing logic (carousel/video/single-image), and the run-synchronously gotcha. Scales swipe collection; contentless on judging winners.

## Mr. Paid Social: popular
- **Angle:** full-stack researcher chaining six methods into one funnel.
- **Uniquely adds:** the ChatGPT Deep Research competitor-discovery prompt (CSV output), the TikTok Creative Center per-ad metrics + AI "why it works", SimilarWeb traffic benchmarking, the Foreplay longest-running sort (416-day example), and the standout Instagram-algorithm-priming trick that surfaces live in-feed engagement the library hides. Flags the library's core blind spot: it does not tell you which ad performs best.

## BKA Content: popular
- **Angle:** polished brand-channel full tutorial, demoed on a home-builder (service business) advertiser.
- **Uniquely adds:** the batch's clearest offer-first framing, "the offer is what hooks people to actually take action on your ads", read off a real stacked service offer (cash-back + fast-cash + a guaranteed three-year lease) to show the offer stack is what to model, not the visuals. One of only three sources (with Nick Parker and Mr. Paid Social) that maps to a service model rather than e-commerce, so its offer-teardown read is weighted higher for our applied section. Its separate longevity heuristic is era-flagged and not used as backing; only the offer-focus point is carried.

## Learn with Bilal: hidden gem (dropshipping-framed, translate)
- **Angle:** e-commerce/dropshipping operator framing research as a full sourcing-plus-advertising workflow.
- **Uniquely adds:** the copyright-strike warning against reusing creative, the CTA-is-not-the-button distinction, the red-flag rule (no competitors advertising = unproven, not blue ocean), and the 3-day learning-phase framing. Dropshipping-specific bits (Shopify best-seller URL trick, local supplier sourcing) are translated to our context, not used literally.

## Source quality notes

- **Usable sources: 14 of 16.** Two dropped from theme backing (Go Digitz thin; Blake Bauer era-flagged on longevity). 14 is comfortably above the 8-source bar, so the consensus reads are well-supported.
- **Freshness:** all 16 videos are within the 18-month window; no freshness exceptions were needed. Several are explicitly 2026-dated.
- **Framing skew:** the batch is heavily e-commerce/dropshipping. Only Nick Parker (gym/local lead-gen), Mr. Paid Social (Facebook-ads course, i.e. an info-product like ours), and BKA Content (home-builder service business) map cleanly to a service/education model. Their reads are weighted higher for our applied section.
- **Thin sub-areas:** the batch is light on measuring a competitor's *back-end* economics from the outside (only Nick Parker and Basil Naser touch it, and Basil's is a paid-tool estimate). It is also light on how often to re-check competitors, only Nick Parker gives a cadence view ("once in a while" to avoid offer saturation). These are noted rather than padded.
