## What the experts agree on

1. **Meta out-targets the advertiser on any account with conversion history.** Detailed targeting fields are suggestions Meta can ignore; Heath's like-for-like test showed identical spend distribution with or without them filled in. (Ben Heath, LYFE Marketing, Chase Chappell, Ash Davis)
2. **Only the hard controls still bind: location, age minimum, gender, exclusions, languages.** Set those correctly, leave everything else broad. (Ben Heath, LYFE Marketing)
3. **Creative is the targeting.** Who appears in the ad and who gets called out in the first 10 seconds decides who Meta serves it to; niche avatar slices beat one generic ad. (Sam Piliero, Ash Davis, Chase Chappell, LYFE Marketing)
4. **Custom audiences do three jobs at once: retarget warm traffic, exclude it from prospecting, and feed Meta signal.** Uploaded first-party lists beat the pixel because a website custom audience can only look back 180 days at most. (Ben Heath, LYFE Marketing, Sam Piliero) **Read that as a ceiling, not a setting.** The retention field is yours to set and **the builder defaults to 30 days**, so an audience you think holds six months of visitors holds one, and it silently drops everyone older every day. Type 180 in the field yourself, on every website audience you build, and re-check it on any audience you inherited.
5. **Bigger audiences and fewer ad sets win.** Long retargeting windows beat tight ones because Meta ranks recency internally; splitting budget across many audiences starves learning in every pocket. (LYFE Marketing, Sam Piliero)
6. **Lookalikes are a background tool now, not a pillar.** Meta builds similar-audience patterns automatically; reach for a lookalike only to force a specific customer type or correct bad prospecting. (LYFE Marketing, Sam Piliero)
7. **The old cold/warm/hot campaign ladder is collapsing.** Advantage+ blends temperatures and creative sequences the journey; the residual manual layer is one cold unit plus one warm unit with exclusions between them. (Ben Heath, Ash Davis, LYFE Marketing)
8. **Meta optimises toward whatever converts, so protect the signal.** Filter unqualified leads before they fire as conversions, and remember in-platform reporting understates recurring revenue. (Ash Davis, Ben Heath)

## Named frameworks & methods

- **Controls vs Suggest an Audience (Ben Heath).** Controls (location, minimum age, exclusions, languages) are hard boundaries Meta never crosses. Suggest an Audience (age range default 18-65, gender, detailed targeting) is take-it-or-leave-it signal. New accounts: fill suggestions. Mature accounts: leave empty, shift effort to Value Rules.
- **N3 system (Sam Piliero).** Prospecting CBO of "broad packs": one ad set per fresh creative batch (broad_pack_1_2026 naming), exclusions on every pack (site visitors 30d, add-to-cart 90d, purchasers all-time). Then single-interest ad sets chosen one step removed from the brand (Range Rover for Nike, not Adidas), populated only with proven winners. Numbers: broad shows 150-184M in the US; one interest drops it to 10-12M; case scaled $46k to $282k monthly spend while revenue went $88k to $544k.
- **The $30k/month ladder (Chase Chappell).** Under $30k/month: single-interest ad sets seed the algorithm ($8.8k in 30 days at 5x example) plus a creative-testing campaign and a retargeting campaign. Over $30k: CBO broad segmented by product ($40k spend, $216k sales, 5.5x). $500k-$1M+: full automation. His Advantage+ container with 10-15 creatives and flexible format conflicts with our verified manual-control base; keep the ladder, drop the container.
- **Hard-controls-only doctrine (LYFE Marketing).** Manually set only location, age (legal reason only), gender (purchase-relevance only). One campaign, two ad sets: broad prospecting plus custom-audience retargeting, warm excluded from cold. Four starter custom audiences: site visitors, segmented email lists, video viewers, social engagers.
- **Avatar-slice method (Sam Piliero, channelling Hormozi).** Ten niche slices of one avatar instead of one generic ad; judge each angle against a ROAS floor independently (his illustration: angles running $100/day at 5x, $1,000/day at 4x, $10,000/day at 2x, $500/day at 3.5x all stay live above a 2x floor) because angles do not cannibalise each other post-Andromeda.
- **Callout-plus-qualification method (Ash Davis).** Name the exact customer in the first 3-10 seconds, describe behaviour not job titles, run awareness/consideration/decision ads simultaneously, and gate bookings with a pre-qualification form so unqualified leads never count as conversions.

## Contrarian / disputed takes

- **Do small accounts still need interests?** Chappell: yes, under $30k/month interest ad sets train the model. Piliero: partly, one broad plus a couple of narrow interest ad sets guides the pixel. LYFE Marketing: no, go broad from day one, interests just thin the data. Our verified base sides with consolidation first, optional narrow interests early, broad once the pixel warms.
- **How much separate warm structure survives?** Heath says Advantage+ Lead/Sales blends warm and cold, so separate retargeting campaigns matter less. Chappell and LYFE still run an explicit retargeting unit. At small spend, keep the explicit warm unit; it is cheap and auditable.
- **Advantage+ as the container.** Chappell prescribes it at every tier; Heath treats it as the direction of travel. Our verified 24-source base (Theriot's controlled test: manual 2.91 ROAS vs ASC 2.14 on the same ads) and our campaign playbook say manual campaigns with Advantage+ Audiences OFF. This brain flags the conflict and sides with the verified base.
- **Premium-segment targeting.** Popka (era-flagged) argues interest stacks still reach wealthy buyers (luxury goods, engaged shoppers, 28-55). Davis argues the label approach is dead and message plus qualification does the job. Side with Davis; keep Popka's one durable rule, premium buyers need premium creative.

## Execution playbook

Operating rules (attributed):

- IF the ad account has conversion history THEN leave Suggest-an-Audience empty and control only location, age floor, gender and exclusions (Heath, LYFE).
- IF the account is new with no conversion data THEN fill detailed targeting as a starting signal and plan to remove it once conversions flow (Heath).
- IF you need a specific person type THEN put them in the creative and call them out in the first 3-10 seconds instead of picking them in the audience panel (Davis, Piliero).
- IF broad performance decays as spend climbs (rising CPM, CPC, frequency) THEN add ONE interest group one step removed from the obvious market, populated only with proven winning ads (Piliero).
- IF building any prospecting unit THEN exclude site visitors, engagers and all-time purchasers so it stays pure acquisition (Piliero, LYFE).
- IF choosing a retargeting window THEN take the bigger window; Meta ranks recency internally (LYFE).
- IF you hold customer or email lists THEN upload them; the pixel forgets everything past 180 days (and past 30 if you left the retention field alone), while lists span years (Heath).
- IF you are building any website custom audience THEN set the retention field deliberately before saving; 30 days is the default, 180 is the maximum, and nothing warns you which one you took.
- IF leads are converting but the wrong type THEN fix the conversion signal (qualification gate, route bad fits to a different page) before touching targeting (Davis).
- IF tempted to add more ad sets or audiences THEN consolidate instead; more pockets means less data per pocket (LYFE).
- IF judging a subscription or recurring offer THEN check revenue outside Meta reporting before killing anything (Heath).

Default numbers the experts use:

- $30k/month: the manual-targeting handover line (Chappell).
- 180 days: **maximum** website custom audience lookback (Heath). **30 days: what the builder gives you if you do not change the retention field.** Set it on purpose every time.
- 30d visitors / 90d add-to-cart / all-time purchasers: prospecting exclusion stack (Piliero).
- 1 interest group per ad set, never 10+ (Piliero); 3 keywords max even in legacy stacking (Popka, legacy).
- 10-12M from 180M: what one interest does to US audience size (Piliero).
- 7 exposures before a brand converts (LYFE).
- 10 avatar slices per concept; roughly 2x ROAS floor per angle (Piliero/Hormozi).
- First 3-10 seconds: the customer callout window (Davis).

Pre-flight checklist before touching audiences:

1. Location set to the real operating area (city radius or countries served), never left at defaults.
2. Age floor set only if a legal or practical reason exists; gender both unless purchase-relevant.
3. Exclusions attached: past purchasers, current customers, warm lists where the unit is cold.
4. Custom audiences built and fresh: site visitors, CRM list uploads, video viewers, engagers. Retention field checked on each website audience (180, not the 30-day default).
5. Detailed targeting empty on mature accounts; one deliberate interest only if running the N3 expansion play.
6. Creative carries the targeting: right avatar on screen, callout in the hook, angle mapped to awareness stage.
7. Conversion signal clean: qualification gate live, no junk events training the account.

Top 5 failure modes and fixes:

1. **Stacking interests to "narrow in" on the buyer.** Post-Andromeda this just shrinks the pool and misleads you. Fix: strip to broad, move the persona into the creative (Heath, LYFE, Davis).
2. **Splitting budget across many small audiences or ad sets.** Every pocket starves. Fix: one cold unit, one warm unit, consolidate until learning exits (LYFE, verified base).
3. **Trusting the pixel to remember customers.** It forgets at 180 days at best, and at 30 if nobody changed the retention field, so old buyers leak back into prospecting. Fix: set retention deliberately, then upload CRM lists and exclude them everywhere cold (Heath, Piliero).
4. **Letting unqualified conversions train the account.** Meta clones whoever converts. Fix: pre-qualification form between ad and booking, route bad fits away from the conversion event (Davis).
5. **Scaling broad past its ceiling with more budget instead of more reach.** CPMs and frequency climb, ROAS decays. Fix: new creative concepts first, then the one-step-removed interest play with proven winners (Piliero).

## Applied to your business

Fill these in before you touch an ad set: ad account `<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>`, pixel
`<YOUR_PIXEL_ID>`, CRM `<your CRM>`, service area or country `<your geography>`, target
customer `<your ICP in one line>`, target cost per lead `<your target CPL>`.

Which of your offers this feeds:

- **A location-locked offer** (an event, a service area): city or radius audience work, `<your radius>`km, applied only while you are actually running paid for it.
- **A national or online offer**: cold audiences across `<your geography>`, once the page and the conversion event are both working.
- **A warm-only or membership offer**: retargeting audiences only, never cold paid.
- **High-ticket or by-application offers**: fed indirectly by the audiences your front-end builds; no direct cold campaigns.

Consensus translated into execution moves:

1. **Run hard controls only.** Location, age floor, gender if genuinely purchase-relevant, exclusions, languages. No detailed-targeting stacks. If you want a soft steer, allow yourself **one** deliberate interest per ad set as the Piliero one-step-removed play, never a pile of them.
2. **Move persona work into creative callouts.** Take your niche list (`<niche 1>`, `<niche 2>`, `<niche 3>`...) and rotate them as Davis-style hooks in the first 10 seconds — name the person and their exact 9pm problem. This is how you reach a segment whose old interest label no longer works.
3. **Upload CRM lists and exclude them from every cold unit.** Past buyers and members drop out of pixel memory fast (180 days at best, 30 if the retention field was left alone); a CRM list does not forget. Exclude past purchasers from cold, and exclude any geography where a different offer is currently selling.
4. **Keep retargeting audiences big and blended.** All site visitors on a long window, video viewers, buyer and attendee lists, and let Meta rank recency internally instead of slicing 7-day pockets yourself.
5. **Protect the conversion signal.** Put qualification questions on the form (team size, revenue band, whatever separates a real buyer from a browser) so bad-fit leads never train the pixel. That is Davis's rule applied to your own anti-ICP list.

What may NOT apply to you:

- **Advantage+ containers, ASC and flexible-format bundles** (Chappell, Piliero's Advantage+ left on): if you run manual campaigns with Advantage+ Audiences off, the audience doctrine transfers but the automation container does not.
- **The over-$30k/month and $500k+ tiers**: at `<your monthly ad spend>` you are likely on the bottom rung of every ladder in this brain, so consolidation rules apply, not scale mechanics like broad packs at $5k/day.
- **E-commerce ROAS framing**: if you sell leads, seats or a single course, your unit is cost per lead against `<your target CPL>` and cost per sale, not purchase ROAS.
- **Legacy premium interest stacks** (Popka): wrong era. A premium segment is reached through avatar callouts and qualification, though his standard for premium-looking creative still holds.
- **Lookalike-centred prospecting** (Param, legacy): demoted by consensus. If prospecting drifts off-ICP, correct with creative callouts and CRM-list signal first, and reach for a lookalike only as the background tool (see Theme 8 in `references/synthesis.md`).
