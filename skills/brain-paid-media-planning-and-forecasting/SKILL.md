## What the experts agree on

1. **Budget is derived backward from a goal, never picked as a number.** Start from sales/seats wanted, run it through close rate and cost per lead, and the spend falls out. (Tracey, Boddington, Rich From Anywhere, Ads Daddy, DoneMaker)
2. **Sale price sizes both the budget and the kill rule.** Cheaper products validate faster on the same spend; kill thresholds are a multiple of the price so the decision scales with the offer. (Ads Daddy, Christian Jamal, Rich From Anywhere)
3. **There is a real spend floor: $5/day buys data, $25-50/day runs a campaign.** Below the floor the algorithm never gets enough signal to exit learning. (Jason Hunt, Ben Heath, Tracey, Joly Tematio, Boddington)
4. **Consolidate budget; complexity is the usual failure, not insufficient budget.** Fragmenting a modest budget across ad sets starves each of signal. Fewer ad sets beats more spend. (Boddington, Ben Heath, Christian Jamal, Nikhil)
5. **Scale only after proof, gradually, gated on backend economics.** Be conservative to start, confirm winners, then step up 10-20% at a time on averaged data. (Joly Tematio, Nikhil, Ben Heath)
6. **Signal accuracy is a prerequisite for every budget decision.** Broken or wrong-event tracking distorts the CAC math before you can budget at all; optimise on the sale event, not proxies. (Nikhil, Joly Tematio, DoneMaker)
7. **Judge a budget on ROI / profit volume, not cost-per-result in isolation.** A £20 CPL is fine if £1,000 returns £20,000; absolute profit dollars beat the ROAS ratio at scale. (Tracey, Ben Heath, Joly Tematio, DoneMaker)
8. **The full at-bat funnel (leads to warm to booked to show to quoted to closed) is the accountability layer that makes a revenue goal real.** (DoneMaker, Tracey, Joly Tematio)

## Named frameworks & methods

- **Break-even CAC / break-even ROAS (Ads Daddy).** Price minus COGS = margin = break-even CAC; break-even ROAS = price / margin. $50 price, $10 COGS = $40 CAC, 1.25 ROAS.
- **1%/1% minimum test spend (Ads Daddy).** Assume 1% CTR, 1% CVR, need ~10,000 reach for a valid read. At $10 CPM = $100 minimum; $3 CPM = $30; $15 CPM = $150. Double CTR or CVR, halve the minimum. Hard kill at 5x sale price with zero conversions.
- **PPS, profit per sale (Rich From Anywhere).** Net profit / order count = the CAC ceiling. $500K / 1,000 orders = $500. Rule: CAC always below PPS.
- **Jobs-to-spend chain (Michael Tracey).** Jobs wanted / close rate = leads needed; leads x CPL = spend. 10 jobs / 10% close = 100 leads x £10 = £1,000. Improving close rate 10% to 20% halves the spend.
- **CAC range with a sweet spot (Brotherdale).** Model a bottom/sweet/high (e.g. $28/$40/$52). Below the sweet spot leaves "meat on the bone"; a 1.5x AOV move shifts it to $42/$61/$79, ~33% more allowable spend.
- **50 optimised actions per week (Nick Boddington).** Expected cost per result x 50 = weekly budget per ad set. £50 CPR = £2,500/week = ~£350/day per ad set. Divide any budget by number of ad sets.
- **Whole-account budget formula (Christian Jamal).** ads x ad sets = ad spend / half the sale price. Per-ad kill ceiling = 2.5x sale price (medium risk). Starter structure: 5 audiences x 3 ads at 2.5x price.
- **75/25 split (Joly Tematio).** With history: 75% budget to proven, 25% to testing. From cold: ~80/20 favouring testing.
- **Spend-to-capability ladder (Jason Hunt).** $5-10/day = buying data; ~100 Facebook actions needed to exit learning; $30/day = test audiences/creatives; $50/day = split tests + funnel + retargeting; separate $1-2/day evergreen video-views for a warm audience.
- **Pre-scale checklist (Nikhil).** 3+ winning creatives, 5-7 consecutive days of consistent orders, purchases under the CAC ceiling, don't cut after one bad day (wait 2-4), only scale if already spending full budget.

## Contrarian / disputed takes

- **CAC ceiling vs CAC range.** Nikhil and Joly Tematio defend a tight CAC ceiling to protect margin (Nikhil: purchases below Rs 450 for an 8-12% net margin). Brotherdale argues the opposite failure is real too: sitting below the sweet spot underspends and leaves customers on the table. Resolution: ceiling protects the front-end margin, the range widens once backend AOV/LTV justifies it.
- **Kill multiple: 2.5x vs 5x price.** Christian Jamal cuts at 2.5x sale price per ad with no sale; Ads Daddy waits to 5x. Lower multiple = faster, cheaper reads but more false negatives on slow-starting creative.
- **Patience window.** Christian Jamal and Nikhil review at 2-3 days; Michael Tracey says let a new account run weeks before judging. The gap is really about account maturity: newer accounts and pixels need longer. **Resolved 2026-07-28:** the verdict gate ($100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions) sets the floor for everyone, and Tracey's advice — wait longer on a young account — is the correct adjustment above it. Nobody gets to go faster than the gate.
- **Starting budget for beginners.** Rich From Anywhere caps brand-new advertisers at $10-20/day to gather signal cheaply; Joly Tematio and Jason Hunt put the real lead-gen floor at $30-50/day. Not a true conflict, they are pricing different goals (learn cheap vs generate leads).

## Execution playbook

IF asked "how much should I spend on this campaign" THEN never answer with a flat number: ask for the sales/seats target, the offer price, the expected close rate, and the expected cost per lead, then run the chain jobs / close = leads, leads x CPL = spend (Tracey, Boddington).

IF sizing the minimum test budget THEN use break-even CAC first (price minus COGS), then the 1%/1% rule off the CPM to get the floor spend, and set a hard kill at a multiple of the price (Ads Daddy).

IF the budget is small (under ~$100/day) THEN consolidate to one offer, one campaign, one ad set, run a leads or sales objective only, and forbid awareness/traffic warm-up spend (Ben Heath, Nikhil, Boddington).

IF a modest budget is underperforming THEN cut ad-set count before adding budget: "consolidating budget always improves performance" (Boddington).

IF a creative has zero conversions THEN kill at the price-multiple threshold (2.5x-5x price), never on a single bad day or on emotion (Christian Jamal, Ads Daddy, Nikhil) — and never before the verdict gate ($100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions) has been cleared. The sources' "2-4 days of data" is faster than the gate allows; the gate wins.

IF a winner is confirmed THEN scale it 10-20% at a time on a multi-day average, only if it is already spending its full budget, and keep 25% of budget on testing (Nikhil, Joly Tematio).

IF CAC is rising as you scale THEN treat it as expected and check AOV/LTV headroom rather than panic-cutting; raising AOV expands allowable spend non-linearly (Brotherdale).

IF clicks come but no sales THEN diagnose in order: offer/ICP first, then landing page/tech, then creative. Changing only the offer took a cost per lead from $97 to $26 (Ads Daddy).

IF setting a revenue goal THEN require the full funnel numbers (leads, warm rate, booking rate, show rate, quote rate, close rate); a ~30% close rate is healthy, materially higher means underpriced (DoneMaker).

**Default numbers experts use:**
- Spend floor: $25-50/day per platform for real lead gen; $5-10/day = data only.
- Minimum test read: reach ~10,000 people, or ~50-100 optimised actions per week.
- Learning: Facebook needs ~100 actions to know who to target.
- Kill threshold: 2.5x (aggressive) to 5x (patient) sale price, zero conversions.
- Review window: 2-4 days on an established account, weeks on a brand-new one — **context for whether a creative has had a fair look, not a verdict rule.** The **single verdict rule across this library: no kill or scale call below $100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions**, on rolling 7-day windows with 7-day-click attribution only. It beats every competing read-window figure quoted in the sibling brains (48-72 hours, 2-3 days per cell, 2,000-3,000 impressions, 1,000 visitors/week). At low volume, three conversions is a fair-look floor, not proof one option beat another — see the noise warning in `brain-meta-creative-strategist-manual` before you act on a small difference.
- Scale step: +10-20% per move on a 3-day average, never same-day.
- Split: 75/25 proven/testing with history, ~80/20 from cold.
- Adjustment cadence on a small budget: no more than once every 7-10 days.

**Pre-flight checklist (before sizing any spend):**
1. Sale price and margin known, so break-even CAC is set.
2. Conversion event correct and tracking verified (pixel + CAPI, sale event not a proxy).
3. Target: how many sales/seats, by when.
4. Expected close rate and cost per lead (conservative).
5. Number of ad sets planned (divide the budget by it).
6. Backend AOV/LTV known, so the CAC ceiling reflects the lifetime path.
7. A kill rule and a scale rule chosen and written down before launch.

**Top 5 failure modes and fixes:**
1. *Picking a spend number off a peer's screenshot.* Fix: derive it backward from the goal (Boddington, Joly Tematio).
2. *Fragmenting a small budget across many ad sets.* Fix: consolidate to one ad set; complexity you can't afford starves learning (Boddington, Ben Heath).
3. *Killing on emotion or day one.* Fix: kill on the price-multiple threshold, and only once the verdict gate is cleared ($100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions) — the sources' 2-4 days is faster than the gate permits (Nikhil, Christian Jamal).
4. *Budgeting on broken or proxy-event tracking.* Fix: verify signal and optimise on the sale event first; under-reporting inflates the learning cost (Nikhil).
5. *Chasing the cheapest CAC / highest ROAS ratio.* Fix: judge on profit volume and allowable spend from AOV/LTV, not the ratio (Ben Heath, Brotherdale, Tracey).

## Applied to your business

This is the one section where filling in your own numbers is not optional — every rule above is a formula with your figures as inputs.

**Write down first:**
- Ad account `<YOUR_AD_ACCOUNT_ID>` · pixel `<YOUR_PIXEL_ID>` · page `<YOUR_PAGE_ID>`
- Each offer and its price: `[offer]` `[$price]`, `[offer]` `[$price]`. Mark which ones actually take cold paid traffic
- **Cost of delivery per unit** `[$X]` → **break-even CAC** = price minus delivery cost = `[$Y]`
- **Close rate** from lead to sale: `[%]`. If you don't know it, that is the first thing to measure — every number below depends on it
- **Backend value**: what a customer is worth over the whole path, not just the first sale: `[$Z]`. This is what sets your *allowable* CAC, usually well above the front-end margin
- Current daily budget `[$X]/day`, current CPM `[$Y]`, current CPL `[$Z]`
- Confirm the pixel and CAPI are firing on the *sale* event, not a proxy. **Broken tracking distorts the CAC math before you can budget at all** — fix signal first, always

**Consensus translated into execution moves**
1. **Size every campaign or event backward from the goal.** Sales wanted ÷ close rate = leads needed; leads × CPL = spend. Never pick a spend number first. For a dated event, pace the back half harder — a meaningful share of registrations arrive in the final stretch. Judge the event on sales and on the path behind them, not on raw CPL. **Count conversions carefully:** in Meta reporting, use lead-type actions only (`action_type == 'lead'`); the aggregate `actions` field double-counts across related action types and will flatter your CPL.
2. **Run the break-even math per offer.** Price minus delivery cost = allowable CAC; then the 1%/1% rule off your real CPM sets the minimum test spend (assume 1% CTR and 1% CVR, need ~10,000 reach; at a `[$X]` CPM that is `[$Y]` minimum). Set a hard kill at a multiple of the sale price.
3. **Hold the floor and consolidate.** The real lead-gen floor is roughly $25-50/day. Whatever you have, keep it as one campaign per offer objective with as few ad sets as the test genuinely needs, and never split it thinner. No awareness or traffic warm-up spend at these budgets.
4. **Kill and scale by rules written down before launch.** A common lead-gen kill rule is ~3x target CPL with no conversion. **Watch the units** — that is a multiple of *target CPL*, not the 2.5x-5x-of-*sale-price* rule the ecommerce sources use. On a `[$12]` CPL target those are $36 and several thousand dollars respectively; confusing them is expensive in both directions. Everything is gated on the house floor ($100 spend + 7 days + 3 conversions) and benchmarked against your own account's rolling 4-week averages, not anyone else's. Scale +20% per 3 days on a multi-day average, once a winner is confirmed and already spending its full budget.
5. **Price the front end against lifetime value, not front-end margin.** If your entry offer produces the qualified audience for everything behind it, the allowable CAC is set by the whole path. That is what makes a CAC that looks unaffordable against seat margin genuinely affordable — and it is also the thing to re-check when the backend conversion rate moves.

**What may NOT apply to you**
- **Ecommerce AOV and upsell tactics** (in-cart upsells, "other customers liked", catalogue budget splits). If you sell services or education, your AOV lever is the ascension path, not cart mechanics.
- **Rupee, pound and dollar figures in the sources are illustrative.** Convert to your own currency. The structure transfers; the raw numbers do not.
- **"Run at a loss 6+ months before payback."** That comes from finance and insurance, where LTV is enormous and patient capital is normal. It does not fit a modest daily budget. Run each campaign to profit or breakeven on the first sale plus near-term ascension, unless you can genuinely fund the gap.
- **Awareness-objective scaling advice.** Out of scope on a small budget. What does apply: decide your funnel shape. Instant forms versus landing pages is decided in `brain-meta-instant-forms-vs-lp`; read it before choosing. Short version: for cheap, simple offers an instant form with a qualifying question can work; for high-ticket or considered offers, send traffic to a landing page.
- **"Fix tracking first" as a blocker.** The sources say it generally because most accounts are broken. If your CAPI and sale event are verified firing, treat signal quality as maintained state rather than a gate, and get on with the math. If they are not, it *is* the gate — nothing below it is trustworthy.
